# Public tower data for fitting floor wind under canopy

Research note, 2026-10-07. Question: which public datasets measure wind at two or more levels at one site, with one level at or below 3 m inside a forest canopy and one above the canopy, at 30 min or finer, over several years, in stands like ours (boreal black spruce, jack pine, aspen/birch, northern hardwood, mixedwood)? Purpose: fit floor-speed ÷ above-canopy-speed against speed, stability, hour, leaf state and stand type, with Sep–Nov well covered.

Short answer: the best material is **AmeriFlux BASE**, which (a) mirrors every NEON tower as a `US-x**` site with wind at all tower levels and (b) holds two Alaskan black spruce towers with multi-level in-canopy wind (US-Prr, US-Uaf). All of it is CC-BY-4.0, 30-min, multi-year, downloadable with a free account and no PI request. The Canadian BERMS/FLUXNET-Canada towers publish only one wind level in BASE. Jack pine is the gap: nothing multi-year was found with wind at ≤3 m under jack pine.

Heights below come from the NEON locations API (`/api/v0/locations/{id}`, zOffset of each "2D Wind L*n*" sensor) and from AmeriFlux's public `BASE_MeasurementHeight` CSV; the two agree to the centimetre. Canopy heights are NEON's `DistZaxsCnpy` site property (the field-site web pages quote slightly different, older numbers, e.g. HARV 26 m vs 23 m). "Not found" means I looked and did not find it.

## (b) Strong sites

**US-Prr (Poker Flat black spruce).** Ten MetOne cup/vane anemometers at 1.5, 1.9, 3, 4.5, 6, 7.5, 9, 11, 13, 16 m (`WS_1_10_1` … `WS_1_1_1`), plus a sonic for `USTAR`. BASE 2010–2022, 30-min; the 1.5 and 3 m records are 78–97 % complete most years, 1.9 m is patchier. Open, patchy black spruce with large canopy gaps (Ikawa et al. 2015 measured understory fluxes in a gap), so it is the "open spruce" end of our range; stand height not found in the public metadata. Cup anemometers stall below ~0.3–0.5 m/s: treat low-speed bins with care. PIs Iwahana, Kobayashi, Ikawa, Suzuki; DOI 10.17190/AMF/1246153.

**NEON BONA / US-xBN and DEJU / US-xDJ (interior Alaska black spruce).** Canopy 6.4 m and 6.1 m, towers 18 and 23 m, five levels. Gill WindObserver 2D sonics at 0.25/2.81/5.34 m (BONA) and 0.18/1.95/5.98 m (DEJU), CSAT3 at the top. BONA's level 2 (2.81 m) is complete 94–97 %; the 0.25 m level is 47–60 % (snow burial and ice). DEJU similar. Both are ≥ 70 % black spruce with aspen/birch and shrub patches, which is close to WMU 21B spruce and to the Yukon area. Data 2017 to present (NEON), 2017–2024 in BASE.

**US-Uaf (Fairbanks open black spruce).** Cups at 2, 4, 8 m, CSAT3 at 6 m, 2003–2024, 2 m level 81–87 % complete. Short, open stand on permafrost; canopy height not found. Longest black spruce record with a 2 m level, and it reaches Sep–Nov every year.

**NEON STEI / US-xST (aspen-maple regrowth, 9.5 m canopy).** 2D sonics at 0.29, 4.43, 7.79 m inside the canopy, 11.94/15.31/22.34 above; best aspen/birch leaf-on vs leaf-off case (the 4.43 m level is 96–99 % complete).

**NEON TREE / US-xTR (mixedwood, 24 m canopy).** 2D sonics at 0.25, 2.06, 9.62, 23.04 m; 26.39 and 35.95 above. Balsam fir, spruce, maple, birch, red/white pine, tamarack: nearest public analogue to Sault Ste Marie mixedwood. The 2.06 m level is 94–99 % complete.

**NEON BART / US-xBR (northern hardwood, 20 m canopy).** 2D sonics at 0.56, 2.48, 15.5, 19.2 m, above at 25.48 and 35.66; 2.48 m level 79–98 % complete. Beech/maple with hemlock and white pine.

**Pine analogues: NEON RMNP / US-xRM (11 m lodgepole/ponderosa) and YELL / US-xYE (16 m lodgepole).** Not jack pine, but similar crown form and closure; both have a ~4 m level and a 0.3 m level under the canopy.

**NEON caveats (apply to all US-x sites).** (1) 2D wind directions at terrestrial sites were offset by up to 90–270° from 2012–2017 sensor-orientation errors; corrected in reprocessing from RELEASE-2022 on, so use RELEASE-2024 or later (or BASE, which is built from released NEON data). (2) The distorted-flow quality flag (wind through the tower) had a calculation error before July 2021, fixed in RELEASE-2023; the 2022 refinement flags only the main structure ± 5°, so expect 10–55° fewer flagged directions than older releases. (3) Documented problems at lower levels: ice on sensor tines (winter 2017–18), branches bending mounting brackets, booms left retracted after maintenance; check `windSpeedFinalQF` (NEON) or the gaps in BASE. (4) Gill WindObserver resolution 0.01 m/s; direction is meaningless below ~0.3 m/s, which is common at the 0.2–0.3 m level. (5) The NEON locations API gives each tower a `DistZaxsGrndOfst` property of 0.2–2 m (e.g. BONA 1.52 m, HARV 0.77 m) whose meaning I did not confirm; if it is the tower base above local ground, the published level heights could be that much higher. Verify against the `sensor_positions` file in the first download.

## (c) Minimum set to pull first

Pull seven AmeriFlux BASE sites in one request: **US-Prr, US-xBN, US-xDJ, US-Uaf** (black spruce, open to moderately closed, 1.5–3 m levels), **US-xST** (aspen/birch, 9.5 m), **US-xTR** (mixedwood, 24 m), **US-xBR** (northern hardwood, 20 m), and add **US-xRM** as the only multi-year pine-with-2-m-level set, standing in for jack pine until something better appears. Every one is 30-min, CC-BY-4.0, spans at least 2017–2024 with full Sep–Nov coverage, and has above-canopy `WS_1_1_1` (CSAT3) plus `USTAR`, `TA` at each level, `SW_IN`, `PA` for stability. Fit the ratio as floor level ÷ top level per site, binned by top-level speed, hour, month (leaf state from month at the deciduous sites) and site closure/height. Then test the fitted curve on the two one-season Saskatchewan sets (BOREAS TF-02 aspen, TF-04 young jack pine) and on Harvard HF288 as out-of-sample checks. Dense 20 m spruce, our worst-case stand, is not represented anywhere in public data; the closest is TREE (24 m mixedwood with fir/spruce) and the 2 m level at US-MMS under a closed hardwood canopy.

## (d) How to download

**AmeriFlux BASE (sites 1–12, 16).** Free account at https://ameriflux-data.lbl.gov/Pages/RequestAccount.aspx; the portal (https://ameriflux.lbl.gov/data/download-data/) asks for an intended-use category and sentence; CC-BY-4.0 sites need no PI approval (Legacy-policy sites do: of our list only CA-SJ3). Scripted: R `amerifluxr::amf_download_base(user_id, user_email, site_id = c("US-Prr","US-xBN",…), data_product = "BASE-BADM", data_policy = "CCBY4.0", agree_policy = TRUE, intended_use = "model", intended_use_text = "…")`, which posts to `https://amfcdn.lbl.gov/api/v1/data_download`. Each site arrives as `AMF_<SITE>_BASE-BADM_<ver>.zip` containing `AMF_<SITE>_BASE_HH_<ver>.csv` (30-min rows, `TIMESTAMP_START/END` as YYYYMMDDHHMM, −9999 missing, tens of MB per site) and a BADM Excel file. Variables: `WS_1_n_1` (m/s, n = 1 top … bottom), `WD_1_n_1`, `USTAR`, `TA_1_n_1`, `RH`, `PA`, `SW_IN`, `NETRAD`, `H`, `LE`, `P`. Heights per variable: public CSV, no login, https://ftp.fluxdata.org/.ameriflux_downloads/measurement_height/BASE_MeasurementHeight_20260908.csv (columns Site_ID, Variable, Height, Instrument_Model, Start_Date). Years per variable: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chuhousen/amerifluxr/master/data-summary/AMF_AA-Flx_BASE_VARIABLE-AVAILABILITY_LATEST.csv (fraction of each year present).

**NEON directly (if you want 2-min data or months after 2024).** `https://data.neonscience.org/api/v0/data/DP1.00001.001/{SITE}/{YYYY-MM}?package=expanded` returned HTTP 403 from this machine for both WebFetch and curl, while `/products`, `/sites` and `/locations` worked; use `neonUtilities::loadByProduct(dpID = "DP1.00001.001", site = …, startdate, enddate, package = "expanded", token = <free NEON API token>)` or the portal download. Files per site-month-level: `NEON.D19.BONA.DP1.00001.001.000.0n0.030.2DWSD_30min.csv` (VER 010 = level 1, 050 = level 5; `windSpeedMean`, `windSpeedMinimum/Maximum`, `windDirMean`, `windSpeedFinalQF`, `windDirFinalQF`), with `…sensor_positions…csv` (zOffset) and a readme. Tower-top 3D wind is in DP4.00200.001 (bundled eddy-covariance, HDF5), which BASE already extracts as `WS_1_1_1`. Expanded package adds the per-test quality metrics needed for the pre-2021 distorted-flow caveat. A site-year of 30-min 2D wind is a few MB per level.

**Harvard Forest.** HF288-03: https://harvardforest1.fas.harvard.edu/exist/apps/datasets/showData.html?id=HF288 → `hf288-03-sonic-data.csv` (30-min; u*, wind speed, direction, sonic T at 2 m; 22,177 rows). HF004: `hf004-01` hourly met incl. `wind_sp`/`wind_dir` at 29 m, `hf004-02` 30-min fluxes incl. `ustar`, 1991–2023, CC0, also on EDI (doi:10.6073/pasta/56c6fe02a07e8a8aaff44a43a9d9a6a5).

**BOREAS (ORNL DAAC, free Earthdata login).** TF-02 SSA-OA 1994 tower flux: doi:10.3334/ORNLDAAC/… (dataset `tf02tflx`; guide `TF02_Flux.txt`): 30-min means; sonic levels 39.5/28.6/18.6/5.9/0.5 m (one TR-61B was rotated among 28.6, 5.9 and 0.5 m "during selected periods"), propeller wind above (39.4 m) and below the overstory. TF-04 SSA-YJP 1994 (`tf04flux`, R1): RM Young propellers at 1.1, 10.0, 11.0 m and sonics at 1.1 and 9.1 m in a 4.6 m jack pine stand, mid-May–mid-Sep 1994. TF-01 understory (`tf01uflx`): sonic at 4 m (1994) / 6 m (1993) under aspen, Oct–Nov 1993 and all 1994, 30-min. TF-10 NSA-YJP (`tf10flux`): wind profiles on a 12 m tower, 1994 and May–Nov 1996, heights in its Table 1 (not extracted). HYD-03 (`h3scmet`, doi:10.3334/ORNLDAAC/265): 2 m wind under OJP (3 days, 1994) and OBS/OA (4 days, 1996), 10-/1-min, many values below the 1 m/s stall — reference only. All ASCII text with .def files.
