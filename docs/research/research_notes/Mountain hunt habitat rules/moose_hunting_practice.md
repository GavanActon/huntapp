## 1. Where are moose in early, mid and late September and in October (elevation bands, subalpine basins, willow flats, river bars, burns)?

### Takeaway
Through September and well into October, mountain moose are still on their summer/rut ranges, and in the Cordillera those ranges are the subalpine shrub belt around and just below treeline (willow and dwarf-birch basins, timberline plateaus), plus willow flats, river bars and burns in the valleys. The move down to winter range does not start until about November and is set by snow depth, not by the calendar. After the rut (mid-October on), bulls rest and feed in secluded willow flats, burns and subalpine basins, often in small bachelor groups.

### Cited Findings
- [G] ADF&G: moose are "most abundant in recently burned areas that contain willow and birch shrubs, on timberline plateaus, and along the major rivers". In fall and winter they eat "large quantities of willow, birch, and aspen twigs". — [ADF&G, Moose Hunting in Alaska](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=moosehunting.main)
- [S] Nelchina Basin, Alaska (62° N; Van Ballenberghe, ADF&G, 28 radio-collared moose, >2,000 relocations, Dec 1974 – Feb 1977). The plateau sits at 500–600 m, and moose "seasonally use habitats" in the Alaska Range foothills "at elevations up to 1400 m". Black spruce dominates below 850 m. Trees thin out between 850 and 1,000 m, with "dense shrub understories of dwarf birch … and various willow species" in the subalpine. — [Van Ballenberghe, Migratory behavior of moose in southcentral Alaska](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/home/library/pdfs/wildlife/research_pdfs/migratory_behavior_moose_southcentral_alaska.pdf)
- [S] Same study, timing. Summer ranges were "normally occupied only during the period June through November". Autumn migration began "as early as November 1", with some moose not leaving until early February. Most "did not arrive [on winter range] until mid-December or later", and migration ran over "10 to 12 weeks beginning in early November". Moose collared in October 1975 "used shrub dominated plant communities extensively" and were "readily observable". 19 of 22 tagged moose came back to the same autumn area the next year (strong range fidelity). — [Van Ballenberghe](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/home/library/pdfs/wildlife/research_pdfs/migratory_behavior_moose_southcentral_alaska.pdf)
- [S] South-central Yukon (South Canol, GPS collars; Alces 2017). In winter, moose used smaller ranges at lower elevations, "presumably limited by snow depths". They "moved up in elevation throughout summer, reaching maximum elevations during rut and early winter". Conifer was the most-used cover class year-round, and shrub classes were used most in early and late winter. No significant differences between the sexes. — [Yukon Moose II, Alces (abstract via search; full text not opened)](https://alcesjournal.org/index.php/alces/article/view/174)
- [S] Northern Yukon (Old Crow) is a counter-example where the pattern runs the other way. 17 of 19 collared moose were migratory: they left the lowland flats between August and November and wintered in alpine terrain 59–144 km away. Above 600 m they selected "shrubby valley bottoms near lakes and rivers" within alpine-dominated home ranges. — [Seasonality of a migratory moose population in northern Yukon, Alces](https://alcesjournal.org/index.php/alces/article/view/247) (snippet; page not opened)
- [G] Yukon early-winter (November) moose surveys are flown in subalpine country. South Canol was surveyed 15–29 Nov 2013 and the Teslin Burn 19–28 Nov 2013. One Yukon survey report gives "most observations … between 3,000–4,000 feet elevation, around treeline" (≈915–1,220 m) and a subalpine shrub zone "from treeline to 1524 m (5000 ft)". — [Yukon survey report PAM 2013-0019](https://archives-ftp.gov.yk.ca/library/normal/PAM_2013_0019.pdf); the 3,000–4,000 ft quote is a snippet from the [Yukon EMR library result set](https://emrlibrary.gov.yk.ca/emrlibrary/environment/population-dynamics-and-early-winter-habitat-utilization-by-moose-in-the-southwest-yukon.pdf) (snippet; I could not confirm which report it came from)
- [H] Post-rut bulls look for "significant quantities of food in a secluded location where they can feed undisturbed for several weeks". Named places: willow flats, river valleys prone to flooding ("ideal conditions for willow growth"), old burns ("vast areas of single-species deciduous growth" with deadfall), cut blocks, and subalpine elevations where bachelor groups form (one group of eight bulls in a river valley NW of Fort St. John, BC). — [Ken Bailey, Outdoor Canada, 2006](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3682)
- [H] Northern BC / Yukon: "Moose love hinterland, be it a swamp-infested spruce forest, a willow-covered valley floor, a forested mountainside, a new growth old burn or simply a remote river valley". The rut is "during the latter part of September". — [Mark Hoffman, BC Outdoors, 2021](https://bcoutdoorsmagazine.com/?p=9377)
- [H] Rut calling works where cows feed (willow, alder, dogwood), and in transition zones such as old burns, cut blocks and shorelines. — [Jim McLennan, Outdoor Canada](https://outdoorcanada.ca/?p=4852)
- [H] Alaska high-country hunt. Camp sat on "a broad flat … covered in tundra with patches of gravel and a few small stands of pine and willow". The hunters glassed a ¾-mile opening and left "deep timbered draws" alone as unhuntable. — [Snow, Outdoor Life, 2019](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)
- [H] Northern BC outfitters: "Moose prefer meadow edges and dense willow thickets". Cassiar Mountains hunts run 1–30 Sept on horseback, with riverboat access in some areas. — [Huntin' Fool listing, NW BC](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/all-inclusive-northwest-bc-canadian-moose-hunt); [Huntin' Fool listing, Cassiar](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/horseback-river-boat-canada-moose-hunts) (snippets; pages JS-rendered, not opened)
- [H] Early September: bulls may sit in "the same small area all summer, once they shed velvet they move". Calling "isn't as effective early in September". — [Rokslide, "Early September Hunt"](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/early-september-hunt.448668/) (snippet; page not opened)
- [H] Rut timing (Alberta-based guide Kevin Wilson): bulls respond "eagerly from Sept. 27 to Oct. 4", and "calling activity abruptly subsides after about Oct. 11". — [Guidefitter, A Guide's Guide to Calling Moose](https://guidefitter.com/blog/a-guides-guide-to-calling-moose)
- [H] Yukon guided hunts run "in mid-September, when mature bulls are highly responsive to calling", with horseback trips "through alpine basins", Argo trips and jet-boat hunts. — [Huntin' Fool, Premier Yukon moose](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/premier-yukon-outfitted-trophy-moose-hunts) (snippet; page not opened)

### Inferences
- **Elevation band, 1 Sept – ~31 Oct:** moose are on the subalpine summer/rut range. At Blanchard (treeline 1,300–1,450 m) that means the willow/birch shrub belt from about 300 m below to about 150–200 m above treeline. At Highland Lake (treeline 1,350–1,430 m) the band is similar. Valley-bottom willow flats on the river are a second, separate hot band. Open alpine above the shrub limit and dense valley spruce are both low. (The Nelchina belt of 850–1,400 m sits about 400 m lower than these areas because its treeline is lower; scale by local treeline, not absolute metres.)
- **No downhill shift in the October model.** Snow-driven descent starts at the earliest in November (Nelchina) and needs real depth (see Q7). A mid-October snow should not shift the heat to the valleys.
- **Early Sept vs late Sept vs Oct.** Early Sept: bulls finishing velvet sit tight in small areas, so glassing basins and feeding edges works best. Mid/late Sept to about 11 Oct: the rut, when bulls travel to cows and calling works (weight cow-feeding willow and shorelines). After about 11 Oct: post-rut, with bulls in secluded willow flats, burns and subalpine basins, sometimes in bachelor groups, so glassing and still-hunting work best. For the friend at Blanchard on 12–18 Oct 2026, this is the post-rut transition.
- Old Crow shows that "high in winter" happens in some far-north populations. That is no reason to change the Sept–Oct band for either area, since both lie in typical montane country.

## 2. Glassing: how much of mountain moose hunting is spotting from high points, and what makes a good glassing spot? Should a heat map score vantage points separately?

### Takeaway
In the mountains, guides and DIY hunters agree that glassing from height is the core method. Hunters camp or sit on knobs and ridges that look "down and across" into willow basins and valley bottoms, glass all day, and often call from the glassing spot, pulling bulls in from up to about 2 miles. The glassing spot and the moose are usually different places, so a vantage score should be its own layer.

### Cited Findings
- [H] Alaska–Yukon guided hunts: "Glassing is done from a high point overlooking good habitat, including areas with plenty of willows, swamps, and cottonwood patches". Hunts are "spot and stalk with an emphasis on calling". Done well, "guides can effectively 'blind call' moose from two miles or more, oftentimes bringing them all the way to the glassing hill". — [Huntin' Fool, Alaska–Yukon moose hunt listing](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/alaska-yukon-moose-hunt-212) (snippet; page JS-rendered, not opened)
- [H] DIY Alaska consensus: "Get up high and spike camp on ridgelines, glassing down and across into moose country", and "Glass all day until your eyes bleed" as the key to success. High country "where you can spot for miles" is preferred. — [Rokslide, DIY Alaska moose hunting strategy](https://www.rokslide.com/forums/threads/diy-alaska-moose-hunting-strategy.383349) (snippet; page 403)
- [H] Post-rut spot-and-stalk is "universally preferred". "You must first find a suitable vantage point (preferably one that makes use of natural relief), then sit and watch". Light reflecting off antler paddles or the dark hide is visible "for miles". — [Ken Bailey, Outdoor Canada](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3682)
- [H] "Use height to your advantage when glassing valley floors or opposite mountain sides". Good optics matter most "in areas where regulations dictate antler type", which applies at Blanchard (see Q8). — [Hoffman, BC Outdoors](https://bcoutdoorsmagazine.com/?p=9377)
- [H] One Alaska team glassed from "an exposed knob that was flanked by several large willows", which gave cover. In fog and rain, visibility fell to "a couple hundred yards". — [Snow, Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)
- [H] Staying power: one Alaska hunter had "success calling from the same location for 2 days before moving", and "most newbies are not patient enough. They call, wait an hour or so and move on". — [HuntTalk, Moose hunting tips](https://www.hunttalk.com/threads/moose-hunting-tips.311976/)
- [H] An outfitter reports that clients on an average Yukon hunt see "around twenty moose". — [Huntin' Fool, Premier Yukon moose](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/premier-yukon-outfitted-trophy-moose-hunts) (snippet; page not opened)
- [S] In October, moose in shrub-dominated subalpine communities were "readily observable" from the air (Nelchina), so open shrub habitat is also the habitat where moose can be spotted. — [Van Ballenberghe](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/home/library/pdfs/wildlife/research_pdfs/migratory_behavior_moose_southcentral_alaska.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Score vantage points as their own layer.** For each candidate point (knobs, ridge noses, benches 50–300 m above a basin floor), score = the sum of moose-habitat score within its viewshed, weighted by distance. Full weight to about 1.5 km (judging antlers with a spotting scope), tapering to about 3 km (2 miles, where a moose can still be picked up and called). Add a bonus for nearby cover (willow or krummholz to sit in) and for being reachable from camp or transport.
- Show the vantage layer as "where to sit", distinct from the heat ("where moose are"). The game layer should not reward a high bare knob just because it sees a lot.
- Open subalpine shrub is both good moose habitat and easy to see into. Dense valley spruce is hard to glass, so a "glassable habitat" term (shrub/open fraction) should feed the viewshed sum.
- Fog and low cloud cut glassing range to about 200 m, so on foggy days the vantage layer should drop out, or switch to call sites near water (Q3).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of glassing distances for moose. The 1.5–3 km bands are my synthesis of "two miles" and "for miles" claims.
- No outfitter-published elevations for preferred glassing points in the Yukon or NW BC.

## 3. Calling in open country: does it work in the subalpine (sound carry, response distances), and how does the late-season (mid-October) approach differ?

### Takeaway
Calling works well in open subalpine and lake country during the rut (about the last week of September to early October). Bulls hear a call at 1.5 km or more, and guides claim blind-call responses from 2 miles on calm, cold mornings. Responding bulls circle downwind, so the set-up must deny them the downwind side (water or a cliff behind the caller). After about 11 October, bull response falls off sharply. The post-rut approach shifts to glassing, still-hunting and snow tracking, though some writers still call at low intensity.

### Cited Findings
- [H] "Bulls can hear you from distances of more than one-and-a-half kilometres". Call every 15–20 minutes for 30 seconds to a minute, and stay up to three hours before moving. Set up to force the bull to come in downwind; shorelines and water positions are preferred. — [McLennan, Outdoor Canada](https://outdoorcanada.ca/?p=4852)
- [H] "Calm conditions – particularly when it's cold – can carry your sounds great distances". "Heavy winds can make calling ineffective". The guide's calling was "foiled on many occasions by bulls working their way to a downwind position". Bulls often "hang up" 50–80 yards out. Call up to 30 minutes per spot (colleagues use up to 2 hours). Peak response 27 Sept – 4 Oct; "abruptly subsides after about Oct. 11". — [Kevin Wilson, Guidefitter](https://guidefitter.com/blog/a-guides-guide-to-calling-moose)
- [H] Guides "blind call" from 2+ miles, often bringing bulls to the glassing hill. — [Huntin' Fool listing](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/alaska-yukon-moose-hunt-212) (snippet; not opened)
- [H] In Alaska high country (fog and rain), a caller did hand-cupped cow calls about every 10 minutes and bulls responded from beyond about 500 yards. — [Snow, Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)
- [H] Calling is "by far the most successful" method in BC/Yukon. The author called in a bull on 4 Sept in the Yukon after hearing distant calls. — [Hoffman, BC Outdoors](https://bcoutdoorsmagazine.com/?p=9377)
- [H] Alaska moose seasons with the rut fall within a 21-day September window. Calling is most effective late in that window. — [Rokslide, Early September Hunt](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/early-september-hunt.448668/) (snippet; not opened)
- [H] One Alaska source: "Early morning, before the thermal winds kick up, is when 90% of calling is done". Electronic calls are prohibited for moose in Alaska. — search result set for Alaska moose tips (snippet; source page not identified, treat as unverified)
- [H] Post-rut, bulls "will no longer be interested in your cow calls". Use spot-and-stalk (preferred) and still-hunting instead. — [Bailey, Outdoor Canada](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3682). Contradicted in part by: "don't be shy about calling, even in the late season". — [ODU Magazine, late-season moose tactics](https://odumagazine.com/?p=39058) (snippet; not opened)

### Inferences
- **Call-site layer (rut window about 20 Sept – 11 Oct):** score a point by the moose-habitat score within about 1.5 km, more on calm, cold mornings. Add a strong bonus where the downwind side is open water or very steep or open ground, so the bull must show itself before it gets your scent. Lake and river shores at the foot of willow basins score highest.
- **Wind interaction:** calling range should shrink as wind rises. In Groundwind terms, scale the call radius down with head-height wind speed.
- **Mid-October (Blanchard, 12–18 Oct):** turn the calling weight down to about a third of its rut value. Raise the weights for glassing-vantage and secluded willow/burn feeding cover. Mention calling in the UI as a low-intensity option, not the main tactic.

### Gaps
- No acoustic study of moose-call carry in subalpine terrain. Distances are practitioner claims (500 yd, 1.5 km, 2 miles).
- No northern-Cordillera source on how the response window varies with latitude (Yukon versus northern BC).

## 4. Access in roadless country: float-plane lakes, river boats, ATV trails, packing distance. How far do hunters realistically go, and how does pack-out shape where to hunt?

### Takeaway
Northern moose hunting is organised around the transport point: the plane on a lake or strip, the river boat, or a horse or Argo string. The widely repeated rule is not to shoot a moose more than about a mile (1.6 km) from camp or transport, and never uphill of a long carry. A bull yields about 500 lb of boneless meat, and in several Alaska units the meat must stay on the bone. Even a 500-yard carry can take most of a day, and a one-mile uphill carry of two bulls took four days. In practice, the heat that matters is habitat within about 1–3 km of a landable lake, boatable river or trail, discounted for climbing back up with meat.

### Cited Findings
- [G] "A 1,600-lb moose will dress out at about 950 lbs, yielding approximately 500 lbs" of meat. Alaskans and nonresidents harvest 6,000–8,000 moose a year. — [ADF&G, Moose Hunting in Alaska](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=moosehunting.main)
- [G] In Alaska Units 13, 21, 23, 24 and 25, the meat of the front quarters, hindquarters and ribs must stay "naturally attached to the bone" until it is out of the field, which adds bone weight to every load. — [ADF&G meat salvage requirements rack card](https://adfg.alaska.gov/static/hunting/pdfs/meat_salvage_requirements_rack_card.pdf) (snippet of the card's content)
- [G] From 1 July 2025, all nonresident moose hunters in Alaska (guided or not) must complete the Nonresident Moose Hunter Orientation, which covers "meat care, hunt preparedness, shot placement, and the legal requirements". The quiz has 16 questions and 15 must be correct. — [ADF&G advisory, 9 July 2025](https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/Portals/5/pub/GUInonresMoose.pdf)
- [H] Alaska rule of thumb, quoted by a 20-year Alaska resident on a Yukon fly-in hunt: "Don't shoot a moose in the water, don't shoot one a mile from camp and never shoot two at the same time". Breaking it, the party took four days to pack two bulls (≈3,000 lb including antlers and bone) one mile uphill to the strip, in ten pack loads, through "spruce jungle, rockslides, and spongy moss". — [Outdoor Life, "Double Trouble", 2007](https://www.outdoorlife.com/articles/steve-sorensen/2007/09/double-trouble-0/)
- [H] Koyukuk River, Alaska (boat hunt 45 river miles from Huslia): it took "almost eight hours the next day to break him down and pack the meat and antlers the 500 or so yards to the boat". — [Huntin' Fool member story, "Don't Shoot"](https://www.huntinfool.com/articles/type/member-stories/dont-shoot)
- [H] Alaska high country: pack-out of "less than half a mile" uphill through bog "with more than 130 pounds of meat" per person, then out by bush plane. — [Snow, Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)
- [H] "The golden rule is to try to shoot a moose within a mile from transportation". Experienced, fit hunters may treat 2 miles as attainable. River-boat pack-outs run from "7 yards on the river bank to half a mile". — search snippets (Huntin' Fool / Outdoor Life result set; exact page not confirmed)
- [H] After a kill, "1 to 2 days are spent packing the meat, hide and skull out of the field", and in Alaska the meat must come out before the trophy. — search snippet from the result set that included [Guidefitter hunt 28181](https://cdnmain.guidefitter.com/hunt/28181-alaskan-yukon-moose-hunt---11-full-hunting-days---1x1) and [Willow Air FAQ](https://www.willowair.com/faq/) (exact page not confirmed; not opened)
- [H] "Packing a moose is to an elk as packing an elk is to a deer". Bring "3 or 4 buddies with sharp knives and strong backs plus a hand winch". — [HuntTalk](https://www.hunttalk.com/threads/moose-hunting-tips.311976/)
- [S] Interior Alaska rural hunters (GIS of harvest locations): about 53%, 21%, 5% and 21% of harvest locations were on navigable rivers 0–24, 25–52, 53–86 and >86 km from the hunter's community. Harvest is organised along the river network. — [Quantifying Rural Hunter Access in Alaska](https://catalog.epscor.alaska.edu/dataset/quantifying-rural-hunter-access-in-alaska)
- [H] Transport modes named by Yukon and northern BC outfitters: horseback through alpine basins, Argo, jet boat, riverboat, horseback with a wrangler "to assist with pack outs". — [Huntin' Fool, Yukon](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/premier-yukon-outfitted-trophy-moose-hunts); [Huntin' Fool, Cassiar BC](https://new.huntinfool.com/trip-details/horseback-river-boat-canada-moose-hunts) (snippets)
- [G] BC Skeena region closes some alpine areas to motor vehicles year-round: above 1,400 m on Tsatia Mtn, Klastline Plateau, Level Mtn and Gnat Pass, and MU 6-18 above 1,450 m. None of these closures lists MU 6-28 or 6-29. — [BC Hunting & Trapping Synopsis 2026–2028, Region 6](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/sports-recreation-arts-and-culture/outdoor-recreation/fishing-and-hunting/hunting/regulations/hunting-trapping-synopsis-region-6-skeena.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Replace the step access curve (1.0 / 0.8 / 0.5 by road distance) with a pack-out cost from the nearest transport point.** Transport points: road; a boat-landable shore on a lake or boatable river; a float-plane lake (any lake long enough to land, roughly 600 m or more of open water for a Super Cub or Beaver; a pilot's figure, not sourced here); a known strip. Cost ≈ horizontal km + (metres climbed on the way BACK to transport) / 100, i.e. each 100 m of vertical counts as about 1 km of flat (Naismith-style; my assumption). Suggested curve: 1.0 to 1.6 km-equivalent (the one-mile rule), falling to 0.6 at 3.2 km-equivalent (two miles, "fit and determined"), and 0.3 beyond 5 km-equivalent.
- **Direction matters.** Habitat above camp or the boat is cheaper to hunt than habitat below it, because the meat comes downhill. That is the reverse of the Bryner disaster ("a mile uphill"). Charge only the climb back to transport.
- **Highland Lake:** the 97% "0.5 long walk" result comes from measuring to roads that don't exist. Measure from the lake shore instead (float-plane or boat transport) and from any other landable lakes. Most of the 3 km core should then score by pack-out from the shore, with the subalpine basins and benches within about 1.5 km and 300 m of climb staying warm.
- **Blanchard:** add the highway as transport (it is), and the Blanchard River only if it is boatable (unknown). Thick buckbrush along the highway raises the effective cost of off-trail travel (see Q8), so a brush penalty on walking cost could use the existing willow/birch layer.

### Gaps
- No published average daily foot radius (km and vertical) for moose hunters in mountains. The 1.6 / 3.2 km figures are the practitioner rule, not measured.
- No figure for float-plane lake length in the sources fetched.
- Yukon meat-salvage and wanton-waste wording could not be retrieved (yukon.ca blocked).

## 5. Hunter pressure: do moose near the Haines Highway, airstrips and boat lakes respond to hunting (moving away, nocturnality), and by how much?

### Takeaway
Yes, but the scale is local. GPS work in Alaska shows that male moose change habitat selection during the hunting season in response to trail density. Moose in remote areas avoid trail-dense ground. Moose in heavily trailed areas tuck into cover close to trails rather than moving far. Avoidance bands run from about 100–250 m (roads, Québec) to about 1 km (vehicle-route intensity, Yakutat). Hunters in the Haines corridor say to get away from road activity by hiking into timber or boating down a roadless lake. I found no northern data on night-shifting.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Interior Alaska, 26 GPS-collared adult bulls before, during and after the hunt. "Moose that resided in areas with extensive trail access selected habitat closer to trails and vegetative cover". "Moose in more remote, less accessible regions avoided areas with high trail densities and selected habitat closer to quality forage during the hunting season". Step lengths rose with trail density and with distance from cover. — [Brown, Kielland, Brinkman, Gilbert & Euskirchen 2018, Ecosphere 9(9): e02405](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.2405)
- [S] Yakutat, Alaska: "Male moose were displaced by routes or areas where routes were in close proximity to primary forage", with "a combined pattern of route avoidance … beyond approximately 1 km of total vehicle travel/km²/day". — [Shanley 2008, UAF thesis](https://scholarworks.alaska.edu/uaf_grad_bio_sciences/440)
- [S] Québec, 47 GPS moose: moose avoided highways and forest roads, "although avoidance usually disappeared beyond 100–250 m". This is outside the Cordillera but the only distance figure found. — [Ecoscience 19(1), Interactions between a large herbivore and a road network](https://bioone.org/journals/ecoscience/volume-19/issue-1/19-1-3461/Interactions-between-a-Large-Herbivore-and-a-Road-Network/10.2980/19-1-3461.full) (snippet)
- [H] "Disturbance of any kind, even in the rut, will move game out of the area". The writer avoids open fires and picks larger lakes for camp. — [Hoffman, BC Outdoors](https://bcoutdoorsmagazine.com/?p=9377)
- [H] Haines Highway / Tatshenshini (MU 6-28/6-29): hunters say they need to "get away from road activity by hiking into timber or taking a boat down a lake where there is no road access". Moose are found along lake shores in spruce patches reachable by small boat. — [HuntingBC forum, Tatshenshini-Alsek thread](https://www.huntingbc.ca/forum/archive/index.php/t-85839.html) (snippet; page 403)
- [G] Mayo-area moose have been hunted under a threshold since the 2022/23 season (opens 1 Sept, threshold 11 moose, closes 31 Oct or when the threshold is "thought to be met"). This is an agency response to harvest pressure in the Silver Trail road and river-accessible area. — [Yukon Environment, 2022/23 Moose Threshold Hunts](https://open.yukon.ca/information/f025950d-4369-4f9b-bb53-5b513df237bc/resource/4cd295a0-3795-3f87-9fff-58d1335dc820/download/env.576.2022-23_3_0.pdf); [Yukon News](https://www.yukon-news.com/news/moose-permits-and-threshold-coming-to-sifton-miners-range-south-canol-and-mayo-areas/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Pressure penalty, Blanchard:** multiply habitat by about 0.6 within 250 m of the Haines Highway and its pullouts, ramping to 1.0 by about 1 km. Apply it from opening day (23 Aug) onward. By mid-October the road-edge moose have been hunted for seven weeks. Also give "cover adjacency" a slight boost near the road (Brown et al.: pressured moose stay close to cover).
- **Highland Lake:** pressure is low and limited to the lake. A small penalty (about 0.8) within about 300 m of the main camp shore is enough. Remote-region moose avoid trail-dense ground, so don't cut new trails into basins you plan to hunt.
- The current model scores access 1.0 near roads (good for effort) but has no pressure term. The two pull in opposite directions near roads and should be kept as separate factors.

### Gaps
- No data on nocturnality or shifted activity timing for hunted moose in the northern Cordillera.
- No harvest-location data for the Haines Highway corridor, and no data on airstrip-centred pressure.

## 6. Mountain wind and thermals as hunters use them

### Takeaway
Hunters plan on morning downslope drainage (air cool and heavy, sliding down until the sun warms the slope), daytime upslope flow, a mixed and swirly transition, and downslope again near dark. Morning approaches come from above or across a basin, and afternoon approaches from below. Moose respond to calls by circling to the downwind side, so wind direction decides set-up even more than in still-hunting. I found no moose-specific source on swirl in basins.

### Cited Findings
- [H] "Typically the thermals move downslope in the mornings until the air heats". "After the air heats up … the thermals will be upslope during the day". "Later in the day, as the air starts to cool, the thermals will mix again and then just before dark, they will be predominately downslope again". "Thermals are weather dependent". — [Rokslide, Understanding Mountain Thermals](https://rokslide.com/forums/threads/understanding-mountain-thermals.121825/) (snippet; not opened)
- [H] Mornings: "be uphill or crosswind of where you expect animals". Afternoons: "positioning yourself downhill of where you expect game movement puts you in the ideal wind direction". — [Divebomb Industries, How to Use Wind Thermals in Mountain Hunts](https://www.divebombindustries.com/blogs/news/how-to-use-wind-thermals-in-mountain-hunts) (snippet)
- [H] Bulls answering calls work "to a downwind position". Set up to force the bull to come downwind, using shorelines and water. — [Wilson, Guidefitter](https://guidefitter.com/blog/a-guides-guide-to-calling-moose); [McLennan, Outdoor Canada](https://outdoorcanada.ca/?p=4852)
- [H] For post-rut stalks, plan the approach on the prevailing wind. Wind and snow cover the noise of movement. — [Bailey, Outdoor Canada](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3682)
- [H] On one Alaska high-country hunt, a steady south wind held for 10 days and the blind was set up to suit it. — [Snow, Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)
- [H] Calling is concentrated in the early morning, "before the thermal winds kick up". — Alaska search snippet (source not identified; unverified)

### Inferences
- Groundwind's existing scent cone already models the physics. The hunter-facing rule to encode is: **morning (until about 2 h after sunrise on sunlit slopes) favours glassing/vantage points ABOVE the basin and call sites on the uphill or upwind side; afternoon favours approaches from BELOW.** The vantage score could be time-of-day dependent, with "above the moose" points scoring higher in the morning window.
- In mid-October the sun is low (on 15 Oct, declination ≈ −8.5°, so noon sun ≈ 21° at Blanchard, 60° N, and ≈ 18° at Highland Lake, 63.4° N). North-facing basins may not warm enough to turn upslope at all, so the morning downslope regime can last all day on shaded aspects. This is my inference from geometry, not from a hunting source.

### Gaps
- No moose-hunting source quantifies basin swirl, or how long the morning-to-afternoon transition takes at these latitudes in October.

## 7. Weather for mountain moose hunting: October snow (tracking, moving down), wind at elevation, fog

### Takeaway
Early snow helps hunters (tracking, visibility of dark moose on white ground, muffled steps) but does not by itself push moose down. In Nelchina the move down began in November at the earliest and tracked snow depth, with moose staying on summer range all winter when snow stayed under about 40 cm. Wind is the enemy of calling. Fog and rain cut glassing to a few hundred metres.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Autumn migration "seemed well correlated with snow depth". "Some moose spent most of one winter on their summer range when snow depths were generally less than 40 cm". In a heavy year, depths over 70 cm were common in the north of the study area, and moose left summer range by late November. — [Van Ballenberghe, ADF&G](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/home/library/pdfs/wildlife/research_pdfs/migratory_behavior_moose_southcentral_alaska.pdf)
- [S] Yukon winter: lower elevations and smaller ranges, "presumably limited by snow depths". Maximum elevations are reached "during rut and early winter". — [Yukon Moose II, Alces](https://alcesjournal.org/index.php/alces/article/view/174) (abstract via search)
- [H] In fresh snow, take "no more than three or four steps before stopping", and walk beside the track rather than on it. Windy or snowy conditions hide the noise of an approach. — [Bailey, Outdoor Canada](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/?p=3682)
- [H] "Tracking in fresh snow or still hunting can be a blast, but both require more stealth and patience". — [Hoffman, BC Outdoors](https://bcoutdoorsmagazine.com/?p=9377)
- [H] Calm, cold conditions carry calls far, and heavy wind makes calling ineffective. — [Wilson, Guidefitter](https://guidefitter.com/blog/a-guides-guide-to-calling-moose)
- [H] Fog and rain cut visibility to "a couple hundred yards", and poor conditions delayed bull activity. — [Snow, Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting-bull-moose-in-last-frontier.md)

### Inferences
- **Snow rule for the heat map:** keep the Sept–Oct elevation band until modelled or reported snow depth at the subalpine band passes about 40 cm. After that, shift weight progressively to valley willow flats and river bars over the following weeks. A mid-October dusting (5–20 cm) should raise the "tracking" tactic in the UI, not move the heat.
- **Daily tactic flag:** calm and cold means call and glass. Windy at elevation means glass from sheltered lee points and drop calling. Fog means stay near water and call sites, and hide the vantage layer.

### Gaps
- No climatology from the sources for October fog or low-cloud frequency, or ridge-top wind, at Haines Pass/Blanchard or Highland Lake.
- No study of moose daily movement versus wind speed in mountain terrain.

## 8. Specific information on the Haines Road / Blanchard River / Tatshenshini area (MU 6-28/6-29) and the Mayo / Stewart / McQuesten area (GMS 4-09)

### Takeaway
Blanchard is in BC's **Haines Highway Area Moose Restricted Area**. From 1 September to 31 October only spike-fork, tripalm or 10-point bulls are legal (any bull only 23–31 Aug). A friend hunting 12–18 Oct must judge antlers, which raises the value of glassing and spotting-scope vantage points. The country beside the highway is thick buckbrush. Hunters get away from the road on foot into timber or by boat on roadless lakes. For Mayo, Yukon runs a threshold hunt in the road- and river-accessible subzones around Mayo (4-04/4-05/4-06 and 2-5x/2-6x). GMS 4-09 is not labelled in that threshold map, and I found nothing specific to Highland Lake.

## 9. Turning it into heat-map terms (synthesis across the questions)

### Takeaway
Mountain moose hunting runs on four quantities the flat-boreal model lacks: elevation relative to treeline (season-dependent), view (vantage over glassable habitat), pack-out cost from transport (distance plus climb, direction-aware), and timing (rut versus post-rut, and snow depth). The existing browse, edge and shore terms still describe *where moose are*. These four add *where hunters should be* and *what is practical*.

### Cited Findings
- [G] Timberline plateaus, burns with willow and birch, and major rivers are the core habitats. — [ADF&G](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/index.cfm?adfg=moosehunting.main)
- [S] Moose stay on subalpine summer/rut range through about November, and the move down depends on snow depth (about 40 cm threshold). — [Van Ballenberghe](https://www.adfg.alaska.gov/static/home/library/pdfs/wildlife/research_pdfs/migratory_behavior_moose_southcentral_alaska.pdf); [Yukon Moose II](https://alcesjournal.org/index.php/alces/article/view/174)
- [H] Glass from height, call from the glassing hill (blind calls to about 2 miles), and don't shoot more than about a mile from transport. — see Q2 and Q4 sources above.
- [S] Hunting-season avoidance runs about 0.1–1 km from roads and trails, with remote moose avoiding trail-dense ground. — [Brown et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.2405); [Shanley 2008](https://scholarworks.alaska.edu/uaf_grad_bio_sciences/440)

### Inferences
Proposed rules (my synthesis; numbers are starting points to field-check):
1. **Elevation band (Sept – late Oct, until about 40 cm of snow):** multiplier 1.0 from treeline −300 m to treeline +150 m. Valley willow flats and river bars keep their own (shore/browse) score. Open alpine above +250 m drops to 0.3. Dense valley conifer away from water drops to 0.6. After about 40 cm of snow at the band, slide the peak downslope over about 2–4 weeks.
2. **Season phases:** 1–15 Sept (velvet off, bulls in small home areas): glassing basins weighted 1.0, calling 0.5. About 16 Sept – 11 Oct (rut): calling 1.0, shoreline call sites with water downwind boosted. After 12 Oct (post-rut): calling 0.3, secluded willow flats, burns and subalpine basins boosted, vantage weighted 1.0.
3. **Vantage layer (separate):** viewshed-weighted sum of habitat × glassability (open/shrub fraction), full to 1.5 km, tapering to 0, about 3 km out. Bonus for adjacent cover, and in the morning for being above the target basin.
4. **Call-site layer (rut only):** habitat within about 1.5 km (radius shrinks with wind), bonus for water or steep open ground on the downwind side.
5. **Pack-out access (replaces road-distance steps):** from the nearest transport (road, boat-landable shore, float-plane lake, strip), cost = km + (climb back to transport)/100 m. Score 1.0 to 1.6, 0.6 at 3.2, 0.3 beyond 5 km-equivalent.
6. **Pressure:** ×0.6 within 250 m of a highway or pullout, ramping to 1.0 at about 1 km (Blanchard). Mild penalty near busy camp shores (Highland). Keep it separate from access.
7. **Regulation overlay (Blanchard, 1 Sept – 31 Oct):** antler-restricted bulls only, so add weight to the vantage layer and a UI note to judge antlers before shooting.

### Gaps
- The weightings above are not validated against harvest locations. No harvest-location dataset for MU 6-28/6-29 or GMS 4-09 was found.
- A walking-cost model for brush (buckbrush, krummholz) has no sourced numbers. It would need field or GPS-track calibration.
