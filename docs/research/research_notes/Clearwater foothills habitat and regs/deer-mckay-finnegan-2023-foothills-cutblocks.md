# Deer in west-central Alberta harvest blocks, camera study (2023)
- **Source:** McKay, T. & Finnegan, L. (2023). Ungulate occurrence in forest harvest blocks is influenced by forage availability, surrounding habitat and silviculture practices. *Ecological Solutions and Evidence* 4: e12226. https://doi.org/10.1002/2688-8319.12226; read at https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2024-02/Ecol%20Sol%20and%20Evidence%20-%202023%20-%20McKay%20-%20Ungulate%20occurrence%20in%20forest%20harvest%20blocks%20is%20influenced%20by%20forage%20availability%20.pdf (abstract page: https://friresearch.ca/publications/ungulate-occurrence-in-forest-harvest-blocks-is-influenced-by-forage-availability-surrounding-habitat-and-silviculture-practices)
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text; Tables 3–5 read from the PDF text)
- **Evidence:** [S] peer-reviewed (fRI Research Caribou Program, Hinton)
- **Where and when:** West-central Alberta: the A La Peche, Little Smoky, Narraway and Redrock Prairie Creek caribou ranges plus a 25 km buffer. Natural subregions: Subalpine, **Upper Foothills, Lower Foothills** and Central Mixedwood. Camera sites at 766–1,590 m. Cameras ran 5 June 2018 – 29 Aug 2020:
  - summer: 117 harvest blocks, 211 site-years, 22,963 camera-days;
  - winter: 98 blocks, 190 site-years, 12,408 camera-days.
  - Block ages were 3–48 yr, stratified into 0–10, 11–20, 21–30 and 31–50 yr.
  - One camera per block, near the centroid, with scent lure (O'Gorman's Long Distance Call).
  - Seasons: summer 8 Apr – 7 Nov; winter 8 Nov – 7 Apr.

## Extracted facts
- **Occurrence (detections per 100 camera-days, Table 3):**

  | Species | Summer | Winter |
  |---|---|---|
  | White-tailed deer | 1.764 (107 sites) | 0.185 (22 sites) |
  | Mule deer | 0.492 (57 sites) | 0.016 (2 sites) |
  | Moose | 1.407 | 0.613 |
  | Elk | 0.340 | 0.073 |

  Summer exceeded winter for all four species (Wilcoxon p < 0.001).
- **White-tailed deer, summer habitat model (standardized β, 90% CI; Table 4):**
  - % cover *Calamagrostis canadensis* +0.906 (0.729–1.084)
  - % cover *Cornus canadensis* −0.175 (−0.321 to −0.028)
  - proportion of harvest blocks within 1 km −0.156 (−0.309 to −0.004)
  - distance to seismic line +0.718 (0.080–1.357)
- **White-tailed deer, winter:** sapling density +0.766 (0.635–0.897).
- **Mule deer, summer habitat model:**
  - tree density −0.114 (−0.195 to −0.032)
  - lateral cover at 0–1 m −0.092
  - elevation +0.154
  - slope +0.132
  - **distance to road +0.653 (0.152–1.155)**
- **Silviculture models (Table 5), summer:**
  - White-tailed deer: "harvest block ≤10 years" +0.379 (0.003–0.755); lodgepole pine planting density −0.274 (−0.465 to −0.083).
  - Mule deer: block ≤10 yr +0.394 (0.209–0.579); stand tending −0.289; pine planting density −0.114; total trees planted −0.147.
  - Elk: ≤10 yr +0.264.
  - Moose (winter): block ≥25 yr +0.649. The ≤ and ≥ signs were lost in the PDF text; the paper's text says "harvested within the previous 10 years".
- "white-tailed deer winter occurrence in harvest blocks was not predicted by silviculture covariates."
- "Deer, elk and moose occurrence was higher in younger harvest blocks."
- **Covariate ranges in the data:**
  - distance to road 30–1,284 m; road density (1 km) 0–2.98 km/km²;
  - distance to seismic line 0–2,102 m; seismic density (1 km) 0–6.27 km/km².
- **Discussion:**
  - Fewer white-tailed deer where more of the surroundings was cut. This contrasts with northern Alberta (Dawe 2011; Darlington et al. 2022) but matches west-central Alberta work: "Nielsen et al. (2017) reported that white-tailed deer abundance decreased with increasing harvest block density".
  - Lower winter use is attributed partly to deeper snow in blocks than in intact forest. Low winter use had been seen before in the same region (Stelfox et al. 1976).
- **Limitations stated by the authors:**
  - All cameras were inside harvest blocks, so they "could not determine whether ungulate species selected or avoided harvest blocks".
  - Many winter camera failures in extreme cold.
  - Lure was likely less effective in snow.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] This is the closest study to Clearwater: same Lower and Upper Foothills subregions, the same pine and spruce cut-block country, similar elevations, and roughly 200–400 km to the north-west (my estimate from the named caribou ranges).
- [I] The deer cut-age curve should peak in blocks ≤10 yr old, not at 10–20 yr as the moose curve does. Proposed curve: 0–2 yr 0.6, 3–10 yr 1.0, 11–20 yr 0.8, 21–30 yr 0.5, 31–60 yr 0.35.
- [I] "Summer" here runs to 7 Nov, so the archery seasons and the first week of the 324 rifle season fall in the high-use period. From about 8 Nov, use of open blocks drops (raw rate 1.764 → 0.185, about 10-fold, partly an artefact of camera failure and lure). In November, weight thick sapling regeneration and block edges over open block interiors.
- [I] Dense planted lodgepole pine lowers deer use. Scale pine-leading regeneration ×0.7 for deer.
- [I] More cut area within 1 km slightly *reduced* white-tailed deer use. Do not let cut density add up without limit; cap the cut bonus per 1 km window.
- [I] Seismic lines: white-tailed deer were found farther from them. Give them no deer bonus.
- [I] Mule deer in cut-block country are on steeper, higher ground, in open blocks, away from roads. In Clearwater that points to the western Upper Foothills and the valley breaks.

## Not confirmed
- Absolute selection (blocks vs. uncut forest) cannot be inferred from this design.
- Effects in Sept–Nov alone were not separated from the 8 Apr – 7 Nov "summer".
- Units: the coefficients are standardized, so they cannot be turned into distances (for example, "X m from a road") without the raw scales, which I did not have. The Dryad data (https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.vdncjsz03) were not opened.
- Nielsen et al. 2017 (Oikos) was not read; cited here only as McKay's citation.
