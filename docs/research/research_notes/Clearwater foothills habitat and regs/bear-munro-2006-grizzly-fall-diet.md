# Grizzly diet and activity in the west-central Alberta foothills (2006)
- **Source:** Munro, R.H.M., S.E. Nielsen, M.H. Price, G.B. Stenhouse & M.S. Boyce. 2006. Seasonal and diel patterns of grizzly bear diet and activity in west-central Alberta. Journal of Mammalogy 87(6):1112–1121. doi:10.1644/05-MAMM-A-410R3.1; author PDF https://sites.ualberta.ca/~scottn/documents/MunroEtAl2006.pdf (abstract page: https://friresearch.ca/publications/seasonal-and-diel-patterns-grizzly-bear-diet-and-activity-west-central-alberta)
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text). Also read in full: Coogan et al. 2014, PLoS ONE 9:e97968, same population, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4026535/
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:** 10,000 km² of west-central Alberta, about 75% foothills and 25% mountains, centred on Robb (53° N, 117° W). Diet: 665 feces from 18 GPS-collared bears, April–October 2001–2003. Activity: 1,032 field-visited GPS locations from 9 females. "Mountain bears" have ≥80% of their home range above 1,700 m; all others are "foothills bears".

  Mountain bears were later on fruit: 62% in late Aug, 80% in early Sep, 78% in late Sep (small n).
- **Fruit species:** "As fruit ripened in early August (late summer), diet increasingly consisted of soopolallie (Shepherdia canadensis) and mountain huckleberry (Vaccinium membranaceum)." Minor fruits were lingonberry, bearberry, raspberry and wild sarsaparilla. "Berries were consumed until mid-September, at which time sweet vetch roots once again increased in dominance … Bears used this root until den entry."
- **Discussion:** "During late summer and early fall, soopolallie dominated diets of grizzly bears with secondary use of Vaccinium fruit."
- **Table 3, foothills seasons (≥10% dry matter):** frugivory late July to early October; sweetvetch digging late April to early June and again early August to early October; ungulate kill late April to late July; insects late June to late August. In the mountains, frugivory ran early August to late September.
- **Ungulates:** at the early-June peak, ungulates were 49% of foothills diets vs 20% in the mountains. Kill sites were 83% moose (54% neonates), 16% deer and 1% elk. Foothills diets held "nearly 2.5 times the amount of animal matter" of mountain diets.
- **Ungulate feeding sites:** kills were found at 54 of 620 potential kill locations. Sites were "more likely to be located in closed forests, open forests, wet forests, and nonvegetated areas", and "female grizzly bears selected wet forest sites most for ungulate feeding". There was "No temporal trend in time of day … for ungulate carcass use".
- **Habitat by activity:** "Forested areas were selected for bedding areas, whereas digging, insect-foraging, and frugivory activities were associated with herbaceous, recently disturbed forest and open-canopy forests." Frugivory was most prevalent in mixed and open forests. Foraging was mostly diurnal and crepuscular, and bedding mostly at night.
- **Coogan et al. 2014** (secondary, same population) restates the seasons: fruit roughly early August to mid-September, then late fall roughly mid-September to mid-October with sweetvetch roots and fewer ungulates. In fall, "ungulate consumption continues for bears in the foothills, but is lower among bears in the mountains". Also: "Bears in the foothills have a much more carnivorous diet than bears in the mountains."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Clearwater (1,014–1,372 m) is all "foothills bear" country. The fall diet runs: buffaloberry-led fruit from late July or early August to mid-September, then a sharp switch to sweetvetch roots (46% in late September, 79% in early October), with ungulates at 8–12% the whole time.
- [I] For grizzlies the "berries done" date is about 15 September, not 15 October. The post-berry grizzly habitat is open or disturbed ground with sweetvetch (see the Nielsen note: cutblocks near 25% canopy, not scarified) plus carcasses in wet and closed forest. It is not "thick conifer".
- [I] Carcass use had no time-of-day pattern. A bear can be on a kill or gut pile at any hour.

## Not confirmed
- Fall samples are small (late Sep n = 9, early Oct n = 10). There are no feces after early October. Activity data are from females only. Ungulate species in fall were not split. Sweetvetch species (Hedysarum alpinum or H. sulphurescens) were not split. The study area is about 150 km north-west of Clearwater, mainly Upper Foothills; Clearwater is 60% Lower Foothills.
