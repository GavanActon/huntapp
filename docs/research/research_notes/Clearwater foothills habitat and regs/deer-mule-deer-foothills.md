## Extracted facts
- **D'Eon & Serrouya 2005:**
  - Aspect: most locations were on south (41% in both seasons) and west (34% summer, 28% winter) aspects; north 16% / 25%; east 10% / 6%.
  - Best winter-range model: "elevation and solar duration".
  - Median date of leaving summer ranges was 30 Oct (range 6 Oct – 21 Dec). Median arrival on winter range was 1 Feb.
  - In winter, deer used fir–pine forest "almost exclusively", in older stands with higher crown closure.
  - Distance to edge: 263 m in winter vs. 127 m in summer. Distance to logging road: 386 m in winter vs. 852 m in summer.
  - Within home ranges, 6 of 12 deer used locations farther from roads than random.
  - "midwinter snow depths >40 cm are common even at the lowest elevations" make migration obligatory there.
- **Telfer 1978 (abstract):**
  - Elk and deer winter use was "not strongly related to browse availability". It appeared tied to traditional wintering areas and human disturbance.
  - Recommendation: maintain "traditional winter range, especially steep south-facing slopes of major drainages and bottoms of large valleys" as essential deer and elk winter habitat in the foothills.
- **WMU 326 survey (Jan 2020):**
  - The unit is 1,046 km², Lower Foothills. "The dominant land-cover is dense conifer forest". "Cut-blocks comprise about 35% of the unit."
  - Over two days, 603.9 km of transects with a 400 m strip were flown. "11 deer (4 mule, 5 white-tailed and 2 unknown species)" were seen, "too low to provide a reliable estimate".
  - Moose: 0.14/km² (141 animals; 417 in 2010). 16 feral horses. "Some steep slopes bare of snow."
- **WMU 318/324 survey (Jan 2018):** WMU 324 is 1,026 km², Lower Foothills. Deer sightings "were too low to provide a reliable estimate". Moose in 324: 0.15/km².
- **Cross-references to other notes:**
  - McKay & Finnegan 2023: mule deer in foothills harvest blocks used higher elevations and steeper slopes, with lower tree density and lateral cover. They were found farther from roads (β +0.653).
  - Merrill et al. 2013 (parkland): "mule deer used more grassland and rugged terrain".
  - FMF HSI: 100 m road, trail, seismic and pipeline zone for mule deer.
  - Alberta CWD: mule deer are the main CWD species.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Where mule deer will be in Clearwater: steep, broken, south-to-west facing ground with open or young cover beside conifer. That means the breaks of the larger creek and river valleys, and the higher Upper Foothills in the west third (Rocky Mountain Forest Reserve). Not the flat Lower Foothills conifer. I did not check which named valleys (Prairie Creek, Clearwater River) fall inside the box.
- [I] Why few: most of the box is rolling, dense lodgepole and spruce, with little escape terrain. Roads are dense (~1.0 km/km²), and mule deer avoid roads more than white-tailed deer. Regulations also matter: in 324 antlered mule deer are by draw only, and there is no antlerless mule deer season in 324 or 326. This fits the 2025 harvest of 4 (324) and 1 (326) and the winter sightings of 4 mule deer in 326.
- [I] Proposed mule deer block, separate from white-tailed deer:
  - Base: the white-tailed deer base.
  - Slopes 10–35° facing 135–270° (S to W): +0.25.
  - Rugged ground (local slope relief, ring slope ≥12°): +0.1.
  - Dense conifer on flats (slope <5°): ×0.5.
  - In-season daylight within 300 m of a drivable road or trail: ×0.6 (within 100 m: ×0.4).
  - Fields and grass openings on or beside slopes: score like white-tailed deer fields.
  - Rut peak about 20 Nov – 10 Dec (see the rut note).
- [I] White-tailed deer aspect: Telfer's south-slope point is about winter range. In the snow-free fall, a +0.3 south-slope bonus on every ≥3° slope overstates aspect for white-tailed deer. Use +0.1 only from November or with snow on the ground, and keep the larger aspect weight for mule deer.

## Not confirmed
- No mule deer collar study from the Alberta foothills or east slopes was found. D'Eon & Serrouya is a deep-snow BC mountain population.
- Telfer (1978): full text not read. No numbers on snow or use were extracted.
- Mule deer density in WMUs 324 and 326 is unknown; no fall or ground survey was found.
- The 10–35° slope band and the multipliers are inference, not measured values.
