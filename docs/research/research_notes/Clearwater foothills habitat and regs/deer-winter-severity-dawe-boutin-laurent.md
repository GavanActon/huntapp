## Extracted facts
- **Winter severity index (Dawe & Boutin, after DelGiudice et al. 2002):** it "sums the number of days between November and April" with a minimum temperature below −17.7 °C and/or snow depth above 38 cm. A snow-water-equivalent version (WSIswe) was used.
- **Dawe & Boutin, results:**
  - Climate change accounted for more than 88% (by area) of the increases in predicted presence across decades. It drove 96% of the cells that increased from 1970 to 1980, 89% for 1980–1990 and 90% for 1990–2000.
  - The predicted range moved about 50–250 km north since 1970 in the east of the study area. A further 80,900 km² is predicted by the 2050s.
  - Land-use footprint was the cumulative share of agriculture, cut blocks and well pads within a 500 m buffer.
- **Dawe et al. 2014 (abstract):**
  - "climate, as measured by an index of winter severity, was the most important individual factor".
  - "Human land use (as measured by total land-use footprint) acted to substantially increase white-tailed deer presence ... only in areas with more severe winter conditions".
  - As cited by Dawe & Boutin, a "7.27-fold increase in the odds of white-tailed deer presence" per standard-deviation decrease in WSIswe.
- **Laurent et al. 2021 (abstract):**
  - "Sites with shallower snow had higher deer presence regardless of latitude."
  - Habitat alteration raised deer presence only in the north. Winter density was best explained by snow alone; spring density by habitat alteration and the previous winter's snow.
- **Other snow thresholds met in this research:**
  - Fisher et al. 2020: days with snow >30 cm (see the oil-sands note).
  - Hinterland Who's Who (CWS): "When snow is deeper than 40 cm" deer have trouble moving and follow broken trails (https://www.hww.ca/wildlife/mammals/white-tailed-deer/).
  - D'Eon & Serrouya 2005: mule deer winter range in the Selkirks, where snow is often >40 cm even at low elevation.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Snow and cold limit white-tailed deer at the boreal edge in winter, not in the fall season. Clearwater (52.3° N) is inside the established range. Sept–Nov rules need no snow-limit term, only a late-November shift to cover once snow builds.
- [I] Proposed snow shift: snow depth ≥30 cm, or the WSI cold condition (Tmin < −17.7 °C) on several days. Then move deer weight toward conifer cover within 140 m of food and toward south-to-west slopes, and scale open cut and field interiors ×0.6. This is unlikely before December, but possible in a late-November cold snap.
- [I] "Footprint matters most where winters are harsh". In Clearwater's milder setting, the deer map should come from forage–cover edges, fields and cuts, not from footprint density as such.
