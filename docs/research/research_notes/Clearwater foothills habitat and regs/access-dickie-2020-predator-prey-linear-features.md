# Predators use linear features as corridors, prey treat them as risk (2020)
- **Source:** Dickie, M., McNay, S.R., Sutherland, G.D., Cody, M. & Avgar, T. "Corridors or risk? Movement along, and use of, linear features varies predictably among large mammal predator and prey species." *Journal of Animal Ecology* 89(2): 623–634 (online Dec 2019), doi:10.1111/1365-2656.13130.
  - Read at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7028095/
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text through the PMC page, via a reading tool; the last ~5% of the page was not read.
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - A 7,826 km² study area straddling the Alberta–Saskatchewan boundary (boreal).
  - GPS collars deployed December 2012–May 2015.
  - 32 wolves, 34 black bears, 18 female moose and 4 female caribou. There were too few caribou for population-level estimates.

## Extracted facts
- **Feature classes:**
  - CLI: conventional seismic, low-grade roads and ice roads.
  - LIS: low-impact seismic.
  - PT: pipelines and transmission lines.
  - TRAN: railways and high-grade roads. This class was dropped because it fell on under 1% of random points.
  - Polygonal disturbances (well pads, clearings, cutblocks) were analysed separately.
- **"On a feature"** meant both ends of a step inside a buffer: transmission 37 m, high-grade road/rail 30 m, pipeline 20 m, low-grade or ice road 12 m, conventional seismic 10 m, low-impact seismic 7 m, riparian 100 m.
- **Abstract:** "all species moved faster while on LFs". Wolves and bears were attracted to linear features. Prey avoided them.
- **Moose (Section 3.1):**
  - At 20 m, moose were 1.17× more likely to move away from linear features than toward them.
  - None of the 18 moose selected to be closer to linear features.
  - Moose selected riparian areas and were indifferent to polygons.
  - Section 4.1: "Moose avoided anthropogenic LFs and moved faster while on them, supporting that moose perceive these features as risk."
  - The measured moose speed gain was small: only on CLI, about +1.5 m per 15 min, from 3 individuals.
- **Wolves:**
  - 1.20× more likely to move toward linear features at 20 m, and 1.26× toward polygons.
  - Displacement on CLI and PT was about +120 m per 15 min more than off them.
- **Black bears:** 1.20× more likely to move toward linear features at 20 m. Displacement about +52–55 m per 15 min on CLI and PT.
- **Avoidance distance:** the paper shows moose avoidance extending into the surrounding landscape (Fig. 3) but gives no single cutoff distance.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **A moose standing on a cutline is moving, not settling.** Moose sit off lines, and "on the line" is where they move faster, so it is a crossing place.
  - In the scorer, treat cells within ~20–50 m of a cutline as slightly worse for bedding or feeding moose (e.g. ×0.9).
  - Do not treat them as better, even though they are easy to see along.
- [I] **Wolves and black bears selected lines.** If the app ever shows predator-risk context, cutline density is the input.
- [I] **Low-grade roads were lumped with conventional seismic here (CLI).** Prey responses therefore do not separate a gravel spur from a seismic line. Traffic, not geometry, separates them; see Ciuti 2012.

## Not confirmed
- A moose avoidance distance in metres. None is given.
- Transfer of the result to the foothills. This was boreal lowland.
- Elk and deer. They were not studied.
