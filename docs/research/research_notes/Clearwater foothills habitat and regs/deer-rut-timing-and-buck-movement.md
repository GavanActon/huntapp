## Extracted facts
- **Hunsaker 2025:**
  - Movement-based peak rut started 23–27 Oct and ended 9–15 Nov, across analyses. Peak-rut length was 20–21 days from movement and 16 days from conceptions. Movement changepoints "aligned with back dated parturition data."
  - Mean hourly movement peaked in the week of 5–11 Nov. The WI DNR summary adds that movement rates "more than doubled from Oct. 15 to the absolute peak, approximately Nov. 1-7".
  - Two-year-olds moved most, "more than 10% higher" (DNR) than other ages, and had the largest peak-rut ranges.
  - "Movement rates showed no significant relationship with the hunting seasons or the timing of the opening weekend." Weather and temperature had no effect either.
  - "3+ males were 'more active during the night, especially during the peak rut'". Yearlings were more active at dawn.
  - Example buck 5700 (DNR): range about 292 acres before the peak vs. 822 acres at the peak at age 2.
- **Ransom 1966 (Manitoba):** white-tailed deer "breed from late October to December". Regional differences "could not be explained by latitudinal climatic factors."
- **ND Game and Fish (biologist Bill Jensen):**
  - "the peak of the rut for whitetails is generally between Nov. 15-20". This is back-dated from a peak fawning date of about 6 June.
  - "Seventy percent of fawns are born within plus or minus two weeks, and over 90% are born within four weeks of that date."
  - Mule deer breed later and taper into late December.
  - "during the rut, bucks are moving around constantly, no matter the time of day".
- **Hinterland Who's Who:**
  - The rut peaks in "the last two weeks in November in Canada". Most breeding falls "during the last three weeks of November".
  - Most fawns are born in the last week of May or first week of June.
  - Bucks "travel almost incessantly, searching out the does."
- **MDWFP:** "Most deer behavior studies have shown that hunter-observable rutting activity peaks about two weeks prior to the mean breeding date."
- **Alberta calendar, November:** "By mid-month, most white-tailed deer are in the 'rut', resulting in more movement during dawn and dusk – vehicle collisions peak at this time."
- **Spokesman-Review 2010 [H]:**
  - Unnamed "Researchers have pegged the statistical peak of the rut as Nov. 21 in northwestern Montana and Nov. 23 in northeastern Washington".
  - Retired MT FWP biologist G. Dusek: "Things come to a head about the 17th of November."
- **FMF HSI model (Banfield 1974):** white-tailed deer breeding mid-Oct to late Dec; mule deer late Oct to early Dec.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Sources from about 46–52° N (North Dakota, NW Montana, NE Washington, Manitoba, Alberta) agree on peak breeding between about 15 and 25 Nov. The Alberta fetal data (North Border Deer Study, median about 22 Nov, 80% by 30 Nov) are the most local. Wisconsin (about 43° N) runs about 2–3 weeks earlier, as expected further south.
- [I] Buck movement. On MDWFP's rule, observable activity peaks about 10 Nov in Alberta (mean breeding about 24 Nov minus two weeks). Hunsaker found movement high throughout the breeding window. So daytime buck movement in Clearwater should be high from about 5 Nov to the end of November. Proposed white-tailed deer rut factor f, with mult = 0.6 + 0.4·f as for moose:

  | Dates | f | Phase |
  |---|---|---|
  | ≤20 Oct | 0.2 | pre-rut |
  | 21–31 Oct | 0.4 | scrapes and rubs |
  | 1–7 Nov | 0.7 | seeking |
  | 8–28 Nov | 1.0 | chasing and breeding |
  | 29 Nov – 7 Dec | 0.7 | late |
  | 8–20 Dec | 0.4 | second rut |
  | after 20 Dec | 0.2 | |

- [I] Hour weight for deer when f ≥ 0.7: midday 0.5 instead of 0.3. Bucks move "no matter the time of day", and movement rose across all 24 h at the peak. Add a note that older bucks shift toward night at the peak.
- [I] Season fit. The WMU 324 rifle season (1–30 Nov) covers the whole rut. WMU 326 rifle (17 Sept – 30 Nov) runs from pre-rut to the end of the rut. Archery (324 to 31 Oct; 326 to 16 Sept) is pre-rut: fields, young cuts and last light matter more than rut behaviour.
- [I] Mule deer: shift the curve about 10–14 days later, peak about 20 Nov – 10 Dec. Most mule deer breeding then falls at or after the end of the November seasons.

## Not confirmed
- No GPS study of buck movement in Alberta or the Canadian prairie was found. The Wisconsin data are about 9° further south.
- The Montana and Washington peak dates (Spokesman-Review) were not traced to a primary study.
- The full text of Ransom (1966) was not read (cdnsciencepub returned 403), so its mean dates are unknown.
- The web search budget ran out before a search for Saskatchewan or Montana fetal studies could be completed.
