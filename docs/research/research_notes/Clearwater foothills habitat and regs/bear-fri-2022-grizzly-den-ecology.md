# Grizzly denning ecology, west-central Alberta (2022)
- **Source:** Graham, K. & G. Stenhouse. 2022. Grizzly Bear Denning Ecology in West-Central Alberta. fRI Research Grizzly Bear Program, final report to West Fraser, Hinton Woodlands, 10 March 2022; https://friresearch.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/GBP_2022_03_DenningWCAlberta_Final.pdf
- **Related source:** Pigeon, K.E., G. Stenhouse & S.D. Côté. 2016. Drivers of hibernation: linking food and weather to denning behaviour of grizzly bears. Behavioral Ecology and Sociobiology, doi:10.1007/s00265-016-2180-5; https://friresearch.ca/publications/drivers-hibernation-linking-food-and-weather-denning-behaviour-grizzly-bears
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (report: full text; Pigeon 2016: abstract only)
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:** BMA 2 (Grande Cache) and BMA 3 (Yellowhead). Den dates 1999–2018 on 139 bears; den habitat from 79 dens, 2000–2011 (Pigeon et al. 2014); den-site plots at 42 dens, 2001–2012 (Pigeon et al. 2016a).

## Extracted facts
- **Den entry:** mean 12 Nov for all classes. Earliest 20 Oct (a pregnant female), latest 19 Dec. By class:
  - Pregnant females: mean 3 Nov.
  - Lone females and females with cubs: means 9–14 Nov.
  - Adult males and subadults: means 14–18 Nov.
- **Den exit:** mean 12 Apr (5 Mar to 12 May). Time in den: mean 150 days (98–198; n = 136).
- **Arrival at the den area (within 500 m), Table 1:** overall mean 5 Nov (14 Oct to 11 Dec; n = 172). Pregnant females arrived 30 Oct on average (17 Oct to 20 Nov); adult males 8 Nov (14 Oct to 6 Dec). Bears spent a mean of 7 days near the den before entry (up to 31); 10% entered the day they arrived.
- **Table 4, mean entry by BMA:** BMA 2: 14 Nov (n = 65). BMA 3: 11 Nov (n = 114). BMA 6 Castle: 17 Nov (n = 9).
- **What sets entry:** "Berry availability had an influence with good berry crop years associated with later den entry dates." Pigeon 2016 abstract (11 yr; 15 males, 58 females): sex and reproductive status explained 22% of variation in den entry. "Weather did not influence the timing of den entry." Autumn berry availability explained 39%, and more berries meant later entry.
- **Den sites:**
  - Landscape scale (5–10 km): slopes in dry, open conifer and habitats with sweetvetch.
  - Within stands: low road density and dense conifer, not within 150 m of Vaccinium or buffaloberry habitat.
  - Distances: at least 300 m from well sites and 150 m from roads. No dens in 0–5 yr harvest blocks.
  - Terrain: slope mean 34° (12–50°); 795–2,284 m elevation, mean 1,705 m.
  - Den trees: subalpine fir 42%, spruce 38%, lodgepole pine 12%, aspen 8%.
- **Roads:** the relative probability of den selection fell 30% as road density rose from 0 to 0.6 km/km², by nearly 70% at 1.2 km/km², and to nearly zero at 2.0 km/km². Pigeon recommended keeping den-habitat road density at or below 0.6 km/km², with temporary closures from early October to mid-May.
- **Safety:** ground surveys of den habitat "should occur before 14 October in west-central Alberta". Keep disturbance at least 1 km from a known den.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Active season.** Grizzlies in BMA 4 are probably active through the whole black bear fall season (1 Sep to 30 Nov) and the October–November ungulate seasons; half of dens are entered after about 12 Nov. A kill-site card should run through November.
- [I] **Mountain rule.** The mountain-area grizzly rule, which dens bears from about 1 Oct, is roughly 6 weeks early for these foothills.
- [I] **Den habitat in the box.** Grizzly den habitat (slopes over 12° in dry conifer near sweetvetch, more than 150 m from roads) is likely scarce at 1,014–1,372 m. A grizzly pre-den weight is low priority here.
- [I] **Berry years.** A big buffaloberry year should push grizzly activity even later into November.

## Not confirmed
- No BMA 4 den data were found. The study bears den mostly higher and further north (mean den elevation 1,705 m).
- The full text of Pigeon 2016 was not read, so which berry was measured is unknown.
- There are no black bear data in this report.
