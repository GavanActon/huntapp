# Alberta black bear management plan (2016)
- **Source:** Alberta Environment and Parks. 2016. Management Plan for Black Bears in Alberta (January 2016; covers data to fall 2012). ISBN 978-1-4601-1929-7. Landing page: https://open.alberta.ca/publications/9781460119297. PDF read: https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/3274cf4f-3cd5-430a-9a6b-20813737642f/resource/79507b50-af50-4b1a-bfcb-00ff614f4fc2/download/2016-managementplan-blackbear-jan2016.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text of sections 2.1–2.2 and 3.3. Also read, abstracts only: Raine & Kansas 1990 and Pelchat & Ruff 1986 (see the end of Extracted facts).
- **Evidence:** [G], an agency plan that includes a literature review of [S] studies
- **Where and when:** Province-wide. The review draws on Alberta studies at Cold Lake, Fort McMurray, Swan Hills, Sheep River (Kananaskis) and Banff.

## Extracted facts
- **Den entry:** "In Alberta, den entry dates of black bears have been reported to occur between late September to early November (Fuller and Keith 1980, Tietje and Ruff 1980)". Fall food supply is "one of the most important factors". Bears den earlier in poor-food years. "In East Central Alberta, annual variation in den entry was attributed to the abundance of blueberries that persisted in the fall (Tietje and Ruff 1980)."
- **Emergence and order:** bears come out "during the first two weeks of April". Pregnant females and females with cubs enter first and males last.
- **Den sites:** in the boreal forest, black bears mostly dig ground dens, usually in mature conifer or mixedwood stands. Black bears "will readily den within 1-2 km of human activity".
- **Fall foods at Cold Lake (boreal mixedwood):** mid-July to August, mainly wild sarsaparilla and blueberries. In fall: "Common bearberry …, beaked hazelnut …, rose …, currants … and vetchling".
- **Fall foods at Sheep River (Kananaskis foothills; Holcroft & Herrero 1991):** late summer, "wild red raspberries …, buffaloberries and ants". Fall: "bearberries, bog cranberries (Vaccinium vitis-idaea), wasps, mammals, and forbs".
- **Habitat:**
  - Boreal black bears select well-drained upland mixedwoods and avoid bogs and fens.
  - They avoid very recent (<5 yr) regeneration and "will select older regenerating forests, generally between 5 and 24 years old". The studies cited for this are from Quebec, Montana, New Mexico and Washington.
  - They also select low-traffic roads, pipelines, seismic lines and well sites.
  - Habitat next to crops "can enhance suitability for black bears, especially during the late summer and fall".
- **Ungulates:** black bears are effective predators of moose, elk and deer newborns in their first 4–6 weeks of life. Otherwise they mostly eat carrion.
- **Density (Table 2.1):** Boreal Foothills 10 bears/100 km²; Boreal Mixedwood 15; Montane and Subalpine 10. The provincial total is still the 1993 estimate of 40,000.
- **Hunting:**
  - The fall season start was standardized to 1 September in most WMUs in 2011.
  - Baiting has been legal since 1987. It is "prohibited in WMUs or portions of WMUs with resident populations of grizzly bears".
  - In 2010, baiting was prohibited in portions of WMUs 320, 324 and 526.
  - The plan keeps baiting only "in those WMUs within the 300 and 500 series where reproducing female grizzly bears are normally absent".
- **Who hunts in fall:** resident hunters are "more likely to pursue black bears incidentally while hunting other species". Of bears killed by residents in 2009–2012, 66.5% were male.
- **Related, Raine & Kansas 1990** (Banff; Ursus 8:297–304; abstract only, https://www.bearbiology.org/download/black-bear-seasonal-food-habits-and-distribution-by-elevation-in-banff-national-park-alberta/):
  - Buffaloberry season runs mid-July to the end of August; buffaloberry made up 91.4% of feeding.
  - After buffaloberry (end of August to den entry): crowberry 85.1%, bearberry 11.1%, juniper 0.7%.
  - Mean elevation was 1,500–1,543 m, rising to 1,694 m after buffaloberry. Bears that stayed low in fall (1,463 m) ate bearberry.
- **Related, Pelchat & Ruff 1986** (Cold Lake; 47 collared bears, 1975–76; abstract only, https://www.bearbiology.org/download/habitat-and-spatial-relationships-of-black-bears-in-boreal-mixedwood-forest-of-alberta/): aspen stands were the most valuable food habitat and muskeg the least. Ranges were larger in the poor-food year (1976).

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Fall diet.** Black bears here probably feed on buffaloberry and raspberry through August. Fall is then lean: lingering fruit (bearberry on dry pine, lingonberry under conifer, rose hips in aspen and riparian), wasps and carrion. The "berries done" condition is largely met by mid-September, so 15 October is late.
- [I] **Denning.** With entry from late September to early November, pregnant females first, a large share of black bears is probably denned by about 15–20 October. Suggested multipliers on black bear presence: ×0.7 from 1 Oct, ×0.4 from 20 Oct, ×0.2 from 5 Nov. A dense upland conifer or mixedwood den weight (×1.6) is consistent with boreal den sites.
- [I] **Cut age.** The 5–24 yr selection matches the app's 3–20 yr window if the window starts at 5 yr.
- [I] **Aspen and mixedwood** should carry more fall weight for black bears (about 0.6) than the Ontario 0.5–0.55. Bogs and fens should carry less (treed wetland 0.25, open wetland 0.2).
- [I] **Farm edge.** Forest within about 1 km of cereal fields (the White Area in the east) is plausible August–October feeding habitat. There are no Alberta distance figures.
- [I] **Bait.** No baiting is allowed in WMU 326 or in the grizzly-occupied parts of WMU 324, so most black bear hunting in the box is spot-and-stalk or incidental.

## Not confirmed
- Tietje & Ruff 1980 and Fuller & Keith 1980 could not be read (JSTOR and BHL blocked). No foothills-specific black bear den dates were found.
- The 5–24 yr cut window comes from studies outside Alberta.
- Holcroft & Herrero 1991 was seen only through the plan's summary.
- No Alberta study measured black bear use of oat or canola fields.
