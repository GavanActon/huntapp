# White-tailed deer at farm-field edges by hour, northern Idaho (2020)
- **Source:** Groth, K. B. (2020). *Summer habitat selection of female white-tailed deer: can we reduce agricultural field use in Northern Idaho?* M.S. thesis, University of Idaho. Record: https://www.lib.uidaho.edu/digital/etd/items/groth_idaho_0089n_12011.html; PDF: https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/etd/pdf/Groth_idaho_0089N_12011.pdf
- **Context source:** Gaynor, K. M., Hojnowski, C. E., Carter, N. H. & Brashares, J. S. (2018). The influence of human disturbance on wildlife nocturnality. *Science* 360(6394): 1232–1235. Abstract via Europe PMC (DOI 10.1126/science.aar7121).
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (Groth: full text from the PDF, methods and results chapters; Gaynor: abstract only)
- **Evidence:** [S] graduate thesis, committee-reviewed but not peer-reviewed; [S] Gaynor, peer-reviewed meta-analysis
- **Where and when:**
  - Clearwater region of northern Idaho: farm fields (hay, pulse, winter wheat) mixed with forest, shrubland and roads.
  - 49 adult female white-tailed deer, GPS fixes every 15 min from March to November, 2017–2018.
  - Summer range analysed 15 May – 6 Oct.
  - Two sub-populations: private land, and an Idaho Fish and Game "Access Yes" parcel open to public hunting.

## Extracted facts
- **Diel periods used:**
  - morning/evening: 2–3 h after sunrise and 2–3 h before sunset;
  - midday: 7–9 h after sunrise;
  - crepuscular: ±0.5 h of sunrise and of sunset;
  - **peak (highest field use): 0.5 h before sunset to 1.5 h after sunset**;
  - night: 4.5–6.5 h after sunset.
- Distance to field edge was significant in every period, and deer "were more likely to be found on the field edge than at any other distance."
- Compared with 2.7 km from a field edge, deer were "~2.7 times more likely" to be at a field edge in morning/evening, midday and crepuscular hours, and "~6.4 and ~6.8 times more likely ... during peak and night hours".
- **Peak hours:** deer "strongly selected for gentle slopes, to be closer to field edges (particularly that of pulse fields), but strongly avoided forested areas". They selected roads, hay and winter wheat. Deer were "~3 times less likely to be 0.9 km away than inside the field" (pulse fields).
- **Midday:** deer stayed near field edges, pulse fields, shrubland and forest, and avoided grassland and hay.
- **Hunting context:**
  - Private-land deer: 62% resident, 35% migratory.
  - Access Yes deer: 38% resident, 65% migratory.
  - Access Yes migrants left about 1 Oct vs. about 1 Dec for private-land migrants. The author links this to the general rifle season opening on 10 Oct, but as a possibility, not a tested cause.
- **Gaynor et al. 2018 (abstract):** across 76 studies of 62 species, "Animals increased their nocturnality by an average factor of 1.36 in response to human disturbance."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Fields in Clearwater's White Area (east edge; hay, pasture, cropland about 3% of the box) should score well for deer at the edge and in the evening, not zero. Proposed field rules:
  - field cell value by distance to woody cover: ≤50 m 0.8, 50–140 m 0.6, 140–220 m 0.3, >220 m 0.1;
  - woody cell within 100 m of a field: +0.2 ("bedding beside a field").
- [I] Hour shift for field cells: ×1.3 in the last-light window (2 h before sunset to legal end), ×0.6 at midday. Most field use falls from 30 min before sunset into darkness, so the shootable part is the last half-hour. Field-edge stands are evening stands.
- [I] Under hunting pressure, deer on publicly hunted ground may leave or go nocturnal earlier. With 73% of the box within 500 m of a road, an in-season daylight pressure penalty near roads is consistent with this (see the Stelfox note).
- [I] Mapping caveat: NRCan "grassland" inside Clearwater's Green Area is mostly 5–14-yr cutblocks. Apply the field rules only where a cell has no CanLaD cut age; otherwise use the deer cut curve, so the two don't stack.

## Not confirmed
- This is summer and early fall only (to 6 Oct), females only, and northern Idaho crops (pulses, winter wheat) rather than Alberta hay, barley and canola.
- No Alberta or Saskatchewan GPS study of fall field use by hour was found. Alberta parkland locations in the North Border Deer Study show field-edge use but not by hour.
- Alberta legal shooting hours were not re-checked here.
