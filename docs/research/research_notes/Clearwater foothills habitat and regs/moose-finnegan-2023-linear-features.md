# Moose and seismic lines, pipelines and roads; wolves on lines (2023)
- **Source:** Finnegan, L., Hebblewhite, M., Pigeon, K.E. 2023. Whose line is it anyway? Moose (*Alces alces*) response to linear features. *Ecosphere* 14:e4636. https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.4636. Read at https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2023-08/Ecosphere%20-%202023%20-%20Finnegan.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text for Finnegan et al. 2023; abstracts or PMC full text for the supporting studies below.
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - West-central Alberta foothills: Little Smoky, A La Peche, Narraway and Redrock–Prairie Creek ranges (subalpine, Upper and Lower Foothills).
  - GPS collars with 4-h fixes on 7 adult moose (3 female, 4 male), 2008–2010, 20,911 locations. Only moose with more than half their locations in the foothills were kept.
  - Seasons: winter 17 Oct–15 May; summer 16 May–16 Oct.

## Extracted facts
- **Share of moose locations within 50 m of each feature:**

  | Season | Pipelines | Roads | Seismic lines |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Summer | 0.7% | 3% | 13% |
  | Winter | 0.8% | 5% | 17% |

- **Base habitat:** "moose generally selected wet areas, wet shrub habitat, and forest with lower percent conifer."
- **Seismic lines, landscape scale.**
  - Moose stayed closer to seismic lines where line density within 1 km was lower. The effect was stronger in winter and driven by males.
  - Winter coefficients: distance to seismic β −0.134 (95% CI −0.219, −0.049); interaction with seismic density +0.204 (0.049, 0.359).
  - In winter, moose were also closer to seismic lines where harvest blocks and fires were denser (interaction −0.138, CI −0.253, −0.024).
- **Seismic lines, fine scale.** In winter, moose selected areas closer to seismic lines "irrespective of the vegetation height on the seismic line or the percent conifer forest surrounding the seismic line". In summer they were indifferent.
- **Pipelines.** Moose moved closer only where other linear features (winter) or harvest and fire (summer) were sparse. At the fine scale they were indifferent.
- **Roads.**
  - Moose were indifferent to roads at the population level; "some individuals selecting areas closer to roads, and some selecting areas farther from roads".
  - Road types ranged from two-lane gravel to winter roads, and the road models rested on small samples.
- **Why seismic lines draw moose (authors' view).** Seismic lines may combine forage (willow, alder), travel routes, snow interception and nearby security cover. Pipelines are kept in grasses and clover, which moose prefer less.
- **Supporting:** Finnegan, Pigeon & MacNearney 2019, *Forest Ecol. Manage.* 446:175–192 (abstract via Semantic Scholar; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2019.05.026).
  - 291 seismic lines in west-central and north-western Alberta, 2014–15.
  - "wet seismic lines and seismic lines adjacent to open forest stands were more likely to have more early seral stage vegetation that is attractive wildlife forage".
  - Vegetation on the lines grew 1–2 m between 2007 and 2015.
- **Supporting:** Dickie et al. 2017, *J. Appl. Ecol.* 54:253–263 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.12732). In NE Alberta and NW Saskatchewan, 22 wolves in six packs, on 5-min GPS fixes, selected most linear-feature classes and "travelled two to three times faster on linear features compared to the natural forest".
- **Supporting:** Dickie et al. 2020, *J. Anim. Ecol.* 89:623–634 (PMC full text; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7028095/).
  - 55–56° N, 109–111° W; 18 female moose on 15-min fixes in the calving period (May–June), 2012–2015.
  - Moose avoided anthropogenic linear features: at 20 m they were 1.17 times more likely to move away than toward, and none of the 18 selected to be closer.
  - Moose selected riparian features: at 20 m they were 1.24 times more likely to move toward them.
  - Moose moved faster on linear features.
- **Supporting:** Latham et al. 2011, *Ecol. Appl.* 21:2854–2865 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1890/11-0666.1). In NE Alberta, wolves "showed strong selection for steps closer to conventional seismic lines during the snow-free season". Rivers and streams shaped wolf movement year-round.
- **Supporting:** Neilson & Boutin 2017, *Ecosphere* 8:e01913 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1002/ecs2.1913).
  - Athabasca oil sands: "Moose were killed closer to mines and rivers and at lower densities of linear features than expected by random."
  - Relative to moose density, "kills only increased with decreasing distance to mines and rivers."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Add seismic lines to the moose layer.** The app's Alberta Access layer has roads only, yet seismic lines are the linear feature moose use most here (13–17% of locations within 50 m).
  - Source: ABMI human-footprint seismic lines.
  - Effect: +0.05 browse within 50 m of a line, from 17 Oct.
  - Condition: only where seismic density within 1 km is below the area median.
  - Pipelines get nothing.
- [I] **Keep any road penalty modest.** Alberta GPS data (n = 7) found no consistent road avoidance. The camera study (McKay & Finnegan 2023) and BC and Québec work still show some avoidance; see the roads note.
- [I] **No predator term.** Wolves use linear features, but in Alberta, moose kills were not concentrated where linear density is high.
- [I] **Add streams to the water bonus.** Moose selected riparian features (Dickie 2020) and wet shrub (this study). The app rewards lakes and wetlands but not streams.
  - Effect: +0.15 within 100 m of a mapped stream (Prairie, North Prairie, Lick creeks).
  - Condition: the cell is shrub, broadleaf, mixed or wetland.

## Not confirmed
- Seven moose, one study area, 2008–2010.
- September to mid-October falls inside the study's "summer", so there is no separate fall result.
- Dickie 2020 is calving-season data only.
- I found no study of how moose respond to well sites in Alberta.
