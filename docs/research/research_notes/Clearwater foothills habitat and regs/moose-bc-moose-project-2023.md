# BC Provincial Moose Research Project: cutblocks, roads and hunting risk (2023)
- **Source:** Anderson, M., Procter, C., Scheideman, M., Hodder, D., Schindler, H., Thacker, C., Bohm, H. 2023. *Factors Affecting Moose Population Declines in British Columbia: Summary and Recommendations, 2012-2022.* BC Ministry of Forests, 20 Sept 2023. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/wildlife-wildlife-habitat/moose/ten_year_provincial_moose_project_report_2023.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text: summary, landscape-change and hunting sections, recommendations. Abstracts for Mumma et al. 2021 and Francis et al. 2021.
- **Evidence:** [G] agency synthesis of [S] studies.
- **Where and when:**
  - Five study areas on the Interior Plateau of central BC: Bonaparte, Big Creek, Entiako, Prince George South (PGS) and John Prince Research Forest. All are pine-beetle salvage-logging landscapes.
  - At least 30 GPS-collared cows per area per year, 2012–2022. 181 cow deaths were investigated in 2013–2022.

## Extracted facts
- **Cut age.** Mumma et al. 2021, *Forest Ecol. Manage.* 481:118763 (abstract via Semantic Scholar; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foreco.2020.118763), studying PGS and Bonaparte:
  - "across seasons, moose tended to avoid new cutblocks (1–8 years), select for regenerating cutblocks (9–24 years), and avoid older cutblocks (25–40 years)".
  - Within cutblocks, moose used more the locations with intermediate regrowth (normalized burn ratio) and with spruce rather than Douglas-fir leading.
- **Cut age and leading species** (report).
  - New cutblocks (1–8 yr) were used less than available "except in early winter".
  - Against Douglas-fir cutblocks, moose used "pine cutblocks more in early winter, and cutblocks dominated by broadleaf species more in early and late winter".
  - "In drier locations, pine cutblocks were avoided."
  - "Older cuts (25-40 yrs) are generally avoided."
- **Deciduous stands and browse.**
  - "Moose selected deciduous stands in all seasons", and those stands gave "less exposure to wolf predation".
  - "Herbicide application should not be used."
  - Important browse: "saskatoon, red osier dogwood, willow species, subalpine fir, trembling aspen, beaked hazelnut, paper birch, and cottonwood/poplar."
- **Home range and cover.**
  - Home ranges were 20–70 km², shrinking to 10–20 km² in late winter. Moose chose home ranges with 50–60% mature forest.
  - "Dash distances for moose are generally considered 200-400 m". The report advises designing blocks to minimize ground more than 400 m from a forest edge.
  - Thermal cover means stands >60 yr with 40–65% canopy closure; security cover means vegetation >5 m. Adjacent blocks should be cut only once the existing ones reach 5 m.
- **Hunting risk.**
  - "Cow moose were more likely to be harvested by hunters if using high road density areas on a given day or a high proportion of new cutblocks over the previous week (Mumma and Gillingham 2019)."
  - Hunting caused 11% of 181 cow deaths and did not drive the decline. Bull ratios stayed above 30:100.
- **Fall is the risk season.**
  - Report: "risk (defined as distance to linear features) best predicted moose selection during calving and fall."
  - Francis et al. 2021, *J. Wildl. Manage.* 85:156–168 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1002/jwmg.21963): 83 adult females on the Bonaparte Plateau, 2012–2016. Forage models beat risk models in every season "except for the calving and fall seasons where the top risk model (distance to road) better predicted moose space use."
- **Wolves.**
  - Wolf predation was the main cause of death for collared cows and 8-month calves.
  - Cows using low-road-density areas over the previous year were more likely to die from wolf predation.
  - Wolves selected new cutblocks, and wolf kills were associated with new and regenerating cutblocks. "Areas of higher road density were not associated with higher risk of moose being killed by wolves."
- **Starvation.** Cows using high-road-density areas and new (0–8 yr) cutblocks were more likely to die of apparent starvation.
- **Access management.**
  - The report recommends deactivating roads 200–400 m from key habitat.
  - "Only physical access management (gates, barricades, deactivation) can be expected to reliably affect hunter access."
- **Birth date.** The project uses an average birth date of 21 May.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Why it is a fair analogue.** This is a second-choice region, but it shares pine-dominated beetle and salvage landscapes, dense resource roads and a similar latitude (52–55° N).
- [I] **Suggested cut-age curve.** BC's shape (avoid 1–8 yr, select 9–24, avoid 25–40) matches the Ontario curve but runs later:

  | Cut age | Current (Ontario) | Suggested |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0–4 yr | 0.2 | 0.2 |
  | 5–8 yr | 0.7 | 0.5 |
  | 9–20 yr | 1.0 (10–20) | 1.0 |
  | 21–25 yr | 0.6 | 0.75 |
  | 26–30 yr | 0.6 | 0.4 |
  | 31–60 yr | 0.3 | 0.3 |
  | older | 0.2 | 0.2 |

- [I] **Moose-presence road factor.** Fall is the season when road distance best predicts where moose are, and hunter-kill risk rises near roads and new cuts. During open seasons (from 25 Aug archery in WMU 326, 1 Sept in WMU 324), apply this factor to habitat cells, separately from the hunter-access factor:
  - ×0.7 within 100 m of an open road
  - ×0.85 at 100–300 m
  - ×0.95 at 300–500 m
  - 1.0 beyond 500 m

  This rests on the 200–400 m dash distance and Québec's ≥500 m avoidance.
- [I] **Early winter (Nov).** Give pine-leading and broadleaf cuts relatively more weight, since BC moose selected both more in early winter.
- [I] **Distance from cover.** Keep the 80 m full-edge band. The current 0.1 beyond 400 m already keeps the score low on ground far from cover, consistent with the 200–400 m dash distance.
