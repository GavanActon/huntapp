# Elk at cameras in west-central Alberta harvest blocks (2022, 2023)
- **Source:**
  - McKay, T., Finnegan, L. 2023. Ungulate occurrence in forest harvest blocks is influenced by forage availability, surrounding habitat and silviculture practices. *Ecological Solutions and Evidence* 4: e12226 (doi 10.1002/2688-8319.12226; CC BY): https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2024-02/Ecol%20Sol%20and%20Evidence%20-%202023%20-%20McKay%20-%20Ungulate%20occurrence%20in%20forest%20harvest%20blocks%20is%20influenced%20by%20forage%20availability%20.pdf
  - McKay, T., Finnegan, L. 2022. Predator–prey co-occurrence in harvest blocks: implications for caribou and forestry. *Conservation Science and Practice* e12847 (doi 10.1111/csp2.12847): https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2022-11/Conservat%20Sci%20and%20Prac%20-%202022%20-%20McKay%20-%20Predator%20prey%20co%E2%80%90occurrence%20in%20harvest%20blocks%20%20Implications%20for%20caribou%20and.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text of both)
- **Evidence:** [S] peer-reviewed
- **Where and when:**
  - fRI Research caribou program, west-central Alberta: the Narraway, Redrock–Prairie Creek, A La Peche and Little Smoky caribou ranges plus 25 km. This is the Hinton–Grande Cache foothills, roughly 200–300 km NW of Clearwater.
  - Subalpine, upper and lower foothills and central mixedwood subregions.
  - Lured cameras in 117 harvest blocks (summer) and 98 (winter), 5 Jun 2018–29 Aug 2020: 22,963 summer and 12,408 winter camera-days.
  - Blocks were stratified by age (0–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–50 yr).
  - Seasons: summer 8 Apr–7 Nov, winter 8 Nov–7 Apr.

## Extracted facts
- **Occurrence, 2023** (detections per 100 camera-days):
  - Elk 0.340 in summer (46 sites) and 0.073 in winter (8 sites).
  - For comparison, summer: moose 1.407, mule deer 0.492, white-tailed deer 1.764.
  - There were too few winter elk detections to model.
- **Summer habitat model, 2023.**
  - Elk, moose and white-tailed deer occurrence was "higher in blocks with higher percent cover of *Calamagrostis canadensis*". Elk and white-tailed deer were "lower with higher percent cover of *Cornus canadensis*".
  - "Elk occurrence was also higher in blocks with a higher overall density of saplings, higher percentage of deciduous saplings, and a higher linear feature density in the surrounding area (5 km radius)."
  - Table 4 elk rows: sapling density β 0.095 (90% CI 0.013–0.177); % deciduous saplings β 0.128 (0.039–0.217).
- **Summer silviculture model, 2023.**
  - Elk occurrence was higher in "Harvest block <10 years" (β 0.264, 90% CI 0.059–0.469) and with a higher density of white spruce planted (β 0.134, 0.032–0.236).
  - "Deer, elk and moose occurrence was higher in younger harvest blocks."
  - "Low winter use of harvest blocks by deer, moose, and elk has been observed previously in our study area (Stelfox et al., 1976)."
- **Occupancy, 2022** (14-day occasions; same cameras):
  - Elk summer: naive 0.393, detection 0.115 (0.092–0.138), occupancy ψ 0.493 (0.394–0.592). Winter ψ 0.222 (0.000–0.450).
  - Summer ψ for others: moose 0.879, white-tailed deer 0.933, mule deer 0.593.
  - "elk and moose occupancy in harvest blocks increased with the percentage of deciduous forest within a 1 km radius" (elk β 4.603, 90% CI 0.671–8.534).
  - Wolf occupancy fell with road density within 1 km (β −1.160).
  - Co-occurrence with elk fell as the proportion of harvest blocks rose, for black bears (1 km, β −0.918) and for grizzlies (5 km, β −0.894).
- Authors: "harvest block age alone may not be an accurate indicator of available ungulate forage in our study area."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] In foothills cutblocks, elk are the patchiest ungulate: summer occupancy ~0.49 against ~0.9 for moose and white-tailed deer, and about a quarter of the occurrence rate. Even good foothills cut country holds elk thinly. That fits Clearwater's near-zero harvest.
- [I] A young-cut peak (<10 yr) supports moving the cut curve's peak earlier (elk-cutblock-forage-succession.md).
- [I] Deciduous matters twice: deciduous saplings in the block and deciduous forest within 1 km. Clearwater has 13% mixedwood and aspen in the stands. A deciduous term (block regen plus 1 km share) earns a place in the elk score.
- [I] Bluejoint (*Calamagrostis*) cover is the grass signal. Bunchberry (*Cornus canadensis*) cover marks shaded, closing stands with less elk use.
- [I] Linear features: these elk used blocks *more* where linear density within 5 km was higher, while Frair found inactive elk away from linear clearings. Use linear features as a fine-scale bedding and security cost, not as a landscape-scale penalty, until the conflict is resolved.
- [I] Winter use of cuts is minimal, so late-November cut value should drop.

## Not confirmed
- Whether lured cameras on trails bias elk detection relative to GPS.
- The exact block age split in the "<10 years" covariate (0–10 vs older classes).
- How far these Hinton-area numbers carry to Clearwater's lower foothills (more aspen, more white-tailed deer, more roads).
