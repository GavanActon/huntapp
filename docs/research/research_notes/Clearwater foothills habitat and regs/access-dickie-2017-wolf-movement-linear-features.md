# Wolves travel faster and farther on linear features (2016/2017)
- **Source:** Dickie, M., Serrouya, R., McNay, R.S. & Boutin, S. "Faster and farther: wolf movement on linear features and implications for hunting behaviour." *Journal of Applied Ecology*, doi:10.1111/1365-2664.12732. The PDF header reads "Journal of Applied Ecology 2016" (online version). The 2017 issue citation, vol. 54, pp. 253–263, was not checked against the PDF.
  - Read at: https://wildlifescience.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/PUB_Dickie_et_al-2016_Faster-and-farther-wolf-movement-on-linear-features-and-implications-for-hunting-behaviour.pdf
  - Data: https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.s7r47
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text, PDF)
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - North-eastern Alberta and north-western Saskatchewan: an 18,000 km² flat boreal mosaic at about 550 m elevation, covering the Cold Lake and East Side Athabasca caribou ranges.
  - 22 wolves in 6 packs, GPS-collared with 5-min fixes.
  - Summers (15 Apr–15 Jul) of 2013 and 2014, and winter (1 Jan–31 Mar) 2014.
  - 145,888 summer locations gave 49,239 travelling steps; 79,633 winter locations came from 11 wolves.

## Extracted facts
- **Linear features in the study area:**
  - "Linear feature density ranges from 0·52 to 15·89 km km⁻²".
  - Linear features covered about 9% of wolf home ranges, each class under 5%.
- **Widths (Table 1):**

  | Class | Width (m) |
  |---|---|
  | Low-impact seismic ("Sinuous, in a grid") | 7 |
  | Conventional seismic ("Long, straight") | 10 |
  | Trail | 12 |
  | Pipeline | 20 |
  | Road (includes winter roads, gravel roads and highways) | 30 |
  | Railway | 30 |
  | Transmission line | 37 |

- **Selection in summer:**
  - Odds of selecting conventional seismic, trails, pipelines, roads and transmission lines were about 2× those of forest. Railways were 6.39×.
  - 75% of wolves selected conventional seismic. 61% selected roads.
  - Low-impact seismic: 29% selected it and 43% avoided it.
- **Selection in winter:** roads 3×, railways 4×, transmission lines 8×; conventional seismic and pipelines about 2× (Results; the PDF text renders "×" as "9").
- **Speed:**
  - The Summary says: "Wolves travelled two to three times faster on linear features compared to the natural forest."
  - Summer: 1.25× faster on trails; 2× on conventional seismic, pipelines, railways and transmission lines; 3× on roads. On low-impact seismic, 31% slower (0.98 km/h).
  - Winter: 2× on conventional seismic, pipelines and railways; 3× on roads. On low-impact seismic 53% slower (0.64 km/h); on transmission lines 48% slower (0.70 km/h).
- **Daily distance:**
  - A 1 km/h rise in speed on linear features gave 12% (summer) and 19% (winter) more distance per day.
  - Daily distance rose 46% (summer) and 54% (winter) "for every hour wolves spent travelling on linear features".
  - In summer that is "6·9 km when using linear features for an hour".
- **Resting break point:** steps slower than 0.21 km/h were treated as resting or feeding.
- **Recommendation:** "mitigation strategies such as silviculture and linear deactivation (i.e. tree-felling and fencing) should prioritize conventional seismic lines … and pipelines … before low-impact seismic lines."
- **Roads were attractive but busy.** In the Discussion, "high human disturbance (i.e. on roads) may decrease attraction, suggesting a trade-off between facilitating movement and human avoidance".

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **The box is full of the line types wolves use most.** It has 3.58 km/km² of Base Features cutline-trails, mostly mapped 1984–2005 (widths unknown), plus 1.0 km/km² of roads. Expect wolves to run the straight, cleared lines: conventional seismic, pipelines and roads.
- [I] **Moose and elk tend to treat lines as risky** (Dickie 2020). A spot whose wind and cover are good but which sits on a straight open line is a predator lane. That argues for a small penalty for sitting right on a line, not for a bonus.
- [I] **Low-impact (narrow, sinuous) lines are not wolf highways.** To tell the classes apart, use the ABMI HFI 2023. Base Features cannot.

## Not confirmed
- Whether the result transfers from flat boreal NE Alberta to the rolling Lower/Upper Foothills. For foothills evidence see Finnegan et al. 2018.
- Which Clearwater cutlines are conventional seismic, low-impact seismic or pipeline.
