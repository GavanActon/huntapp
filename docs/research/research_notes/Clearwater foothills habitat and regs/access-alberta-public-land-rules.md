## Extracted facts
### Camping pass and random camping
- **Who needs the pass.** "Anyone 18 and older needs a Public Lands Camping Pass to random camp along the Eastern Slopes of the Rocky Mountains."
  - Required year-round in the pass area. It also applies on agricultural Crown land in the pass area, with the leaseholder's permission.
- **Cost:**
  - 3-day pass $20 per person, valid 72 h from 2 pm on the chosen date.
  - Annual pass $30 per person, valid 365 days.
  - Plus a $3.25 processing fee and GST per transaction, and a one-time $8 Wildlife Identification Number (WIN).
- **Exempt:**
  - under 18;
  - First Nations people with a Status card;
  - north of the Red Deer River: Métis Settlement members, recognized Métis harvesters and Aseniwuche Winewak Nation members;
  - AISH, Adult Health Benefit and Income Support recipients;
  - established fee campgrounds.
- **Day use** for other recreation needs no pass. Hunters who are clients of commercial outfitters still need one.
- **14-day rule.** "The maximum stay length is 14 days for random camping on public land." After that, move at least 1 km for at least 72 h. In law this is PLAR s.33.2(3), and stays longer than 14 days need an access permit (PLAR s.32(2)(a)).
- **Setbacks:**
  - No random camping within 1 km of a Public Land Recreation Area, Provincial Park or Provincial Recreation Area.
  - Camp ≥30 m from lakes and streams, keep human waste ≥70 m from water, and camp ≥100 m from oil and gas facilities.
  - A 1 km road setback applies only in the Kananaskis, McLean Creek, Sibbald and Cataract Creek areas, or "where posted".
### OHVs
- **Equipment and age (alberta.ca):** OHVs need "valid insurance and registration", a visible plate, a CSA-compliant helmet, a muffler and spark arrestor. Riders under 14 must be directly supervised.
- **Where (alberta.ca):** outside a PLUZ, motorized recreation is allowed on public land; follow posted rules and signs. "Stay off private and leased land unless permitted." OHVs are not allowed on highways.
- **Water (alberta.ca):** "Keep wheels out of water and off the bed and shore of waterbodies and wetlands", except at a designated crossing.
- **Water (PLAR s.43, the law):** no person shall enter or occupy the bed or shore of a permanent, naturally occurring water body, river, stream, watercourse or lake, unless:
  - boating;
  - travelling on frozen ground or ice that bears the load;
  - the use "does not involve the use of a wheeled or tracked conveyance" (or is within a designated water crossing);
  - the person holds a disposition or consent, or is exercising constitutional rights.
- **Closed roads (PLAR s.33(2)(d)):** an access permit is needed to enter a trail that "is a closed road as defined in section 54.01 of the Act".
- **Firearms on public land (PLAR s.31.1):** "No person shall discharge a firearm on public land in such a manner as to endanger other persons."
- **Off-road driving in general (Grizzly Bear Recovery Plan 2020, p. 49):** "it is not illegal to drive off road on vacant public land in Alberta unless there is a Public Land Use Zone that specifically restricts off-road use to designated trails."
- **Hunting guide, vehicles:**
  - "All off highway vehicles (OHVs) operated on public land must be registered, insured and have a visible licence plate".
  - "Off-highway vehicle (OHV) access is prohibited in most provincial parks and provincial recreation areas".
  - "Special conditions, such as extreme fire hazard, may warrant additional temporary access limitations."
  - The rule against carrying a weapon on an OHV from 1 h before sunrise to noon in big-game season applies only on public land in **WMUs 400–446**.
  - Kiska/Willson PLUZ: "Designated trails only with some seasonal restrictions" (WMUs 326, 328, 416–418, 420, 422, 426, 428–430).
### Hunting near roads, buildings and parks (hunting guide)
- **Vehicles:** no loaded or discharged firearm from "any kind of aircraft or vehicle whether it is moving or stationary".
- **Roads:** no discharge across or along "a provincial highway", or along "a road that is paved, oiled, graded or regularly maintained". Where there is no ditch or fence, that applies to "20 feet from the edge of the traveled portion".
- **Occupied buildings:** "Discharge a weapon within 183 m (200 yards)" of an occupied building is prohibited, as is letting a projectile pass within that distance.
- **Parks:** "In general, hunting or discharging a firearm (or bow) is prohibited in provincial parks and provincial recreation areas." Also: "It is unlawful to discharge a firearm within a forest recreation area."
- **Road corridor sanctuaries** (no hunting within 365 m of the centre-line): the named list does not include Hwy 752, the North Fork Road, or WMU 324/326. It does include parts of Hwy 734 and Hwy 11 elsewhere.
- **Permission:** "Permission is always required before entering or crossing … Public land under agricultural or grazing lease (from leaseholder)."
### Grazing dispositions (Recreational Access Regulation)
- **Covered dispositions (s.1(1)(b)):** "agricultural disposition" means a grazing lease, grazing permit, farm development lease, cultivation permit, grazing licence, authorization to harvest hay, or head tax grazing permit. "Recreational purposes" include hunting, camping and "the use of motor vehicles".
- **Grazing leases (GRL) and farm development leases:**
  - Users must contact the holder's contact person first, giving numbers, names and vehicle plates on request, the purpose, the date, time and duration, and the location (s.5(1)).
  - No contact is needed if the holder has not provided contact information, or has given notice that contact is not required (s.5(2)).
  - Do not enter if contact was not made, or if the holder does not respond (s.5(3)). A local settlement officer may then allow access (s.5(4)).
- **The holder must allow access (s.6(1))** unless:
  - (a) "the proposed use would involve the use of one or more bicycles, animals for transportation or motor vehicles";
  - (b) livestock are present in a fenced pasture unit, or a crop is unharvested;
  - (c) a fire ban is in force;
  - (d) the hunting would be "unreasonably close to a fenced pasture in which livestock … are present";
  - (d.1) or (d.2): discharge or explosives near such a pasture, or target shooting;
  - (e) the use is camping;
  - (f) the use breaches a recreational management plan.
  - A refusal must come with reasons (s.6(3)).
- **Users on a GRL may not** "park a vehicle within the boundaries of the agricultural disposition land or block an approach to the land without the consent" of the holder (s.9(1)(c)). They must also leave gates as found (s.9(2)(a)).
- **Grazing permits, cultivation permits, grazing licences, hay authorizations and head-tax permits:** the holder "shall not restrict or prohibit access for recreational purposes" unless a recreational management plan, condition or director's order does (s.8).
  - Users must not litter, must control animals, must not block approaches, must not enter buildings and must not cause damage. They must leave gates as found (s.9(3)–(4)).
- **"FGL" is the forest grazing licence.** PLAR Schedule 3 lists "FGL = Forest Grazing Licence" alongside "GRL = Grazing Lease" and "GRP = Grazing Permit". PLAR ss.62–67 ("Grazing Licences") give "the exclusive right to graze livestock in the licensed area", and the holder must act "consistent with the rights of other authorized users".
- **Alberta.ca:**
  - The regulation was amended on March 26, 2025.
  - Leaseholders can now limit e-biking and may "deny access for target shooting across the entire lease area".
  - "This does not change access for hunting, which remains the same."
  - Leaseholder contacts are on the RecAccess web map (geospatial.alberta.ca/RecAccess), or call 310-LAND (5263).
### Forest Reserve
- **Purpose (FRA s.4):** reserves are "for the conservation of the forests and other vegetation in the forests and for the maintenance of conditions favourable to an optimum water supply".
- **Other Acts apply (FRA s.11(1)):** "Except as otherwise provided in the regulations, the Forests Act and the Public Lands Act apply to all public land within a forest reserve." PLAR s.34(4): the Forest Reserves Act and its regulations prevail in any conflict.
- **Vehicle limits:** regulations may restrict "vehicular, pedestrian or other traffic" (FRA s.7(b)).
- **Grazing (FRR s.2(2)):** "grazing rights in public land are to be conferred under this Regulation rather than by disposition under the Public Lands Act". Grazing is by permits on designated range allotments.
- **Closures (FRR s.18):** an officer may close an area to all vehicles, or to specified types, where there is "a high risk of imminent harm to grazing livestock or of imminent damage to grazing rights or rangeland health". Signs must be posted on the roads and trails leading in.
- **Expiry:** the FRR expires May 31, 2028 unless re-passed.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Grazing leases (4.5% of the box): contact first, walk in.**
  - The leaseholder may refuse vehicles and horses, and may refuse access when cattle are in a fenced pasture.
  - Camping on the lease can be refused. Park outside the lease.
  - The app should flag GRL polygons as "contact leaseholder (RecAccess map)" and route access from the lease edge on foot.
- [I] **The forest grazing licence (8.7% of the box) is open.** The holder "shall not restrict or prohibit access for recreational purposes", so no contact is needed. That includes motor-vehicle use as a recreational purpose, subject to any recreational management plan or order. Leave gates as found.
  - The hunting guide's general "permission … grazing lease" line is about leases. Show the FGL as "open to the public; leave gates as found". This is an inference from s.8 and the FGL definition.
- [I] **Forest Reserve grazing (western third).** place-land-status-and-zones.md maps the Forest Reserve allotment "Prairie Creek" (Dry Creek distribution unit) over 9.9% of the box. Grazing there is by Forest Reserve permits, not Public Lands Act dispositions, so the Recreational Access Regulation's contact rule does not apply. Watch for posted vehicle closures under FRR s.18 and for the usual Public Lands Act / PLAR rules.
- [I] **Motorized access is legal almost everywhere in the box.** There is no PLUZ, OHVs may use cutlines, pipelines and trails, and off-road driving is not illegal outside PLUZs. The exceptions are water-body beds and shores, closed roads, PRAs, leases that refuse vehicles, and fire closures.
  - The pressure model should not assume that distance from a road means distance from hunters.
  - Stream and lake edges stay non-motorized by law, a minor "quieter" signal in their favour.
- [I] **The three PRAs** (Prairie Creek, Prairie Creek Group Camp, Strachan) are no-hunting and no-OHV areas. They are also people magnets.
  - Random camping is barred within 1 km of them: 14.0 km², 5.5% of the box.
  - Show them as "no hunting" and add a seasonal pressure ring around them, e.g. ×0.85 within 500 m (an inference).
- [I] **Farmland on the White Area edge.** Each driveway (Access FEATURE_TYPE 10) marks a likely occupied building. Draw a 183 m no-discharge ring around the end of each driveway; permission is needed on occupied private land anyway.
