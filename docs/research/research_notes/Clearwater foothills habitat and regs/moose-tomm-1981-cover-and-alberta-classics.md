## Extracted facts
- **Tomm et al. 1981 (abstract).** "Use of cut blocks by moose was related to size and interspersion of cut blocks with uncut mature timber and to levels of harassment... Moose used large clear cuts more effectively than deer. However, moose showed greater dependence on residual cut-block cover in areas of high levels of harassment."
- **Tomm et al. 1981, as cited by Higgelke & MacLeod 2000** (full text, https://isfort.uqo.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Higgelke-et-MacLeod.-2000.-Moose-Alces-alces.pdf):
  - "Where harassment by human activity is high, Tomm et al. (1981) have determined that Moose prefer to remain within 60 to 80 m of hiding cover though they will move up to 200 m outside of hiding cover where access is controlled."
  - "it is best if the animals can remain within 200 m of hiding cover though < 75 m is always optimal (Tomm et al. 1981)."
- **Tomm et al. 1981, as cited by Lamy & Finnegan 2019:** moose preferred clearcuts of 41–80 ha with at least 101 m between neighbouring blocks.
- **Higgelke & MacLeod 2000, other points** (from a KBM Forestry Consultants model for Millar Western; much of it is Gerry Lynch's personal communication, 1999):
  - "Lynch (pers. comm. 1999) has pointed out that Moose commonly seek open muskeg or riparian habitat with abundant willow growth in early winter... willow branches remain in reach of Moose as the tree grows whereas aspen quickly grows out of the animal's reach."
  - Moose "forage for willow along stream sides and for leaf litter on the forest floor of mature aspen stands in early winter".
  - Calving cows stay within 100 m of hiding cover (Penner 1997).
  - Dense seismic and utility lines (~50 m apart, with no cover) let hunters deplete moose.
  - The model allows at most 400 m between food and cover year-round. That figure comes from Ontario; Lynch endorsed it for Alberta.
  - Heat stress begins above 14 °C in summer and above 0 °C in winter (citing Renecker & Hudson 1990).
  - "It is possible that Moose in west-central Alberta do not often utilise severe winter cover."
  - Swan Hills seasonal ranges were 2,200–5,200 ha for males and 1,500–4,700 ha for females (Lynch & Morgantini 1980).
- **Telfer 1978**, *J. Wildl. Manage.* 42:352–361 (abstract, https://doi.org/10.2307/3800271): "moose utilization of study areas was positively related to the weight of winter browse per unit area". Elk and deer use was not strongly related to browse.
- **Rolley & Keith 1980**, *Can. Field-Nat.* 94:9–18 (abstract, https://doi.org/10.5962/p.347029):
  - "Moose selected treed muskegs and upland aspen (Populus tremuloides) stands < 10 m in height, and avoided agricultural clearings, roads, and dwellings."
  - Density rose from 0.016 to 0.75 moose/km² between 1965 and 1979.
  - Productivity tracked the previous winter's temperatures and snow depths.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **The 80 m edge band fits Alberta.** The app's 80 m full-edge band (Hamilton 1980, Ontario) matches Tomm's 60–80 m for harassed moose.
- [I] **Steepen the bands beyond 80 m.** Clearwater is heavily accessed: 73% of the box is within 500 m of a road, at ~1.0 km/km². Moose there should behave like Tomm's harassed animals. Tomm's 200 m figure applies only where access is controlled.

  | Distance from cover | Current edge value | Proposed |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0–80 m | 1.0 | 1.0 |
  | 80–200 m | 0.6 | 0.5 |
  | 200–400 m | 0.3 | 0.2 |
  | beyond 400 m | 0.1 | 0.1 |

- [I] **Early winter (from ~15 Oct).** Add +0.1 browse to:
  - shrubby wetland and riparian willow (Lynch's local knowledge via the model; Rolley & Keith's treed-muskeg selection);
  - mature aspen and balsam poplar stands, for leaf litter.
- [I] **November wetland values.** Raise treed wetland from 0.5 to 0.55, and open shrubby wetland from 0.45 to 0.6. This draws on Rolley & Keith (treed muskeg), Finnegan et al. 2023 (wet shrub) and ABMI's high relative abundance in marsh, fen and treed swamp. Leave open graminoid wetland at 0.45.
