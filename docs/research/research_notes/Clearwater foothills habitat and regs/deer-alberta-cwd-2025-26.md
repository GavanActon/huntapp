# Chronic wasting disease in Alberta deer, 2025–26 results (2026)
- **Source:**
  - Government of Alberta, "CWD updates" (2025–26 surveillance results). https://www.alberta.ca/chronic-wasting-disease-updates
  - Government of Alberta, "Chronic wasting disease history in Alberta". https://www.alberta.ca/chronic-wasting-disease-history-in-alberta
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (agency pages; the updates page displayed a date of 16 Oct 2026 when fetched)
- **Evidence:** [S] agency surveillance data; [G] agency statements on submission. The mandatory WMU list itself is in the 2026 Guide, which I did not re-read.
- **Where and when:** Province-wide hunter and targeted surveillance. Latest full season 2025–26: 6,819 wild cervids tested.

## Extracted facts
- **2025–26 results:**
  - "Overall, CWD was detected in 654 wild deer, elk and moose (9.7% overall". 654 of 6,819 is about 9.6%.
  - Mule deer: 25.1% of 1,794.
  - White-tailed deer: 3.7% of 4,477.
  - Elk: 9.7% of 308, mostly CFB Suffield.
  - Moose: 4.2% of 164, all CFB Wainwright.
- **By sex:**

  | Group | Positive | Tested |
  |---|---|---|
  | Male mule deer | 29.5% | 1,115 |
  | Female mule deer | 18.0% | 678 |
  | Male white-tailed deer | 4.1% | 3,327 |
  | Female white-tailed deer | 2.4% | 1,144 |

- Weighted occurrence estimates relative to male mule deer: male mule deer 1.00, female mule deer 0.4, male white-tailed deer 0.3, female white-tailed deer 0.1.
- "CWD continues to occur primarily in mule deer in comparison to white-tailed deer". Males are more likely infected than females.
- "The geographic distribution of CWD continues to expand westward." The lower provincial prevalence reflects more white-tailed deer sampled "along the western edge of the known CWD distribution".
- "CWD was detected in 7 Wildlife Management Units where it was not previously documented": **222, 300, 316, 320, 322, 503, 504**.
- **History page:**
  - First wild case on 2 Sept 2005: a mule deer near Acadia Valley.
  - 2010: "a significant extension of the disease westward along the Red Deer River", and the first case in the North Saskatchewan River valley.
  - 2021: "steady progression westward, particularly along the Bow river corridor northwest of Calgary".
  - The 2023–24 summary gives "42.3% of 870 male mule deer".
  - WMUs 324 and 326 are not named on either page.
- **Adjacency:** the WMU 326 aerial survey report (Government of Alberta, 2021) notes a moose seen "in adjacent WMU 320". See the mule deer note.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Mule deer, especially bucks, carry most CWD in Alberta. White-tailed deer carry it at about one-seventh of the mule deer rate in these samples (3.7% vs. 25.1%; 4.1% vs. 29.5% in males).
- [I] CWD was first found in WMU 320 (adjacent to 326) and in 322 in 2025–26. The western front is reaching the Clearwater foothills. The app's regs card should keep the WMU 324 mandatory head-submission line and could say the disease front is next door.
- [I] For habitat scoring CWD is not a habitat term. It is a regulation and handling note: head submission, and do not move carcass parts.
