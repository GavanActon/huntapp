# Ruffed grouse winter foraging HSI, Foothills Model Forest (1999)
- **Source:** Schaffer, W., B. Beck, J. Beck, M. Todd, R. Bonar and R. Quinlan. 1999. Ruffed Grouse Winter Foraging Habitat: Habitat Suitability Index Model, Version 3 (last modified 21 October 1999). Foothills Model Forest and Weldwood of Canada (Hinton Division), Hinton, Alberta; https://friresearch.ca/data/null/HSP_1999_10_Rpt_RuffedGrouseWinterForagingHabitat.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text, 7 pp.)
- **Evidence:** [S] agency/industry science. This is a literature-based HSI model. Its own rating: "verification level is 4: local data was used to develop the model but model predictions have not been tested."
- **Where and when:** Foothills Model Forest, Hinton, west-central Alberta. The model "applies to all forest and non-forest habitat areas of the Lower and Upper Foothills, Montane and Subalpine Natural Subregions". It covers the winter foraging season. Its Alberta inputs are Rusch & Keith 1971 (central Alberta), Boag & Sumanik 1969 (foothills), Doerr et al. 1974 (central Alberta), Schieck & Nietfeld 1995 and Rangen (FMF; cited as 1998 in the text, listed as 1997 in the references).

## Extracted facts
- "Populations of ruffed grouse are cyclic with major fluctuations every 8-10 years (Gullion 1984)." Ruffed grouse are "the most common grouse species in Alberta" (Salt & Salt 1976).
- "In central Alberta, ruffed grouse primarily occupy aspen forests, and utilize ground, shrub, and tree canopy strata for obtaining food and cover (Rusch and Keith 1971)."
- Fall foods: "a variety of fruiting shrubs, such as cranberries (Viburnum spp.), red-osier dogwood (Cornus stolonifera), and roses (Rosa acicularis)". These persist into winter and supplement "buds, twigs, and catkins of aspen".
- "The average age of 100 aspen trees used for foraging by ruffed grouse in Alberta was 36 years (range 20–80; Doerr et al. 1974)." One mature aspen gives 8–9 days of food for one grouse, so a grouse needs about 20 mature male aspen over an Alberta winter.
- Central Alberta: "80% (by volume) of the ruffed grouse winter diet was trembling aspen and willow buds (Doerr et al. 1974)". Beaked hazelnut, rose, balsam poplar and buffaloberry were also eaten.
- Fire or clearcut in aspen gives brood cover 4 years after disturbance (Gullion 1977). "After 10 years, stands open up due to self thinning and become too open for brood use but remain good for wintering and breeding ruffed grouse." "When fairly young habitat is within 100 m of mature male aspen trees, densities of grouse can be expected to be high (Gullion 1977)."
- "In central Alberta during the breeding season, ruffed grouse were in dense aspen stands with trees < 20 cm dbh that were between 20-27 yr old (Schieck and Nietfeld 1995)."
- "In the foothills of Alberta drumming males were in areas with the greatest density of young aspen and white spruce (Boag and Sumanik 1969)." Young spruce and fir in aspen were the immediate cover at drumming sites.
- Rusch & Keith 1971 drumming logs vs stations: shrub cover 66% vs 109%; saplings 3,815 vs 5,600 stems/ha; trees 850 vs 585/ha.
- Forests that are mostly conifer, "especially 'high pine' conifer", hold lower densities because predators ambush more easily (Gullion & Alm 1983).
- Densities: central Alberta 0.12–0.22 males/ha over a 3-year study (Rusch & Keith 1971). FMF young mixedwood had 0.22 drumming males/ha (Rangen 1998).
- Model: HSI = S1 × S2 × S3 (deciduous share of canopy; canopy closure; deciduous height). "Best winter foraging is provided by deciduous or mixedwood sites with > 20% deciduous tree composition, > 6% tree canopy closure, with trees > 10 m tall." Trees 0–2 m are unsuitable, and suitability rises from 2 to 10 m.
- Assumption 3: "Cover habitat is not limiting in the FMF", because regenerating mixedwood is abundant. Assumption 5: "Optimal winter food is provided by mature aspen stands."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] From October, feeding shifts to aspen ≥10 m tall (roughly ≥20 yr; mean feeding-tree age 36 yr). The current rule drops aspen to 0.6 at 26–40 yr and 0.35 past 40 yr. That undervalues October feeding stands; keep 25–60 yr aspen/poplar at about 0.7.
- [I] The model counts mixedwood with only ~20% deciduous as full winter food. The app's "hardwood ≥40%" gate for "aspen-ish" is stricter. A 20–40% deciduous mixedwood should score as mixedwood, not "other conifer 0.3".
- [I] The 100 m young-aspen-to-mature-aspen adjacency supports a cutblock-edge bonus: 5–25 yr aspen within 100 m of aspen ≥30 yr.
- [I] The October understory foods are highbush cranberry, red-osier dogwood and rose, in aspen understory and along streams. Alder alone is not a food source.
- [I] Pure lodgepole ("high pine") is poor ruffed grouse habitat. Lodgepole matters for spruce grouse, not ruffed.
