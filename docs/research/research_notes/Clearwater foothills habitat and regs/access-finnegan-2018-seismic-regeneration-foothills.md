# Seismic line regeneration and wolf/grizzly movement in the Alberta foothills (2018)
- **Source:** Finnegan, L., Pigeon, K.E., Cranston, J., Hebblewhite, M., Musiani, M., Neufeld, L., Schmiegelow, F., Duval, J. & Stenhouse, G.B. "Natural regeneration on seismic lines influences movement behaviour of wolves and grizzly bears." *PLoS ONE* 13(4): e0195480, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0195480.
  - Read at: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5901995/
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text through the PMC page, via a reading tool; the last ~25% of the page text was not read.
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - West-central Alberta, about 53–55 N and 117–120 W, over 10,772 km² of the Upper and Lower Foothills subregions.
  - It covers the Little Smoky caribou range and the low-elevation winter ranges of the A La Peche, Narraway and Redrock–Prairie Creek herds.
  - Animals: 24 wolves (7 packs; captured 2003–2004 and 2007–2009) and 19 grizzlies (GPS 2005–2009).
  - LiDAR collected 2005–2007, leaf-on.

## Extracted facts
- **Lines:**
  - "Mean seismic line densities were 1.45 km/km2". The total was 15,588 km.
  - Lines are about 8 m wide and were cleared before 1990.
- **Regrowth:**
  - Mean vegetation height on lines was 0.81 m (range 0–15 m).
  - 11,755 km (75%) of lines had mean vegetation under 1 m, decades after cutting.
- **Wolves:**
  - Moved toward lines regardless of vegetation height in winter. In spring they moved toward lines with higher vegetation.
  - In summer they favoured lower-vegetation lines and "moved faster near lines with vegetation <0.7 m". There was a piecewise break at 0.7 m.
- **Grizzlies:** "Seismic lines with lower vegetation height were preferred by grizzly bears during spring and summer". Vegetation height was not related to their movement rate.
- **Distance effect:** modelled with exponential decay, which "caused the effect of distance to decrease rapidly beyond 500 m" and holds constant beyond 2 km. The movement-rate analysis used locations within 100 m of lines.
- **Introduction, citing other work:** "predator movement rates on seismic lines can be up to double those in the forest interior".
- **Conclusions:**
  - "seismic lines continue to influence wolf movement behaviour decades after they are built".
  - "natural regeneration of seismic lines may be insufficient to successfully restore seismic lines".
  - Lines "with vegetation of less than 1 m could be prioritized for restoration".

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Expect old Clearwater cutlines to be mostly open.** Most were mapped by 1984–2005 (widths unknown), in foothills like Finnegan's. Finnegan's foothills lines averaged 0.81 m of vegetation and 75% were under 1 m. Open lines are where wolves move fast in summer and where grizzlies feed in spring and summer.
- [I] **The baked LiDAR (canopy height) can grade every cutline.**
  - Vegetation under 0.7–1 m means an open lane: predator travel, a sight line, and an easy OHV or foot route.
  - Vegetation over 1–2 m means a closing line.
  - That grading is a better input than line presence alone.
- [I] **Ungulates walk lines that carry a trail and forage** (Tattersall). Elk in the central foothills spent "little time near cutlines" (FRIAA).
  - Score a cutline as a travel lane at crossings and edges.
  - Do not score it as a sit-on-it feeding spot.
- [I] **Range of the line effect.** Use ≤500 m for any predator-lane effect, from Finnegan's decay; nothing beyond 2 km.

## Not confirmed
- Vegetation height on Clearwater's own cutlines. It is not measured yet but can be derived from the area's LiDAR.
- Elk, deer and moose speed or selection on foothills seismic lines in metres. Only the summary of Tattersall 2023 was read.
