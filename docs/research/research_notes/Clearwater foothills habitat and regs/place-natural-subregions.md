## Extracted facts
- Box shares (polygon layer): **Lower Foothills 59.8%** of the region, 72.0% of the core, 75.5% of the 5 km ring, and the centre; **Upper Foothills 40.2%** (28.0% of the core), on the higher west and south ground. Natural Region: Foothills throughout.
- Upper Foothills (s. 4.2.1): "Elevations range from about 1050 m in the north to more than 1700 m in the south and west along its boundary with the Subalpine"; average 1,300 m (950–1,750 m). Theme: "Short, wet summers and snowy, cold winters … Closed-canopy conifer stands of lodgepole pine, black spruce and white spruce on rolling to steeply sloping terrain." Key features: "Lodgepole pine is usually dominant in pure stands on mesic sites"; "Pure deciduous stands are generally restricted to southerly aspects, and mixed conifer–deciduous stands are much less common in the Upper than in the Lower Foothills." It has "the highest July precipitation of any Natural Subregion"; Labrador tea is a common understory.
- Lower Foothills (s. 4.2.2): from "about 700-800 m in the north and east … to over 1500 m in the south and west"; average 950 m (650–1,625 m). Theme: "a climatic transition area, with cold winters typical of Boreal climates and higher winter snowfalls typical of Cordilleran climates. The rolling, till-covered plateaus are forested by mesic, closed canopy mixed stands of aspen, lodgepole pine, white spruce and balsam poplar." "The Lower Foothills Natural Subregion has the most diverse forests in Alberta … Aspen, balsam poplar, white birch, lodgepole pine, black spruce, white spruce, balsam fir and tamarack grow as pure stands or as mixtures … Shrubby grasslands occur on the driest sites, and poor to rich fens dominated by black spruce, tamarack, shrubs and herbs occur on low, wet sites." "The occurrence of pure deciduous stands on all aspects distinguishes this Natural Subregion from the Upper Foothills." Low-bush cranberry is a common understory.
- Alpine (s. 4.1.1): "includes all areas above tree line in the Rocky Mountain Front and Main Ranges. It occurs at elevations as low as 1900 m in the north"; average 2,350 m (1,900–3,650 m).
- Distances from the centre (polygon layer): Upper Foothills 0.2 km; Dry Mixedwood 14.5 km (east); Subalpine 18.2 km (SW, edge at about 1,700 m); Alpine 38.5 km (SSW, edge at about 1,960 m); Montane 61.5 km; Central Parkland 56.7 km.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] The box is the Lower-to-Upper Foothills transition at 1,000–1,370 m: mixedwood with aspen and balsam poplar on all aspects in the Lower Foothills part, closed lodgepole pine and spruce in the Upper Foothills part, with black spruce–tamarack fens in the low spots. Pure aspen on the higher (Upper Foothills) ground is mostly on south faces.
- [I] Treeline is 600 m or more above the box's highest ground; the alpine is 30–40 km away. Ptarmigan and treeline bands have no place here.
- [I] Snowier than boreal Ontario in winter (Cordilleran snowfall), with wet Julys; snow, not the date, ends the fall pattern later in November.
