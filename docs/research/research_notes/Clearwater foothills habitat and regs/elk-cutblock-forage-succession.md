## Extracted facts
- **Visscher & Merrill 2009** (snippet text):
  - "forage biomass peaked approximately 9 years following felling for both herbaceous (graminoids and forbs) and palatable browse forage."
  - Forage was "determined primarily by the age of the cutblock (time since felling) and to a lesser extent elevation, compound topographic index … and the distance to the nearest deciduous forest patch."
  - Browse composition "shifted from palatable species to unpalatable species" after about 30 years.
  - Home-range simulations compared "even-flow" with "pulsed" cutting, the latter likened to mountain pine beetle control. Under even-flow, forage stayed above current levels when more than ~10 ha were cut a year, and cover stayed above a 60% guideline. Pulsed cutting made forage swing as the cut cohort aged.
- **Stelfox et al. 1976** (Hinton; 10-chain cut strips, 5-chain leave strips):
  - "Use by deer, elk and moose decreased during the first 5 years following clearcut logging with the decrease most pronounced in scarified cut-overs."
  - Browse: by year 5 the unscarified cut exceeded the unlogged forest's 529 lb/acre. In years 9 and 17 it produced 3× and 4× the unlogged forest's browse; the scarified cut averaged 10% below the unscarified one.
  - Forage capacity was one elk per 3.6 acres, but summer use was one elk per 71.1 acres.
  - Winter use "dropped drastically … apparently due to the absence of adequate cover, to no moose or elk".
- **Farr et al. 2025:**
  - Herbaceous land cover declined while shrub and forest area grew (woody encroachment), lowering the share of annual biomass in herbaceous areas.
  - "rising forage biomass in conjunction with increased area of logged forests in the foothills ecoregion"; burned area declined.
- **Alberta feral horse page:** "Feral horses prefer grassy meadows, shrublands, and cutblocks about 7 years old or younger" (paraphrased by the fetch tool).
- **Sibling notes on Stelfox 1988 (Hinton, 32 years;** moose-stelfox-1988-hinton-clearcuts.md and deer-stelfox-1988-hinton-clearcuts.md; I did not re-read the report):
  - "Security (hiding) and thermal cover was a greater determinant of habitat use of clear-cuts by deer, elk and moose than forage availability".
  - Elk use fell sharply after the 100 m residual seed blocks were cut at years 12–13.
  - Elk largely left road-accessible cuts within a week of the September opener.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] The foothills forage peak is ~9 years (Visscher & Merrill) and elk avoid the first ~5 years (Stelfox), most of all on scarified sites. The plan's cut curve, built on Starkey (peak 14 yr, 1.0 for 10–20 yr, 0.7 for 21–33 yr), peaks ~4–6 years too late and keeps the tail too long for pine and spruce foothills.
- [I] Suggested foothills cut curve (forage value at night and twilight; hand-set):

  | Years since cut | 0–2 | 3–5 | 6–12 | 13–17 | 18–25 | 26–33 | older |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Value | 0.4 (0.3 if scarified) | 0.7 | 1.0 | 0.8 | 0.5 | 0.3 | 0.2 |

  - Plus up to +0.15 for deciduous regeneration or a deciduous patch nearby (Visscher & Merrill's distance-to-deciduous driver).
- [I] At Clearwater this re-weights the box's cut classes: 5–9 yr (4.2% of the box) becomes the peak, and 21–30 yr (8.1%, the largest class) drops from 0.7 to ~0.3–0.5.
- [I] Cuts are a summer and fall resource; winter use is near zero. In late November (rifle, snow) cut value should fall.
- [I] Cutblocks ≤7 yr old are also what feral horses select (elk-feral-horses-sundre.md); inside equine zones the youngest cuts are shared.
- [I] Pulsed (beetle-control) cutting gives synchronous forage booms and busts. The box's largest cut class (21–30 yr) is such a cohort now ageing out.

## Not confirmed
- The Visscher & Merrill full text: curves by forage class, the cover definition, and the "10 ha/yr" condition (seen only in a snippet).
- Whether the 9-year peak differs between pine and spruce sites, or with herbicide use (the moose herbicide note covers forage, not elk use).
- No foothills study of elk use of mountain pine beetle-killed standing stands was found.
