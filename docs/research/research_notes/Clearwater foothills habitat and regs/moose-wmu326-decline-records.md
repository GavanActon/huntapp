# WMU 326 moose decline: survey and harvest records (2005–2025)
- **Source:** Alberta Environment and Parks 2021. *Wildlife Management Unit 326 Aerial Ungulate Survey for Moose (2019-2020).* Aerial Wildlife Survey Report, published April 2021. https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/0f127e10-1495-43ff-9304-4ecc58664cb7/resource/d7e165e9-b97a-4d97-a4a3-7d5d5ace6614/download/aep-awsr-wmu-326-aerial-ungulate-survey-moose-2019-2020.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text of the WMU 326 and WMU 318/324 reports, the harvest tables for 2010–2025, and Pybus 1999; abstracts for the other supporting papers.
- **Evidence:** [G] agency survey and harvest data; [S] supporting papers; one [H] press release.
- **Where and when:**
  - WMU 326 is 1,046 km² of Lower Foothills, south of the North Saskatchewan, west of the Clearwater and east of the Ram, with Rocky Mountain House to the north-east.
  - Surveyed in February 2010 by random stratified block (RSB) and on 22–23 Jan 2020 by strip transects.

## Extracted facts
- **WMU 326, 2020 survey.**
  - 28 east–west transects 1.6 km apart, each a 400 m strip (25% coverage); 603.9 km flown in 10.4 h. The report states the area searched as "970 km2", which is apparently an error: 603.9 km × 0.4 km = 242 km².
  - Conditions: −5 to +5 °C, "some steep slopes bare of snow".
  - Count: 35 moose in 22 groups; 13 bulls, 12 cows, 9 calves.
  - Density 0.14/km² (90% CI 0.07–0.20); abundance 141 (75–206). "No correction factor was applied."
- **WMU 326 history (Table 1):**

  | Year | Method | Moose | Density per km² | Bull:cow:calf |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2005 | RSB | 300 | 0.29 | unknown |
  | 2010 | RSB | 417 | 0.40 (±24%) | 80:100:13 |
  | 2020 | Transect | 141 | 0.14 (±66%) | 108:100:75 |

  The report calls the 2020 ratios "artificially high due to the small numbers".
- **The report's only explanation for the drop.**
  - "The dominant land-cover is dense conifer forest making wildlife sighting challenging. Cut-blocks comprise about 35% of the unit."
  - "While the different survey methods (transect vs. random stratified block) may partially explain the change in moose numbers observed, the marked decline in moose populations is a concern."
  - Ticks, wolves and harvest are not mentioned.
- **WMU 326 seasons and other sightings (2020 report).**
  - Antlered moose: archery 25 Aug–23 Sept; rifle 24 Sept–31 Oct and 1–30 Nov. Special licence only; no antlerless season.
  - Five-year mean antlered success ~35% (range 25–50%).
  - Also seen on the survey: 16 feral horses and 11 deer (4 mule, 5 white-tailed, 2 unknown). No elk.
- **WMU 324 (and 318), 2018 report.** https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/bc9fef0e-8dd8-4681-873e-661c41ed8608/resource/a411940b-028c-41a9-bda5-3ba746450cc9/download/wmu318-324moosesurvey-2018.pdf
  - WMU 324 (1,026 km²): 2010 RSB 195 moose (0.19/km²); 2018 transect 150 (0.15/km², 90% CI 0.08–0.21, ±43%); bull:cow:calf 59:100:59.
  - The combined 318/324 RSB series ran 0.37 (1996), 0.22 (2000), 0.30 (2003), 0.24 (2005), 0.32 (2008); the 2018 transect gave 0.22.
  - WMU 324 seasons in 2018: antlered and antlerless archery 1 Sept–31 Oct, rifle 1–30 Nov, special licence only. Five-year success: antlered 37%, antlerless 26%.

  - "Estimated sightability on the transect line was 67% in 2010 and 46% in 2012."

- **Winter ticks** [G]. Pybus 1999, *Moose and ticks in Alberta: a dieoff in 1998/99*, NRS Occasional Paper 20. https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/b19cad0d-21bc-4f08-b430-f1e2b9d62c05/resource/619f6471-3676-4deb-9e28-db1cf868494e/download/1999-mooseticksalbertaadieoff-oct-1999.pdf
  - 1,130 moose occurrence reports, Jan–Apr 1999: 92% showed hair loss and 28% of those were dead; 96% came from boreal habitats of northern and western Alberta.
  - 69% came from four districts: Grande Prairie, Valleyview, Whitecourt and Athabasca. Nordegg reported none and Sundre reported 2.
  - The WMU table lists one record for WMU 320 and none for WMU 324 or 326.
  - Drivers: a mild, low-snow winter in 1997/98, a hot dry summer, a long warm fall, then snow arriving late, in January.
- **Wolves** [S]. Webb, Allen & Merrill 2011, *Can. J. Zool.* 89:744–752 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1139/z11-043). West-central Alberta wolves, 2003–2008: annual harvest rate 0.34 (trappers 0.22, hunters 0.12), harvest "likely sustainable".
- **Deer** [S]. Latham et al. 2011, *J. Wildl. Manage.* 75:204–212 (abstract; https://doi.org/10.1002/jwmg.28). NE Alberta, 1994–97 vs 2005–09:
  - White-tailed deer rose 17.5-fold while "moose remained unchanged".
  - Wolves rose from about 6 to 11.5 per 1,000 km², and deer replaced moose as their main prey.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Part of the drop is survey method.** The 2020 strip-transect count in dense conifer was uncorrected.
  - On-line sightability in the region was 46–67% (Peters 2014) and is lower across a 200 m half-strip. So the true 2020 density was probably ≥0.2/km².
  - That is still below 2010, and WMU 324 also fell (0.19 → 0.15).
  - Treat the area as low-density moose country (~0.15–0.25/km², or 1.5–2.5 per 10 km²) rather than a collapse.
- [I] **Licensed harvest is an unlikely main cause.** It was bull-only at 6–21 per year, and bull:cow ratios were 80–108:100. Unreported Indigenous and illegal kills are unknown; provincially, undocumented kill was estimated at 30% of legal harvest (Rippin 2001).
- [I] **Recruitment.** The 2010 calf:cow ratio of 13:100 is very low and would come before a decline. Its cause (predation, ticks or nutrition) is unknown. The 1999 tick die-off left no records in WMUs 324 or 326.
- [I] **Ageing cutblocks fit the timing.** Cutting in the box peaked 21–30 yr ago (8.1% of land cut about 1996–2005) and has slowed since (6.1% at 10–20 yr, 4.2% at 5–9 yr, 2.7% at 0–4 yr).
  - Those blocks were 5–14 yr old (prime browse) at the 2010 survey and 15–24 yr (closing pine and spruce) by 2020.
  - This matches Stelfox's Hinton peak at ~17 yr and decline by ~26 yr.
  - Untested: the box covers only ~11% of WMU 326.
- [I] **For the app.** Absolute moose density is low, so a high habitat score should not suggest a high chance of seeing moose. With draw-only, antlered-only hunting and 3–10 licences, the moose layer serves few hunters.
