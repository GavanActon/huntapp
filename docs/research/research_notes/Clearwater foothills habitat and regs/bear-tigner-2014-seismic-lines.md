# Black bear use of seismic lines in northern Canada (2014)
- **Source:** Tigner, J., E.M. Bayne & S. Boutin. 2014. Black bear use of seismic lines in Northern Canada. Journal of Wildlife Management 78(2):282–292. doi:10.1002/jwmg.664. Abstract from Crossref, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/jwmg.664, and the CCLM summary, https://www.cclmportal.ca/resource/black-bear-use-seismic-lines-northern-canada
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (abstract only; the Wiley full text is paywalled and the University of Alberta ERA thesis copy refused connection)
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:** Boreal caribou range with oil and gas seismic networks. The abstract says only "northwestern Canada". The paper is also a chapter of Tigner's 2014 University of Alberta thesis; that it was set in north-eastern Alberta is my assumption, not verified. Remote cameras were used at two scales: individual lines and 5-km² cells.

## Extracted facts
- "Black bears used most types of seismic lines more frequently than undisturbed forest interior and upland forest more than lowland forest."
- There was no interaction between lines and forest type. "At the 5-km² scale, we found no evidence of increased use of lowland forest in areas with greater seismic line density."
- "Bears do not appear to use seismic lines ≤2 m wide more often than the forest interior." The authors recommend new lines be no wider than 2 m.
- **Related, abstract only:** Latham, Latham & Boyce 2011 (north-eastern Alberta, during caribou calving), https://www.cclmportal.ca/resource/habitat-selection-and-spatial-relationships-black-bears-ursus-americanus-woodland-caribou. "Black bears avoided bogs and fens, while selecting upland mixed woods and various industrial features."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Seismic lines and pipelines are common in the box. Black bears probably travel and feed along lines wider than 2 m that have regrown with shrubs, mostly in upland forest.
- [I] A small bonus is defensible: +0.1 within about 50 m of a line or pipeline wider than 2 m in upland forest. No bonus for low-impact lines 2 m wide or less.
- [I] These lines are also where hunters see bears and where hunting pressure falls. The road and line pressure term (see the FMF note) partly offsets the bonus in fall.

## Not confirmed
- The season of camera deployment, effect sizes, exact study area and years are all unknown.
- It is unknown whether the result holds in the foothills.
- Nothing here is fall-specific.
