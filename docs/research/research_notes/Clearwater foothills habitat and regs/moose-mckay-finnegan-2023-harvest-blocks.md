# Moose, deer and elk use of harvest blocks, west-central Alberta (2023)
- **Source:** McKay, T., Finnegan, L. 2023. Ungulate occurrence in forest harvest blocks is influenced by forage availability, surrounding habitat and silviculture practices. *Ecological Solutions and Evidence* 4:e12226. https://doi.org/10.1002/2688-8319.12226. Read at https://www.cclmportal.ca/sites/default/files/2024-02/Ecol%20Sol%20and%20Evidence%20-%202023%20-%20McKay%20-%20Ungulate%20occurrence%20in%20forest%20harvest%20blocks%20is%20influenced%20by%20forage%20availability%20.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Full text; supplementary tables not read.
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - West-central Alberta, in the Narraway, Redrock–Prairie Creek, A La Peche and Little Smoky caribou ranges plus a 25 km buffer. This is roughly the Grande Cache–Hinton–Fox Creek country, north-west of Clearwater (position approximate).
  - Subalpine, Upper Foothills, Lower Foothills and Central Mixedwood subregions; elevation 766–1,590 m.
  - Lured camera traps at the centroids of 117 harvest blocks in summer and 98 in winter, 5 Jun 2018 to 29 Aug 2020.
  - Blocks were 3–48 yr old, stratified by age (0–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–50), ecosite and surrounding disturbance.
  - Seasons: summer 8 Apr–7 Nov, winter 8 Nov–7 Apr.

## Extracted facts
- **Seasonal occurrence.** Moose detections per 100 camera-days were 1.407 in summer (98 sites) and 0.613 in winter (49 sites). Summer use exceeded winter use for every ungulate (p < 0.001).
- **Summer moose habitat model** (standardized β, 90% CI, Table 4):

  | Covariate | β | 90% CI |
  |---|---|---|
  | % cover *Salix* (willow) | +0.268 | 0.135, 0.400 |
  | % cover *Calamagrostis canadensis* (bluejoint) | +0.175 | 0.007, 0.343 |
  | % cover *Equisetum* (horsetail) | +0.290 | 0.155, 0.425 |
  | Road density within 1 km | −0.188 | −0.320, −0.055 |

- **Winter moose habitat model:**

  | Covariate | β | 90% CI |
  |---|---|---|
  | Deciduous sapling density | +0.667 | 0.420, 0.914 |
  | *Salix* biomass | +0.179 | 0.051, 0.307 |
  | *Viburnum* (highbush cranberry) biomass | +0.467 | 0.265, 0.670 |

- **Silviculture models** (Table 5):
  - Summer moose: number of times planted, +0.272 (0.100, 0.443). No block-age term entered.
  - Winter moose: "Harvest block ≤25 years" (a 0/1 term), +0.649 (0.170, 1.129).
  - The authors' general line: "Deer, elk and moose occurrence was higher in younger harvest blocks."
- **Herbicide and planting.**
  - No chemical stand-tending or herbicide term entered a final moose model. Herbicide rates of 0–6.02 L/ha were tested.
  - Planting density of lodgepole pine lowered mule deer (β −0.114) and white-tailed deer (β −0.274), not moose.
  - Authors: silviculture "resulting in higher pine densities with less competing vegetation could result in reduced use of harvest blocks by deer, moose and elk".
- **Disturbance.**
  - Moose occurrence was lower where road density within 1 km was higher.
  - White-tailed deer were more common farther from seismic lines.
  - Active-wellsite density was a candidate term but entered no final model.
- **Ranges of the candidate disturbance terms in the study area:** road density 0–2.98 km/km² (1 km radius), seismic lines 0–6.27 km/km², active wellsites 0–0.757 per km².

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Sept to early Nov.** This hunting window falls in the study's "summer" season. Then, block age did not predict moose but willow, horsetail and bluejoint cover did. Score wet, willowy, horsetail-rich blocks above dry pine blocks of the same age, and lean on the cut-age curve mainly from November.
- [I] **Winter (from about 8 Nov).** Blocks ≤25 yr scored higher, as did deciduous saplings, willow and highbush cranberry. The Ontario curve's 0.6 for 21–30 yr should hold to ~25 yr and then drop to ~0.4 for conifer-regenerated blocks.
- [I] **Roads.** Road density in the surrounding 1 km lowered moose use of blocks. Clearwater's ~1.0 km/km² sits inside this study's range (0–2.98), so the result transfers.
- [I] **Herbicide.** No measurable effect on moose occurrence. Any herbicide factor should be mild until block-level treatment data exist; see the FRI review note.

## Not confirmed
- Cameras were only inside harvest blocks, so the study could not test selection for blocks against other habitat.
- Lure was used. Winter models rest on few detections, and some cameras failed in the cold.
- The pairwise comparisons of site-preparation and herbicide methods are in the supplement, which I did not read.
- The study area is caribou range with less road than Clearwater, some 150–300 km away (approximate).
