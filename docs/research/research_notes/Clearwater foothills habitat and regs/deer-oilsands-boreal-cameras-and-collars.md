- **Where and when:**
  - Fisher papers: NE Alberta boreal, ~3,000–3,500 km² of spruce, aspen, jack pine and muskeg with in-situ oil sands. 62 cameras on wildlife trails, ≥200 m from access roads and ≥2 km apart, Oct/Nov 2011 – Oct/Nov 2014; 112,648 white-tailed deer images.
  - Darlington: the Christina Lake area near Conklin, 3,500 km². 36 GPS-collared female white-tailed deer, 2012–2014, 99,148 fixes at 2-h intervals.

## Extracted facts
- **Fisher et al. 2020, winter:**
  - Snow >30 cm (Fort McMurray station; 30-yr mean maximum 30 cm) on 0 days in 2011–12, 125 days in 2012–13 and 73 days in 2013–14.
  - Site extinction ε was 0.15 after the mild winter, then 0.39 and 0.46. Spatial growth λ fell to 0.61 in the severe winter (0.8 in the mild one).
  - Springs rebounded: "λ = 1.8, SE = 0.24". Distribution "dropped nearly 50%" in severe winters.
- **Fisher et al. 2020, landscape:**
  - Of 20 radii from 250 to 5,000 m, the 1,000 m radius fit best (AICw 0.98 annual, 0.90 winter).
  - Annual coefficients: upland deciduous +0.03, forest harvest blocks +0.07, petroleum well sites +0.24, industrial block features +0.41, "all trails (unpaved roads)" −0.88.
  - Winter coefficients: +0.02, +0.11, +0.14 (n.s.), +0.57 and −1.50 respectively. "Deer's negative response to trails ... was twice as great in winter".
  - Fall and rut were not analysed separately.
- **Fisher & Burton 2021, fawns:**
  - Fawns were detected at 58.1% of sites in 2012, 35.5% in 2013 and 19.4% in 2014, after the severe winters.
  - "Probability of fawn occurrence more than tripled from ~0% seismic line density to only 1.5% seismic line density"; by contrast it "roughly doubles from 0% broadleaf forest to 80% broadleaf forest".
  - Effects of anthropogenic features were about 100× the best natural land-cover feature.
  - For cutblocks driving reproductive success: "we found no evidence to support this".
- **Darlington et al. 2022:**
  - Seasons: winter 1 Jan – 30 Apr; parturition 1 May – 30 Jun; summer 1 Jul – 30 Sep; **rut 1 Oct – 31 Dec**.
  - The cumulative model (footprint + natural + predators) was best in every season.
  - Log distance to 0–10-yr cutblocks (negative = selection): winter −0.53, parturition −0.65, **rut −1.01 (CI −1.07 to −0.95)**, summer −1.06.
  - "Deer strongly selected for linear features such as roads and trails". Trails in the rut −0.44. 3D seismic lines were avoided (winter +0.28).
  - Wolf-occurrence selection in the rut +0.35. Aspen was "the strongest positive driver" among natural features.
  - Available points came from a buffered seasonal MCP; the rut buffer radius was 3,903 m.
  - Agriculture, hunting and time of day were not analysed.
- **Latham et al. 2011 (abstract):** white-tailed deer increased about 17.5-fold between 1994–97 and 2005–09 while moose stayed level. Wolves rose from about 6 to 11.5 per 1,000 km², and deer replaced moose as main prey.
- **Fisher & Burton 2018 (abstract):** anthropogenic features best explained the distributions of 10 mammals, favouring "generalist predators and browsers".

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Clearwater is oil and gas country. Well sites and pads, pipelines, and industrial clearings that stay in grass and forbs are deer forage, as are 0–10-yr cutblocks. At the 1 km scale these raised white-tailed deer occurrence in the boreal. Treat grassed pads and pipeline rights-of-way like a field or early-seral patch under the deer edge rules, not as roads.
- [I] Roads and trails: camera sites near trails had fewer deer, yet 2-hourly GPS does (night included) selected roads. Read roads as travel and night features. In the hunting season, a daytime stand on or beside a road is poor (see the Stelfox note).
- [I] Seismic lines: positive for fawns at 1 km, avoided (3D lines) by collared does, and negative locally in McKay & Finnegan. Keep seismic lines neutral in the deer score.
- [I] Aspen and upland deciduous carry the deer more than conifer. Keep hardwood and mixed browse at 0.85–0.9 for deer.
- [I] Winter severity limits deer at the boreal edge. Clearwater sits in core white-tailed deer range at 52° N, so this limit matters little in the Sept–Nov season (see the winter severity note).

## Not confirmed
- Units of the Fisher et al. 2020 coefficients (proportion vs. density at 1 km) were not captured.
- Darlington's rut-season coefficients for roads, wells, pipelines and tree species were shown only in figures. The text gives "strongly selected" for roads, without a number.
- No fall-specific diel or hunting analysis in any of these studies.
- These are NE Alberta oil-sands boreal studies, not the foothills.
