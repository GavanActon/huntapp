# Grouse through 32 years of clearcut succession near Hinton (1988)
- **Source:** Stelfox, J.G. 1988. Forest Succession and Wildlife Abundance Following Clear-cut Logging in West-Central Alberta. Report dated 15 December 1988. It acknowledges Weldwood of Canada (Hinton Division), the Alberta Forest Technology School and Alberta Fish and Wildlife (Edson). https://www.archive.org/download/forestsuccession00stel/forestsuccession00stel.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text, OCR scan of 166 pp.; tables are garbled, so text sections §4.3.5 and the summary were used)
- **Evidence:** [S] agency science: a long-term field study with small samples, using sightings and pellet-group counts.
- **Where and when:** Foothills near Hinton (53° N, 117° W). Blocks: old (125–140 yr) white spruce and mature (80–100 yr) mixedwood at about 1,000 m, and young-mature (60–70 yr) lodgepole pine at about 1,350 m. Logged, some blocks scarified, in 1956–57 and followed to Year 32 (1988). Snow cover runs from early November to mid/late April.

## Extracted facts
- Summary: "Spruce and ruffed grouse disappeared following logging while light use by sharp-tailed and blue grouse occurred."
- "Grouse were absent or scarce during Years 1-15 (Grass-forb and Shrub-seedling stages). Only 25% of grouse observations during the first 27 years occurred during Years 1-15 compared with 75% during Years 16-27 (Pole-sapling stage)."
- Years 16–27: "Mixedwood clear-cuts were most productive (59% of observations), then white spruce, (32%), and lastly lodgepole pine (9%)". "More grouse were observed in unscarified clear-cuts (86%) compared with 14% for scarified."
- Spruce grouse "were not seen in any clear-cuts during the first 15 years although they were common in all three mature forests." The first spruce grouse in clearcuts appeared in Year 17 (unscarified spruce), Year 26 (unscarified mixedwood) and Year 27 (unscarified pine). "In Year 32, spruce grouse were abundant in both pine clear-cuts".
- Ruffed grouse were "72% of all grouse seen during Years 1-27": 44% in Years 1–15 and 81% in Years 16–27. "No ruffed grouse were seen in the unscarified pine clear-cut during Years 1-32", and 2 were seen in the scarified pine block in Years 16–27.
- Year 32: "ruffed grouse were observed in the transitional zone between these pine clear-cuts and the open, deciduous cover adjacent to the perimeter road." In the scarified spruce clearcut there were three ruffed grouse broods and an average of 22 faecal groups/ha.
- "Blue grouse summered only in the spruce scarified clear-cut near the mountains during Years 1-26. They did not occur in the adjacent unscarified and mature spruce blocks nor in the mixedwood and pine blocks that were more than 15 km from the mountains." "None were seen in winter."
- "Sharp-tailed grouse were only observed in the scarified pine clear-cut at Year 3".
- "During Years 1-27, only 11% of the grouse were observed during winter indicating that these young clear-cuts were generally not providing adequate winter cover. However, in Year 32, both pine and spruce clear-cuts appeared to be supporting grouse equally well in winter and summer."
- In mixedwood clearcuts, grouse were 4 times more abundant in unscarified than in scarified and mature blocks.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] This is the closest local analogue: the same subregions, 1,000–1,350 m against the box's 1,014–1,372 m, and the same three forest types.
- [I] Pine- and spruce-regenerating cutblocks should score about 0.1–0.2 for both species until ~15 yr. Spruce grouse arrive at ~17–27 yr and are "abundant" by ~30 yr. That fits the Boag & Schroeder lodgepole curve, but with the onset closer to 15 yr than 10 yr.
- [I] Ruffed grouse use mixedwood and spruce blocks with deciduous regrowth from ~16 yr, and pine blocks almost never. Regen value should depend on what the block regrows as (aspen/mixed vs pine), not only on years since cutting.
- [I] Dusky (blue) grouse were summer-only and only beside the mountain front, absent from blocks more than 15 km out. This supports a dusky score of 0 for the Clearwater box in October.
- [I] Ruffed grouse in the pine-block / deciduous / road-edge transition supports the hardwood–conifer edge and road-edge bonuses.

## Not confirmed
- Tables 3 and Appendix 10 are OCR-garbled, so per-block counts were not extracted. Samples are small (tens of birds).
- Methods mix sightings and pellet groups, and observer effort per year is not normalized in the text read.
- The distance from the Clearwater box to the mountain front was not measured.
