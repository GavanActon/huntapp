# Roads and grizzly bear survival in the Alberta foothills; the recovery plan's road thresholds (2014, 2020)
- **Source:**
  - Boulanger, J. & Stenhouse, G.B. "The impact of roads on the demography of grizzly bears in Alberta." *PLoS ONE* 9(12): e115535 (2014), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0115535: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4274100/
  - Government of Alberta, *Alberta Grizzly Bear Recovery Plan* (Alberta Species at Risk Recovery Plan No. 39), 2020: https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/5bc2f11a-7360-48fe-a768-73127d24f039/resource/0f4abf49-9bdd-42a8-892f-a74f1a143770/download/aep-alberta-grizzly-bear-recovery-2020.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Boulanger & Stenhouse: full text via a reading tool; Fig. 2 was not read. Recovery plan: full PDF text, sections 4.0 and 6.2.2 and Table 4.
- **Evidence:** [S] for the paper. [G] for the plan.
- **Where and when:**
  - Paper: the Yellowhead and Grande Cache units, south and north of Hinton, in the west-central Alberta foothills. 1999–2012.
  - Known-fate sample: 51 adult females, 40 adult males, 24 subadult females, 27 subadult males. The reproductive analysis used 54 adult females. Cub survival came from 24 females with 53 cubs.
  - Plan: the provincial Recovery Zone.

## Extracted facts
- **How road density was measured (paper):** "the kilometers of all weather (gravel or paved) road encountered within a 300 m radius of each GPS location".
  - Traffic volume was not a covariate.
  - Most roads were gravel secondary roads (96.5%).
- **Thresholds (paper):**
  - "If females with cubs/yearlings had reduced survival then λ was reduced to below 1 at road densities of 0.75".
  - With adult female survival pooled across reproductive states, the threshold is 1.25 km/km² (female survival 0.95 adult, 0.83 subadult).
  - "We suggest that the 0.75 road density threshold is most applicable to ensure viable grizzly bear populations."
- **Plan, p. 49:** the 2008 plan defined open routes as "roads or trails that receive motorized use (including seismic lines)". It also noted regulatory limits "since it is not illegal to drive off road on vacant public land in Alberta unless there is a Public Land Use Zone that specifically restricts off-road use to designated trails."
- **Plan, Table 4:** only 35% of the Recovery Zone has a designation that can regulate open routes. BMA 4 is 14,907 km²: 38% protected area, 29% PLUZ, 67% in total.
- **Plan, p. 50:**
  - Open road density is "defined as open all-weather gravel or paved roads".
  - "Of 22 deaths, 19 occurred less than 500 m from an open road, and none of these mortalities were adjacent to an open route that was not also an open road" (Stenhouse, personal communication).
- **Plan recommendation:** open road density "in the Core Access Management Areas remain at less than or equal to 0.6 km/km2 but be reduced in the Secondary Access Management Areas to less than or equal to 0.75 km/km2".
  - Density is computed per Grizzly Bear Watershed Unit, about 500 km².
  - Currently 26.7% (Core) and 32.9% (Secondary) of these units are above threshold.
  - The plan notes "lower road density in the western portions … and higher to the east, associated with natural resource extraction-related road construction."
- **2008 plan thresholds:** ≤0.6 km/km² roads in Grizzly Bear Priority Areas, and ≤1.2 km/km² open routes elsewhere.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **The box is well above the plan's road thresholds.** Its all-weather (gravel + paved) road density, measured from the Access layer, is 0.82 km/km², above both 0.6 (Core) and 0.75 (Secondary). The plan's figure is computed per ~500 km² watershed unit, so this is only indicative. Grizzly survival risk from roads is real here, and Alberta may close or decommission roads in future.
- [I] **Mortality concentrates within 500 m of open roads** (19 of 22 deaths), not along trails or seismic lines. That supports keeping the pressure penalty on open gravel and paved roads, not on cutlines.
- [I] **No legal limit on driving cutlines here.** The box has no PLUZ, so "it is not illegal to drive off road on vacant public land". Hunters on OHVs can use cutlines and pipelines across the Green Area, apart from the bed-and-shore rule; see access-alberta-public-land-rules.md. Motorized hunter pressure is not confined to the road network.
- [I] **A cheap road-density input.** All-weather road length within 300 m (the paper's radius) or within 1 km is easy to bake and matches the published methods.
