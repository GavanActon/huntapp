July 2020, 84 pp. ISBN 978-1-4601-4791-7. Landing page: https://open.alberta.ca/publications/9781460147917. - **Second source:** Boulanger, J. & G.B. Stenhouse. 2014. The impact of roads on the demography of grizzly bears in Alberta. PLoS ONE 9(12):e115535; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4274100/
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Plan: full text. Boulanger & Stenhouse: full text, except the last ~1,200 characters.
- **Evidence:** [G] for the plan; [S] for Boulanger & Stenhouse
- **Where and when:** Alberta grizzly Bear Management Areas (BMAs) 1–7. The plan uses mortality data from 2000–2015. Boulanger & Stenhouse used 1999–2012 data on 53 bears each in Yellowhead and Grande Cache, plus 36 elsewhere.

## Extracted facts
- **Status:** "Grizzly bears (Ursus arctos) were listed as a Threatened species by the province of Alberta in 2010." "sport hunting of grizzly bears was discontinued in 2006". "licenced hunting of grizzly bears ceased in 2006 following the completion of the first two BMA population inventories". Permits were cut in 2003–2004. The Endangered Species Conservation Committee had first recommended a Threatened listing in 2002.
- **Zones:** each BMA has a Recovery, Support and Habitat Linkage Zone. The non-protected part of the Recovery Zone is split into Core and Secondary Access Management Areas.
- **Road thresholds:** "Clear open road density threshold recommendations … Core and Secondary Access Management Areas with 0.6 km/km2 and 0.75 km/km2 road densities, respectively." Grizzly Bear Watershed Units are about 500 km². Of these, 26.7% (Core) and 32.9% (Secondary) were above threshold.
- **BMA 4 Clearwater, Table 1:** 2005 estimate 47 (44–60), trend unknown. Banff National Park and Siffleur Wilderness Area were not included (source: Boulanger et al. 2005).
- **BMA 4, Table 2 (2010–2015 averages, population 47):**
  - Human-caused mortality: 2.50/yr (5.32% of the population). Females: 0.67/yr (1.42%).
  - Total removals, including translocations: 2.83/yr (6.03%).
  - These are above the 2008 plan's thresholds of 4% total and 1.2% female. BMAs 4, 5 and 6 are prioritized for mortality reduction.
- **Mortality causes, 2010–2015 (outside parks):**
  - In order: poaching; vehicle and train collisions; self-defence kills ("usually by hunters"); black bear hunters misidentifying grizzlies.
  - "Since most defensive kills are by hunters, getting hunters to carry and use bear spray is an important strategy." Bear spray deterred attacks 92% of the time (Smith et al. 2008, Alaska).
  - The plan proposes mandatory bear-identification certification for black bear hunters. It cites the "continued practice of restricting black bear baiting to areas that are not occupied grizzly bear habitat".
  - Females were 36.2% of known human-caused deaths.
- **Disturbance:**
  - Grizzlies in west-central Alberta select cutblocks, well sites and roads.
  - "Laberee et al. (2014) reported that the majority of grizzly bears were closer than expected to roads … in the fall."
  - Northrup et al. 2012 (south-west Alberta): bears avoided roads with 20–100 vehicles/day and strongly avoided roads with more than 100/day.
  - The plan warns that food-rich disturbed areas near open roads can become "attractive sinks".
- **Conflict:** very high in BMAs 5–6 and "to a lesser extent, in BMAs 2 and 4". Most conflicts are outside the Recovery Zone. Translocations out of BMA 4 in 2010–2015: 3 from the Recovery Zone and 6 from the Support Zone.
- **Boulanger & Stenhouse 2014:**
  - "Roads" = km of all-weather road within 300 m of each GPS fix.
  - Survival: subadult males 0.78, adult males 0.88, subadult females 0.88, adult females 0.96.
  - Population growth falls below replacement (λ < 1) at about 0.75 km/km² when females with cubs or yearlings survive at 0.90, versus about 1.25 km/km² with pooled female survival.
  - "19 of 22 deaths occurred within 500 m of a road." Of the 22: 7 poaching, 3 legal hunting, 4 management, 2 road kill, 1 natural, 5 unknown. Cub survival 0.55.
  - In 2014 the thresholds in use were 0.6 (Core) and 1.2 (Secondary). The 2020 plan cut Secondary to 0.75.
- **Federal status (separate source):** SARA Schedule 1, Part 4 (Special Concern) lists "Bear, Grizzly (Ursus arctos) Western population". Added by SOR/2018-112, registered 30 May 2018; https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2018/2018-06-13/html/sor-dors112-eng.html

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Road density.** The box is 77% Secondary zone, so it sits in a Secondary Access Management Area of the Recovery Zone (target ≤0.75 km/km² of open road). The box has about 1.0 km/km² of roads of all kinds, which exceeds the target if most are open. Expect grizzlies to use roadsides mainly at night, and grizzly deaths to cluster near roads.
- [I] **Bear ID.** Misidentification by black bear hunters is a top-four cause of grizzly deaths. The black bear verdict for WMUs 324 and 326 should carry an ID line: hump, short round ears, long pale claws; colour does not tell.
- [I] **Bear spray first** is official Alberta advice for hunters and belongs on the kill-site card.
