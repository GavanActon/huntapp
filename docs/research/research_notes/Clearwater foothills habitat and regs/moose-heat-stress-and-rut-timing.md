# Moose heat thresholds (central Alberta), Clearwater fall temperatures and rut timing (1986)
- **Source:** Renecker, L.A., Hudson, R.J. 1986. Seasonal energy expenditures and thermoregulatory responses of moose. *Canadian Journal of Zoology* 64:322–327. https://doi.org/10.1139/z86-052. Abstract read via https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1139/z86-052
- **Read:** 2026-10-17.
  - Abstracts only for Renecker & Hudson 1986, 1988 and 1989.
  - Full text for Jensen et al. 2018.
  - Daily ECCC climate data, summarized by me.
- **Evidence:** [S] study and supporting papers; [G] agency data; [H] guide statement.
- **Where and when:**
  - Renecker & Hudson worked with two hand-reared moose cows in a 65-ha enclosure in the aspen-dominated boreal forest of central Alberta, in the 1980s.
  - Climate: Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) daily station data from two stations:
    - Prairie Creek RS: 52.25° N, 115.30° W, 1,173.5 m, inside the Clearwater box; 1967–1984.
    - Rocky Mtn House (AUT): 988 m; 2001–2025.

## Extracted facts
- **Renecker & Hudson 1986 thresholds.** "During winter, moose increased respiration rates when ambient temperatures rose above −5 °C. Resistance to heat load was greater for standing moose during summer; respiration rate increased above 14 °C and open-mouthed panting began at 20 °C." Piloerection appeared at −25 to −30 °C without any rise in metabolism.
- **Same research program, other papers.**
  - Renecker & Hudson 1989, *Can. J. Zool.* 67:1923–1928 (https://doi.org/10.1139/z89-275): two cows, "aspen-dominated boreal forests, central Alberta".
  - Renecker & Hudson 1988, *Ecography* 11:111–118 (https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0587.1988.tb00788.x): forage digestibility reached 70% in spring and summer and fell to 30% at dormancy; protein >20% vs <7%.
  - Renecker & Hudson 1986, *J. Wildl. Manage.* 50:143–147 (https://doi.org/10.2307/3801504): foraging-rate slopes were lowest "in October when a mixture of green and cured forage offered the greatest opportunities for selective feeding". Maximum intake fell from 23 g/min in July to 11 g/min in January.
- **Critiques** (secondary, via Lamy & Finnegan 2019, https://friresearch.ca/data/CP_2021_07_Moose_Lit_Review.pdf):
  - Ontario moose showed no signs of heat stress within the expected range (Lowe et al. 2010).
  - Nova Scotia moose sought thermal cover at about 24 °C (Broders et al. 2012).
  - Wind raises the onset of heat stress by 4–10 °C (McCann et al. 2013).
  - Areas above 27 °C without shade are unsuitable (Kelsall & Telfer 1974).
  - Seen only in search snippets: McCann et al. 2013 put the summer threshold at 17 °C without wind, and the 14 °C figure rests on two cows.
- **Clearwater fall temperatures.** Share of days whose maximum exceeded each threshold, computed from ECCC daily Tmax (https://api.weather.gc.ca/collections/climate-daily). Each half-month row covers ~210–250 days at Prairie Creek and ~365–395 at Rocky Mtn House.

- **Rut timing.**
  - I found no Alberta study of conception dates.
  - North Dakota: Jensen et al. 2018, *Alces* 54:1–10 (full text, https://gf.nd.gov/sites/default/files/publications/reproductive-parameters-of-nd-moose.pdf). From hunter-killed cows (1978–86): "The mean conception date of 45 cows was 2 October ± 8.0 d (SD; range = 3 September to 21 October)."
  - BC: the Provincial Moose Research Project uses an average birth date of 21 May. Fewer than 10% of calvings were second-estrus births, which come about 20 days after first-estrus births (Anderson et al. 2023, https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/wildlife-wildlife-habitat/moose/ten_year_provincial_moose_project_report_2023.pdf).
  - [H] Kevin Wilson, a guide based in Edmonton, writes that bulls "respond eagerly from Sept. 27 to Oct. 4", that calling "abruptly subsides after about Oct. 11", and that hunters should look for eager bulls from Sept. 23 through at least Oct. 8. https://guidefitter.com/blog/a-guides-guide-to-calling-moose
  - Seasons relative to the rut:
    - In WMU 326 the rifle season opens 24 Sept, inside the rut (2020 report).
    - In WMU 324, archery runs 1 Sept–31 Oct and rifle 1–30 Nov (2018 report).

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Temperature factor: keep.** The break points (≤10 °C 1.0, 10–14 0.8, 14–20 0.5, >20 0.3) come from this Alberta work.
- [I] **Warm-afternoon rule: smooth the schedule.** At the current >14 °C threshold, the rule fires on ~60–76% of early-September afternoons and ~40% of early-October ones. The 1 Oct switch to 10 °C fires on ~60% of early-October days. Since moose in winter coat pant above about −5 °C, use:

  | Dates | Threshold |
  |---|---|
  | to 15 Sept | 14 °C |
  | 16–30 Sept | 12 °C |
  | October | 10 °C |
  | from 1 Nov | 5 °C |

- [I] **Use the ground-wind model.** When modelled head-height wind is at least ~2–3 m/s, raise all thresholds by +4 °C, the low end of McCann's 4–10 °C. The wind-speed cut-off is not confirmed.
- [I] **Rut curve: keep.** It matches a mean conception date of ~2 Oct (North Dakota; the 21 May birth date minus a ~231-day gestation gives the same, though the gestation figure is unverified) and the guide's 27 Sept–4 Oct peak. Optionally lower 9–15 Oct from 0.7 to 0.6, after the guide's "abruptly subsides after about Oct. 11"; low confidence.
- [I] **Early-to-mid October.** Moose feed most selectively then, choosing among green and cured forage. That supports giving still-green wet shrub and riparian sites extra weight in early to mid October.

## Not confirmed
- Where the Renecker & Hudson enclosure was. I assume a University of Alberta field station near Edmonton; the abstracts do not say.
- No thermal threshold data for the fall coat change (Sept–Oct).
- The 231-day gestation figure appeared only in a search summary. I did not read the Schwartz reviews (scanned PDF).
- The Prairie Creek data are from 1967–84, a cooler period. Recent warming at that site is not measured; the Rocky Mtn House rows are ~2 °C warmer in September.
