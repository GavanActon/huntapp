# Forest grouse monitoring in Alberta, ACA final report (2026)
- **Source:** Jokinen, M. 2026. Investigating methods for monitoring forest grouse trends in Alberta. ACA Project Report: Final. Alberta Conservation Association, Sherwood Park, Alberta (April 2026); https://www.ab-conservation.com/downloads/report_series/aca_project_report_2025_forest_grouse_monitoring_initiative_final.pdf (summary page: https://www.ab-conservation.com/publications/report-series/investigating-methods-for-monitoring-forest-grouse-trends-in-alberta-final-report)
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text). The 2024/25 and 2021/22 project summaries were also read; they repeat these findings.
- **Evidence:** [S] agency science: a voluntary harvest survey summary and a pellet-survey trial. The trapper survey inside it is opinion, so treat those parts as [H].
- **Where and when:**
  - Alberta-wide voluntary game bird harvest survey, 2015–2021.
  - 131 trapline (RFMA) holders, from their 2020/21 logbooks.
  - 13 pellet transects in April–May 2021: Rocky Mountains NR (5), Foothills NR (3), Boreal NR (5).

## Extracted facts
- "the game bird season runs from September 1 to January 15 in most Wildlife Management Units (WMUs). A regulation change was implemented in 2015 extending the end date ... from November 30 to January 15." The licence allows 5 a day and 15 in possession.
- ABMI cameras: of 1,161 sites, "only eight sites detected spruce grouse, whereas ruffed grouse were detected at 297 sites."
- "In 2020, about 6,900 spruce grouse harvests were voluntarily reported by 3,300 hunters. In comparison, approximately 61,000 ruffed grouse were harvested by 18,700 hunters in 2020."
- Reported spruce grouse harvest averaged 6,648 a year (range 2,998–8,856) over 2015–2021, taken by an average of 2,863 hunters. Effort was about 19,826 hunter-days a year, roughly 7 days per hunter. "The average effort to harvest a spruce grouse is near three days." It is "possible (perhaps probable) that most spruce grouse are harvested opportunistically while hunting big game or targeting ruffed grouse."
- Trappers' opinion: spruce grouse "were most often associated with black spruce (30%), mixedwood (28%), and white spruce (19%) stands in the 20- to 50-year class."
- Foothills RFMAs, 2018–2020 trend for both forest grouse: 15 decreasing, 5 increasing, 14 stable. Declining lines near Fox Creek, Edson, Whitecourt and Swan Hills blamed "forest harvest (and herbicide spraying associated with forest harvest) and hunter harvest". Of trappers who thought grouse cycle, "half believed the cycle occurred about every four to seven years."
- Pellet trial results:
  - Southern (Rocky Mountains NR): ruffed grouse pellets on 4 of 5 transects, no spruce grouse. "The Franklin's spruce grouse (Canachites canadensis franklinii) subspecies inhabits higher elevation forests in the southern region."
  - Central (Foothills NR, n = 3): no pellets, but one spruce grouse was flushed.
  - NW boreal: spruce grouse pellets on 3 of 5 transects, all in mature spruce.
- Productivity: Smyth & Boag (1984) found poor spruce grouse production in years with wet, cool June weather. Nest losses to predation were high (Redmond et al. 1982).
- Misidentification: spruce grouse harvests reported from prairie WMUs are "almost certainly due to entry errors or species misidentification (e.g., mistaking sharp-tailed grouse or ruffed grouse for spruce grouse)."
- ACA's 2024/25 fall forecast covers pheasant and partridge only: "More data is required for forest grouse species to understand population dynamics."

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Ruffed grouse are the main bird: about 9:1 over spruce grouse in reported 2020 harvest and 37:1 in ABMI camera detections. The Clearwater grouse heat should be ruffed-led, with spruce grouse as an overlay on pine and spruce.
- [I] The trappers' 20–50 yr class for spruce grouse matches the Boag & Schroeder 10–40 yr lodgepole window. Black spruce and white spruce stands also count, not only pine.
- [I] October is early-to-mid season (it runs to 15 January). Spruce grouse are mostly incidental to big game hunts, so near-road spruce grouse may be less depleted than ruffed grouse.
- [I] Sharp-tailed grouse are closed in WMUs 324 and 326, and ACA documents hunters confusing species. An ID warning is worth adding.

## Not confirmed
- The harvest data are voluntary and province-wide. The WMU 324/326 values (Figure 2 map) could not be read numerically.
- No ruffed grouse trend or cycle data are given.
