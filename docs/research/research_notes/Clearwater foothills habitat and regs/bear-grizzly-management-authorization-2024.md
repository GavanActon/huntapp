## Extracted facts
- **When an authorization can be issued.** Under s. 53.1(2), the Minister may issue a grizzly bear management authorization "for the purpose of hunting a grizzly bear if (a) the grizzly bear is not accompanied by a cub, and (b) a wildlife officer has determined that the grizzly bear (i) is involved in a human-bear conflict situation, or (ii) is in an area of concern."
- **What counts as a conflict.** The bear is habituated or food-conditioned and an imminent public-safety risk, or it has killed livestock, damaged private property, or made contact with a person causing injury or death. "Livestock" includes bees and captive elk, deer and bison.
- **Who can hold one.** An adult resident who is eligible for a recreational licence. The Minister may set up a pool of eligible people. A person drawn from the pool must agree to be onsite within 24 h.
- **What the authorization sets.** The area, the time period, and the methods and equipment allowed.
- **The legal exception.** A new Schedule 6 footnote reads: "Despite its status as a threatened animal, the hunting of Ursus arctos (Brown (Grizzly) Bear) is permitted under section 53.1."
- **Guide, use by zone:**
  - The process follows the Grizzly Bear Response Guide (2023). "During the period 2020-2024, an average of 10 management grizzly bears were lethally removed each year."
  - "RECOVERY ZONE: … the use of the management authorization will rarely, if ever, be implemented in this zone."
  - SUPPORT ZONE: an authorization "can be implemented in this zone for a bear without cubs".
  - Outside the zones is "typically where management authorizations will be issued to a member of the public".
  - An "area of concern" is where conflicts are handled regularly and end in lethal removal. Some caribou ranges can also count.
- **alberta.ca page:**
  - Responders "work alongside wildlife enforcement officers". They act "only when deemed appropriate and safe by wildlife enforcement officers".
  - Responders are chosen by randomized lottery onto a priority list and must respond within 24 h for grizzlies.
  - They keep all parts of the bear except the gall bladder and must deliver it to the government for sampling.
  - The authorization gives no automatic access to private land.
- **2026 draw booklet:**
  - Application code GBR, residents 18+, $50. "NOTE: This is not a recreational hunting opportunity."

## Status line (2026)
No open or recreational grizzly season exists in Alberta. Licensed hunting ceased in 2006, and the grizzly has been listed as Threatened under Alberta's Wildlife Act since 2010.

Since 17 June 2024, Ministerial Order 43/2024 (Wildlife Regulation s. 53.1) allows only one kind of grizzly kill by the public. Fish and Wildlife may direct that a specific conflict grizzly, not with a cub, be killed by a resident "responder" drawn from a lottery pool, within the area and period set by the authorization.

WMUs 324 and 326 are in the Central responder region, so this can apply there in principle. But the guide says it will be used "rarely, if ever" in the Recovery Zone, which covers the box's 77% Secondary part. It can be used in the Support Zone (23% of the box) for a bear without cubs. For an ordinary hunter in the box, a grizzly is never legal game.

Federally, the Western population has been listed as Special Concern under the Species at Risk Act (Schedule 1, Part 4) since SOR/2018-112, registered 30 May 2018.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Legal line on the card.** The app should state: no grizzly season; grizzlies are protected here. The responder program is not a hunt, and a hunter cannot join it in the field.
- [I] **Where responder kills happen.** The four kills reported with a location followed livestock conflicts in the south-west (Pincher Creek, Twin Butte) and near Grande Prairie. None was reported from BMA 4. The 2026 total of up to 13 was reported without locations.
