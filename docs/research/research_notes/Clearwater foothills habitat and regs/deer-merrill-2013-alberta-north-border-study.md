# Alberta North Border Deer Study: conception dates, activity, field use (2013)
- **Source:** Merrill, E., Pybus, M., Nobert, B., Habib, T., Potapov, A., Brownrigg, E., Jones, P., Garrett, C., Bertrand, A., Wachowski, J., Stevens, J. & Flaig, C. (2013). *Alberta Chronic Wasting Disease: North Border Deer Study.* Final report, 16 April 2013. Department of Biological Sciences, University of Alberta, Edmonton, 90 pp. https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/4fc0a791-6ade-441c-a2d9-e750ff55da5e/resource/ab3a3905-f238-465e-8645-0dc038add2fa/download/2013-cwdnorthborderdeerstudyreport-apr2013.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text: executive summary and sections 5, 7 and 8; Figs 5.4, 7.3 and 8.3 viewed as images)
- **Evidence:** [S] agency–university science report (Alberta Fish and Wildlife with the University of Alberta; not peer-reviewed as a whole)
- **Where and when:**
  - East-central Alberta aspen parkland along the Saskatchewan border, in the CWD zone. Agriculture dominant, with cattle grazing and petroleum development.
  - Jan 2006 – Apr 2010. 272 deer collared (130 mule deer, 142 white-tailed deer; 99 GPS, 173 VHF).
  - Fetuses from the 2005 herd reduction in WMU 234: 227 white-tailed deer, 23 mule deer.

## Extracted facts
- Winter snow depth averaged 23.1 cm (2007–08) and 19.0 cm (2008–09).
- Aerial density, Jan 2007–09: mule deer 0.79 ± 0.08/km²; white-tailed deer 1.07 ± 0.10/km².
- **Conception (Fig. 8.3, crown–rump lengths, 5-day bins):**
  - Text: "Eighty percent of the white-tailed deer had conceived by 30 November." Collections were in late winter, so the early-sampling bias (Rhodes et al. 1991) "should not exist".
  - White-tailed deer bars, read from the figure (approximate):

    | Bin centred on | Share of conceptions |
    |---|---|
    | 3 Nov | ~1% |
    | 8 Nov | ~1.5% |
    | 13 Nov | ~8% |
    | 18 Nov | ~26% |
    | 23 Nov | ~26% |
    | 28 Nov | ~18% |
    | 3 Dec | ~9% |
    | 8 Dec | ~5% |
    | 13–28 Dec | ≤2% per bin |

  - Mule deer (n = 23, "too low" to assess): about 73% in the bin centred on 8 Dec, about 9% and 14% in the 13 and 18 Nov bins.
- **Habitat use:**
  - "mule deer used more grassland and rugged terrain while white-tailed deer used more woody cover. In winter, male white-tailed deer used agriculture more than male mule deer but this was reversed in the summer."
  - Fig. 5.4 (24-h GPS locations, 100 m buffer; bars read approximately):
    - white-tailed deer females: about 30% agriculture in summer, 21% in winter;
    - white-tailed deer males: about 14% in summer, 28% in winter;
    - woody cover within 100 m: about 55% (females) to 66% (males, summer).
- Diet: summer forbs, chiefly alfalfa (*Medicago*), plus aspen and wolf willow. Winter: graminoids, willow and juniper.
- **Seasonal movement:**
  - 25% of deer collared for a full year migrated, moving to winter range in Nov–Jan.
  - White-tailed deer migrated farther: 68% went more than 10 km, while 75% of mule deer went less than 10 km.
  - Winter home ranges were 5–6 km² and summer ranges 10–12 km².
- **Activity (§7.3, Fig. 7.3; hourly GPS):**
  - "Females of both species had consistent bimodal, crepuscular activity periods throughout the year". Males were more variable.
  - "only male white-tailed deer were more active than females particularly during the fall".
  - Fig. 7.3h, white-tailed deer in fall (Oct–Dec), read approximately; the figure does not state the time zone:

    | Clock hours | Males (m/h) | Females (m/h) |
    |---|---|---|
    | 05–07 h | 430–440 | 210–260 |
    | 09–15 h | ~100 | 70–100 |
    | 17–19 h | 470–480 | up to ~340 |
    | 00–04 h | 300–350 | — |

- **Mortality and harvest:**
  - Among the 272 collared deer, hunting caused 44.3% of deaths, coyotes 8.0%, vehicles 4.5%.
  - "Number of deer harvested ... was nonlinearly related to deer density and to the amount of cover and rugged terrain, but not road density." Road density in the area ranged 0–2.5 km/km².
- **CWD:**
  - Hunter-harvest prevalence, 2005–2011: male mule deer 0.79%, female mule deer 0.29%, male white-tailed deer 0.14%, female white-tailed deer 0.01%.
  - Risk was highest in the Battle River and Ribstone Creek drainages compared with the "adjacent uplands devoid of woody cover".

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] WMU 234 is in "central east Alberta"; the study region includes the Battle River and Ribstone Creek drainages. I take it to be roughly the latitude of Clearwater (52.28° N), within about a degree, but I did not verify its exact latitude. This is the best Alberta rut calendar found:
  - about 70% of white-tailed deer conceptions fell from about 16 to 30 Nov;
  - the median was about 22 Nov and the mean about 24 Nov (computed from the figure readings above).
- [I] Proposed white-tailed deer rut factor for Clearwater:

  | Dates | f |
  |---|---|
  | to 20 Oct | 0.2 |
  | 21–31 Oct | 0.4 |
  | 1–7 Nov | 0.7 |
  | 8–28 Nov | 1.0 |
  | 29 Nov – 7 Dec | 0.7 |
  | 8–20 Dec | 0.4 |
  | after 20 Dec | 0.2 |

  The WMU 324 rifle season (1–30 Nov) spans the whole rut; archery (to 31 Oct) ends before it.
- [I] Mule deer breed later (mostly early December in this small sample), largely after the November seasons.
- [I] Fields: white-tailed deer locations sat where about a fifth to a third of the 100 m surroundings was cropland and about 55–65% was woody. That is the bush–field edge, not open field. Score fields by distance to woody cover, and woody cells by distance to a field.
- [I] Hour weights: fall white-tailed deer are crepuscular. Midday movement is about 0.2–0.3 of the dawn and dusk peaks, which fits the current midday 0.3. Bucks in the fall also move heavily at night.
- [I] Harvest followed cover and deer density, not road density, in parkland with ≤2.5 km/km² of roads. In parkland the road penalty should be mild; Stelfox 1988 shows a stronger forest-road effect.
- [I] Transfer limits: this is parkland with ~1 deer/km² and about 20 cm of winter snow. The Clearwater White Area (8%, east edge) is the closest analogue, not the forested Green Area.

## Not confirmed
- All bar heights are read from figures, not from tables.
- Conception dates come from one year (2005) and one WMU.
- Fig. 7.3 does not state its time zone.
- The exact latitude of WMU 234 and of the study blocks was not checked.
- Habitat use is by location (use), not by selection against availability.
