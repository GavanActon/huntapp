- **Where and when:**
  - Sundre Equine Management Zone (EMZ), ~2,205 km², ~100 km NW of Calgary: "bordered by the Clearwater River to the north, the Red Deer River to the south, and Banff National Park to the west".
  - 120 trail cameras on a 4 km grid, 2017/18–2020 (~79% working); five GPS-collared mares, 2018–2020.

## Extracted facts
- **Sundre EMZ landscape:**
  - ~21% has been logged at some time; combined human footprint ~25% (ABMI).
  - Two FMAs cover ~51% of the zone.
  - Cover: conifer ~60%, mixedwood ~10%, native rangeland 7%, cutblocks 7%.
  - "Within the EMZ, elk and cattle are priority species for management."
- **Summer naive occupancy:** horses 0.80 (0.65–0.89), cattle 0.46 (0.36–0.60), elk **0.19** (0.088–0.36). Detection probability 0.44, 0.43 and 0.14.
  - "spatial coverage of elk was low compared to cattle and horses."
- **Elk model:** the top-ranked model was proportion of native rangeland. Model-averaged, "elk occupancy was lower at sites farther from conifer forest patches (−0.858)". Rangeland had a positive effect for elk and horses.
- **Horses:**
  - In summer horses "favored forestry cutblocks over native rangelands". Proportion of cutblocks was the largest effect for horses (0.471); it was negative for cattle (−0.768).
  - Horse–cattle diet overlap was 64%, but contemporaneous habitat overlap only 2%.
  - "Forage quality for herbivores in cutblocks is generally lower than in native rangelands, with reduced crude protein and higher fiber content, particularly in older regenerating stands".
- **OCS 2023:**
  - Horses, moose, deer and elk share the western foothills, with spatial overlap "particularly with elk and cattle" (Salter & Hudson 1980).
  - A "negative association between feral horse and native ungulate abundance" (Girard et al. 2013a), "mechanism … unclear".
  - "at high herbivore densities the feral horse should outcompete a ruminant".
  - Horse biomass in parts of the foothills could support "10 wolves/1000 km²". Horses "can comprise a significant portion of the diet of cougar in west-central Alberta" (Knopff et al. 2010). The effect of a horse crash on elk via shared predators is "unknown".
- **Counts** (alberta.ca): six EMZs (Brazeau, Nordegg, Clearwater, Sundre, Ghost River, Elbow) between the Sheep and Brazeau rivers.
  - Minimum counts: 1,428 (2023), 1,478 (2024), 1,485 (2025), 2,072 (Jan–Mar 2026, all six zones).
  - Horses "prefer grassy meadows, shrublands, and cutblocks about 7 years old or younger."
  - The 24 Feb 2026 data summary logs observations in the "Clearwater" EMZ. A search summary put the 2023 count at 969 in Sundre and 97 in Clearwater (not verified).
- WMU 326 (Jan 2020): 16 feral horses seen; no elk.
- Minimum-count point layers (OGL-Alberta, WGS84), counted against the box by me on 2026-10-17:
  - 2026: the survey's flight tracks crossed the box (199 track vertices inside) but logged **0 horses in the box**; 0 within 10 km of the centre, 74 within 25 km, 477 within 50 km (province total 2,072).
  - 2025: 0 in the box, 0 within 10 km, 16 within 25 km, 267 within 50 km (total 1,485).
  - Layers: https://extranet.gov.ab.ca/srd/geodiscover/srd_pub/biota/Rangelands/FeralHorse2026MinimumCountSHP.zip and …/FeralHorse2025MinimumCountSHP.zip (listed in https://open.alberta.ca/opendata/gda-9582c7ad-027d-4719-9e90-94e77a7224f2).
- **TWS 2025** [H, snippet]: horse diets "overlapped substantially with elk and bighorn sheep, mainly in winter and spring", and "Elk appeared to avoid areas where horses had been foraging" (Ya Ha Tinda cameras and scats, 2022–2025).

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Even in Sundre, the best-known foothills elk landscape near the box, cameras detected elk at under a fifth of sites in summer, against 80% for horses. Horses dominate the cutblocks and meadows that the plan scores as elk night forage.
- [I] Elk occupancy rose near conifer patches and with *native* rangeland. In Clearwater, "grassland" land cover (6%) is mostly 5–14-yr cutblocks, not native rangeland. The model should not treat it as meadow.
- [I] Horses are not a Clearwater-box factor on current counts: none were seen in the box in late winter 2025 or 2026, although the 2026 flights crossed it, and most lie 25–50 km away. Horse competition belongs in an elk model for Sundre, Bearberry or the Ya Ha Tinda edge. There, an optional factor (e.g. ×0.8 for elk on cuts ≤7 yr and grassy openings inside EMZs with many horses) is defensible but weak evidence.
- [I] Horses also feed cougars and wolves: one more pressure on a sparse elk population.
