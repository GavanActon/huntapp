# How far hunters get from roads: GPS-tracked elk hunters (1998) and deer hunters (2004)
- **Source:**
  - Lyon, L.J. & Burcham, M.G. *Tracking elk hunters with the Global Positioning System.* USDA Forest Service, Rocky Mountain Research Station, Research Paper RMRS-RP-3, February 1998, 6 pp: https://www.fs.usda.gov/rm/pubs/rmrs_rp003.pdf (Treesearch record https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/24603)
  - Stedman, R., Diefenbach, D.R., Swope, C.B., Finley, J.C., Luloff, A.E., Zinn, H.C., San Julian, G.J. & Wang, G.A. (2004) "Integrating wildlife and human-dimensions research methods to study hunters." *Journal of Wildlife Management* 68(4): 762–773, doi:10.2193/0022-541X(2004)068[0762:IWAHRM]2.0.CO;2: https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/integrating-wildlife-and-human-dimensions-research-methods-to-stu
- **Read:** 2026-10-17. Lyon & Burcham: full text (scanned PDF). Stedman et al.: abstract only.
- **Evidence:** [S]
- **Where and when:**
  - Lyon & Burcham: the Blackfoot Management Area, Garnet Range, western Montana. It is a road-closure (walk-in) unit, described as "an area that has no motorized access". 99 hunts by 50 hunters, 1993–1995, GPS fixes every 15 s.
  - Stedman et al.: a Pennsylvania white-tailed deer season on 45,749 ha of unrestricted public land.

## Extracted facts
- **Hunt length and pace (Lyon & Burcham):** hunters averaged 4.7 h per hunt, 40 m/min and 10.7 km. Horseback hunts averaged 16.8 km; hunts on foot 9.5 km.
- **Distance from the start point:** averaged 2.6 km. "Only one-half of all hunters traveled more than 2.5 km from their starting point, and only 12.5 percent traveled more than 4.5 km."
- **Distance from roads:**
  - "the average distance to any road for all hunters while hunting was only 267 m (n = 96)", excluding fixes on or within 50 m of a road.
  - "Only three hunters managed to get farther than 1.0 km from a road, and the average maximum distance from a road for all hunts was only 565 m."
  - Average maximum distance was 591 m on foot vs 420 m with horses, and 702 m for the most experienced hunters vs 444 m for the least (not significant).
- **Time on roads:**
  - "hunters on foot spent 26 percent of their hunting time on roads" (70 of 271 min).
  - Beyond 1,500 m from the start, "one-third to one-half of all hunting takes place on roads if roads are present."
  - Walking speed on roads was 42 m/min vs 36 m/min off roads. The authors conclude that "roads are more important for getting from one place to another than for actual hunting."
  - They hypothesise that "closed roads facilitate access to areas most distant from open roads".
- **Terrain and vegetation:** "Sixty percent of all hunting took place on slopes less steep than the study area average." Hunters favoured more open forest types with mature trees.
- **Stedman et al. (abstract):**
  - "The average maximum distance hunters reached from a road open to public vehicles was 0.84 km (SE = 0.03)". Hunters walked 5.48 km per day.
  - Asked afterwards, hunters said they walked more than 2.5 times farther from the nearest road (mean 2.23 km) than their GPS showed.
  - Density was 7 hunters per 1,000 ha in the morning and 6.3 in the afternoon.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **The hunter gradient is a few hundred metres, not kilometres.**
  - Typical hunting happens within ~270 m of a road. The average farthest point is 0.6–0.8 km, and only ~3% of hunters pass 1 km.
  - In the Clearwater box, 73% of the ground is within 500 m and 96% within 1 km of a road. Nearly all of it is within normal hunter reach.
  - The useful pressure signal is distance from 0 to ~1 km, graded by road class and traffic. The app's 2.5 km and 4 km steps never apply here.
- [I] **Suggested pressure curve for roads open to vehicles** (gravel + paved + dry-weather), whichever factor carries it:
  - 0.8 at ≤150 m; 0.9 at 150–400 m; 0.95 at 400–700 m; 1.0 beyond ~700 m.
  - A ×1.1 "security" bonus beyond 1 km of all-weather roads (10.7% of the box).
- [I] **Cutlines and closed roads are the walking routes** "to areas most distant from open roads". In Clearwater the cutline grid (3.6 km/km²) plays that role, and OHVs are legal on it.
  - Expect foot and quad hunters along cutlines beyond the road band.
  - That argues against treating "far from a road" as "unhunted" if a cutline runs through it.
  - A mild extra ×0.95 within 100 m of a cutline that connects to a road would represent this. That connection test is an inference and not baked.
- [I] **Hunters' own estimates of how far they walk are unreliable**, 2.5× overstated (Stedman). That matters when interpreting user comments or devlog notes about "deep" spots.

## Not confirmed
- Any Alberta hunter GPS or distance-from-road study. None was found before the web-search budget ran out.
- The closest Alberta evidence is behavioural: south-west Alberta hunters use "forestry roads and trails, searching at dawn and dusk", with weekend use about 1.7× weekday use (Ciuti et al. 2012 Proc B).
- Stedman et al.'s GPS sample size. It is not in the abstract.
