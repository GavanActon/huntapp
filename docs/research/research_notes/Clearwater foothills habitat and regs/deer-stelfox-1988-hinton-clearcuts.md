# 32 years of deer in clear-cuts near Hinton (1988)
- **Source:** Stelfox, J. G. (1988). *Forest Succession and Wildlife Abundance Following Clear-cut Logging in West-Central Alberta.* Final report, 15 Dec 1988. Funded by Alberta Recreation, Parks and Wildlife Foundation; Weldwood of Canada; Wildlife Habitat Canada; Alberta Fish and Game Association; Alberta Fish and Wildlife Division. https://archive.org/stream/forestsuccession00stel/forestsuccession00stel_djvu.txt
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full OCR text; key passages checked line by line; some tables garbled)
- **Evidence:** [S] agency-funded long-term field study (report, not peer-reviewed)
- **Where and when:** Foothills near Hinton, about 53° N:
  - spruce and mixedwood blocks at about 1,000 m; lodgepole pine blocks at about 1,350 m;
  - logged 1956–57; wildlife data in years 1–9, 17, 26–27 and 32 after logging;
  - scarified vs. unscarified clear-cuts with 100 × 100 m residual seed blocks;
  - helicopter and ground surveys, track and pellet counts.

## Extracted facts
- "White-tailed deer quickly moved into the clear-cuts whereas they were not observed in mature forests prior to logging." (p. 33)
- **Big game densities in spruce (deer, moose, elk):**
  - mature forest: 1.5/km²;
  - one year after logging (scarified): 0.8/km² in summer, 0/km² in winter;
  - year 5: 3.5 summer, 0 winter. "Virtually all big game use during the first five years was summer use."
- "Most use during the first ten years was summer use by deer and elk with summer use increasing rapidly, especially during the period 5-10 years after logging."
- Year 6: big game use was 7× and 13× greater in mixedwood than in spruce and pine clear-cuts (7.4× and 12.6× at year 7).
- Year 17: big game use was 59× (unscarified) and 9× (scarified) greater in spruce clear-cuts than in mature spruce. The rise in the first 17 years "was almost entirely summer use by white-tailed and mule deer." (p. 45)
- Year 17 spruce, per the OCR appendix: about 10 big game/km² in summer (6 deer, 1 moose, 3 elk) vs. 0.2/km² in winter (p. 133).
- Deer use of clear-cuts "was mostly by whitetails in the spruce and mixedwood clear-cuts and by both whitetails and mule deer in pine clear-cuts" (Table 5 footnote).
- **Edges and residual cover:**
  - "The mature, residual seed blocks (100 x 100 m) provided important security and winter shelter cover for all three species."
  - "In the scarified block, big game use was greatest within 6 m of seed blocks."
  - In pine clear-cuts, big game use was confined to a 25% perimeter strip next to uncut forest (p. 133; from the summary of the OCR appendix).
- **Hunting and access:**
  - Mixedwood, years 25–27: "Within a week of the opening of the September hunting season, the elk had moved north across the Athabasca River and deer had vacated the clear-cuts." (pp. 134–135)
  - Years 26–27: "negligible big game sign within 335 m of gravel pit activities."
  - Year 32: mixedwood clear-cuts with open trails and a gravel pit held "less than six whitetails and no elk or moose". "In spruce clear-cuts along the same valley where vehicular use was denied, 6-12 whitetails, 3-5 mule deer and 5-6 elk" used a similar-sized area.
  - The author attributes deer, elk and moose staying below carrying capacity to "Road access and continuous harassment from human activities, plus inadequate cover".
- **Browse and capacity (pp. 38–45, from summary; OCR tables partly garbled):**
  - Browse under 2.4 m peaked around year 17 (unscarified) and year 26 (scarified).
  - Theoretical year-17 capacity was about 382–536 deer/km², "assuming adequate cover and no significant harassment".
- **Recommendations (pp. 63–64, from summary):** retain conifer blocks at least 100 m wide (preferably 200 m), spaced no more than 200 m apart, until clear-cuts give winter cover. Minimize human activity in clear-cuts.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] Deer use starts in the first summers after a cut. The deer cut curve should not be the moose 0–4 yr = 0.2. Use 0–2 yr ≈ 0.6 and 3–10 yr = 1.0 (see the McKay & Finnegan note).
- [I] Use clings to uncut forest and residual blocks (within metres to about 100 m). This supports a deer edge term with full value close to cover, as in the FMF HSI note (140 m, falling to 0 by 220 m).
- [I] The **road and pressure term is real in the hunting season**:
  - deer left road-accessible clear-cuts within a week of the opening;
  - a vehicle-closed valley held 2× or more the whitetails of an open one.
  - Clearwater has ~1.0 km/km² of roads and 73% of the box within 500 m of a road, so an in-season daylight penalty near open roads and active industrial sites is warranted. Proposed: ≤100 m ×0.5, 100–300 m ×0.8; active gravel pit, plant or drilling pad ≤335 m ×0.5.
- [I] Mule deer here favoured the high pine blocks (about 1,350 m). That matches McKay: higher and steeper ground.

## Not confirmed
- The OCR of Tables 1–4 and Appendix 3 is garbled. Pellet-group densities per treatment (for example, 406 vs. 24 groups/ha, unscarified spruce vs. mature spruce, year 32) came from an extraction pass and were not checked cell by cell.
- The 1956–57 logging and roading regime differs from today's.
- Deer use by distance from edge was not measured as a curve; plots were set at least 30.48 m from edges "to eliminate edge effect".
- Stelfox, Lynch & McGillis (1976, *Forestry Chronicle*) was not found online and not read.
