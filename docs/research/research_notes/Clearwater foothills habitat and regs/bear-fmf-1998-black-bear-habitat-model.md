# Black bear summer–fall habitat model, Foothills Model Forest (1998)
- **Source:** Zapisocki, R., M. Todd, R. Bonar, J. Beck, B. Beck & R. Quinlan. 1998. Black Bear Summer/Fall Habitat: Habitat Suitability Index Model, Version 5 (last modified 19 October 1998). Foothills Model Forest / Weldwood of Canada, Hinton. https://friresearch.ca/data/null/HSP_1998_10_Rpt_BlackBearSummerFallHabitat.pdf
- **Read:** 2026-10-17 (full text)
- **Evidence:** [S]. The model is built from the literature and is used in forest planning. Its own text says it "has not been evaluated against local data" (verification level 1).
- **Where and when:** Foothills Model Forest, west-central Alberta (Hinton). It covers the Lower Foothills, Upper Foothills, Montane and Subalpine subregions. Season: "The critical summer-fall feeding season."

## Extracted facts
- "Although detailed studies on black bear food habits have not been undertaken in the foothills region…". Berries are "the preferred late summer/fall food".
- **Cover:**
  - S1, stand dbh: 0 below 10 cm, rising to 1 at 20 cm or more. Trees of 20 cm dbh or more can support a climbing bear.
  - S2, tree canopy: 0 below 10%, rising linearly to the optimum at 70% or more.
  - Cover index = S1 × S2.
- **Forage:**
  - S3, shrub cover: 0% is unsuitable; the optimum is reached by 10%.
  - S4, distance from cover (a stand with dbh of 10 cm or more and canopy of 10% or more): "Food habitat is only fully suitable when it is within 200 m of cover … Between 200 and 400 m, food habitat becomes less suitable and becomes completely unsuitable after 400 m."
  - Forage index = S3 × S4.
- **Habitat effectiveness:**
  - S5, roads and trails: cover and food are "only fully suitable when … at least 250 m from a road". This includes trails with horse or motorized access.
  - S6, human activity: cover is "less useful if it is < 5 km from regular human activity" (industrial sites, active wells, active logging, settlements, ranches, camps).
- **Context cited:** use falls off as the distance from cover to the open edge grows to 180–200 m (McCollum 1973).
- **Density cited:** boreal foothills 10 bears/100 km² (Gunson 1989). Berland area 5.7/1,000 km² (Nagy et al. 1989), flagged as an underestimate. Berland females did not breed until age 7; mean litter 2.3.

## What it means for Clearwater
- [I] **Cover bonus.** The app's "+0.2 if hiding cover within 300 m" is close to this model. Suggested change: +0.2 with cover within 200 m, falling linearly to 0 at 400 m. Black bear forage more than 400 m from cover would score ×0.5.
- [I] **Road term.** Add a black bear road term for September–November: ×0.7 within 250 m of an open road or OHV trail, ×1.0 beyond. In this box (about 1.0 km/km² of roads), a 500 m-wide band along each road covers at most about 50% of the area, and less where roads run close together. If seismic lines and OHV trails count as trails, the share is higher.
- [I] **Not checked locally.** The model is unvalidated, so treat it as a sensible shape, not field-measured numbers.

## Not confirmed
- The S5 and S6 curve values below their thresholds exist only as figures and are not readable from the text.
- No fall-specific (September–November) parameters are given.
- There is no local test of the model.
