## 1. Copy tells: what phrasings, structures and rhythms mark LLM writing, and which are Claude's?

### Takeaway
The best-documented tells are structural, not lexical: negative parallelism ("It's not X, it's Y"), rule-of-three lists, "-ing" tails that claim significance ("highlighting its importance"), avoiding plain "is/has", em dashes, synonym cycling, and punchy fragments. Vocabulary tells ("delve", "tapestry") come and go by model generation; "delve" faded sharply in 2025. Claude is documented as more concise and less heavily formatted than ChatGPT. By mid-2026 it is the model most associated with em dashes, "It's not X, it's Y", "load-bearing", "honest caveat" and "mannered" metaphor ("a dial worth turning"). Anthropic's own docs name and target that last habit.

### Cited Findings

#### The reference catalogue: Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing"
- [Primary/Community] The guide's "words to watch" list, verbatim: "*Additionally* (especially beginning a sentence), *align with*, *boasts* (meaning "has"), *bolstered*, *crucial*, *deep dive*, *delve*, *emphasizing*, *enduring*, *enhance*, *fostering*, *garner*, *highlight* (as a verb), *interplay*, *intricate/intricacies*, *key* (as an adjective), *landscape* (as an abstract noun), *meticulous/meticulously*, *pivotal*, *robust*, *showcase*, *tapestry* (as an abstract noun), *testament*, *underscore* (as a verb), *valuable*, *vibrant*" — [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] The vocabulary shifts by model "era": "2023 to mid-2024 (GPT-4): Additionally, boasts, bolstered, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring, garner, intricate/intricacies, interplay, key, landscape, meticulous/meticulously, pivotal, underscore, tapestry, testament, valuable, vibrant"; "Mid-2024 to mid-2025 (GPT-4o): align with, bolstered, crucial, emphasizing, enhance, enduring, fostering, highlighting, pivotal, showcasing, underscore, vibrant"; "Mid-2025 and on (GPT-5): emphasizing, enhance, highlighting, showcasing". It also says "delve" "was famously overused by ChatGPT in 2023 and early 2024, but became less frequent later in 2024, then dropped off sharply in 2025" (citing the Washington Post, 13 Nov 2025). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] Rule of three, verbatim: "LLMs overuse the rule of three. This can take different forms, from 'adjective, adjective, adjective' to 'short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase'. … LLMs often use this structure to make superficial analyses appear more comprehensive." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] Negative parallelism: "When LLMs describe a subject, their output may seem as though it is clearing up a common misconception … While it is common among human writers (especially in 'common misconceptions' or 'myths busted' listicles), it is stereotypically an 'AI sign.'" Forms listed: "Not only … but …", "It is not just …, it's …", "It's not …, it's …", "no …, no …, just …". Example given: "not a mirror but a portal: not a representation of self, but a mechanism". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] Superficial analysis: "AI chatbots tend to insert superficial analysis of information, often in relation to its significance, recognition, or impact. This is often done by attaching a present participle ('-ing') phrase at the end of sentences". Words to watch: "highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing …, ensuring …, reflecting/symbolizing …, contributing to …, cultivating/fostering …, encompassing …, enhancing …, valuable insights, align/resonate with". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] Copula avoidance: LLMs replace "is/are" with "serves as/stands as/marks/functions as/represents" and prefer "marketing-related verbs like *features*, *offers*" to "has". One cited study found "an over 10% decrease in the usage of the words *is* and *are* in academic writing in 2023". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), citing [Geng & Trotta, arXiv 2404.08627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08627)
- [Primary/Community] Em dashes, verbatim: "LLM output uses them more often than nonprofessional human-written text of the same genre, and uses them in places where humans are more likely to use commas, parentheses, colons … LLMs especially tend to use em dashes in a formulaic, pat way, often mimicking 'punched up' sales-like writing … AI-generated em dashes are usually surrounded by spaces". And: "**A July 2026 study found that of contemporary models only Claude used em dashes more than professional writers, and ChatGPT used them less.**" (citing The Economist, "How to spot AI writing", 30 July 2026). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); underlying article [The Economist](https://www.economist.com/culture/2026/07/30/how-to-spot-ai-writing) (paywalled; not read directly)
- [Primary/Community] Other style tells listed: overuse of title case, boldface, "inline-header vertical lists", emoji as formatting, unusual tables, synonym cycling ("elegant variation"), "Challenges and Future Prospects" closers that begin "Despite its … faces several challenges…", vague attributions ("Experts argue", "Observers have cited"), and promotional words ("boasts a, vibrant, rich, profound, enhancing, showcasing, exemplifies, commitment to, natural beauty, nestled, groundbreaking, renowned"). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- [Primary/Community] Signs of *human* writing, observed "over 25 years of Wikipedia writing": "Simple is/has phrases", plain verbs ("wrote" not "authored", "used" not "utilized", "died" not "passed away"), "Superlative or definitive statements, such as *one of the best*, *is the only*, *was the first*", qualifiers like "very, perhaps, tends to". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

#### Academic studies of lexical fingerprints
- [Study] Kobak et al. analysed 15M+ PubMed abstracts (2010–2024). They estimate "at least 13.5% of 2024 abstracts were processed with LLMs", reaching 40% in some subcorpora. Excess words included "delves", "showcasing", "underscores", "potential", "findings", "critical". Pandemic-era excess words were content nouns, but in 2024 they were style words: 66% verbs and 14% adjectives. Published in Science Advances in 2025. — [arXiv 2406.07016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07016v5)
- [Study] Juzek & Ward, "Why Does ChatGPT 'Delve' So Much?" (COLING 2025), found 21 focal words whose rise in scientific abstracts is likely due to LLMs. Their model comparison was "consistent with RLHF playing a role": human raters may prefer answers that contain certain words, so models learn to use them more. — [arXiv 2412.11385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.11385)
- [Study] Sun et al., "Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models" (ICML 2025), told apart ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini and DeepSeek with 97.1% accuracy. Per the paper, ChatGPT favours "such as", "certainly", "overall" and opens with "certainly"/"below is". Claude favours "here", "according to", "based on". On formatting, "ChatGPT emphasizes key points within enumerations using bold formatting and highlights titles with markdown headers", while Claude uses "minimal bold text and headers", and "Gemini uses substantially more italic text". Markdown alone gave 73.1% accuracy. Summary line: "ChatGPT features descriptive language, sophisticated markdown formatting, and in-depth details, while Claude highlights straightforward tone, minimal structure, and summarized content." The models tested were GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Grok-2, Gemini-1.5-Pro and DeepSeek-V3, with responses collected Nov 2024–Feb 2025. — [arXiv 2502.12150v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150v2); [CMU news](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2025/april/cmu-study-shows-large-language-models-have-distinctive-styles)
- [Study] Freeburg, "The Last Fingerprint: How Markdown Training Shapes LLM Prose" (arXiv, 27 Mar 2026) argues "the em dash is markdown leaking into prose". Across 12 models from 5 providers, "when models are instructed to avoid markdown formatting, overt features (headers, bullets, bold) are eliminated or nearly eliminated, but em dashes persist", and "even explicit em dash prohibition fails to eliminate the artifact in some models". The fetch summary reported Llama at 0.0 per 1,000 words and GPT-4.1 at 9.1 per 1,000 words under suppression. I could not confirm Claude's figure. — [arXiv 2603.27006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27006)
- [Study] Human detection is weak on average and strong for heavy users. Wikipedia summarises: "a 2025 study has shown that human ability to distinguish LLM text from human is no better than random chance". In Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer (ACL 2025), "'expert' annotators correctly identified 90% of AI-generated text (and misidentified 3.3% of human-written text)", while participants "who didn't use LLMs much did only slightly better than random chance". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); [Russell et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267/)

#### Claude-specific tells (2026)
- [Primary] Anthropic's own prompting guide for Claude Fable 5.1 (published around 1 Sep 2026) names "mannered prose" as an anti-pattern, verbatim: "Mannered prose substitutes metaphor and flourish for direct statement. Instead of 'a parameter worth varying,' the mannered writer produces 'a dial worth turning.' Instead of 'this point still matters,' they write 'this point earns its keep.' The phrases exist to display the writer, not to convey the idea, and readers can tell. That is why mannered prose irritates: it makes the reader work harder so the writer can perform. It is also imprecise. Metaphors drag in connotations the writer did not choose and cannot control. The fix is to say what you mean. When a literal phrase is available, use it." The short form: "Please remove all mannered prose." The same page says Fable 5.1 "has few stock phrases and little unexplained jargon" but its prose can be "denser … sentences run longer and there are fewer paragraph breaks", and that it "uses bold less and is less likely to reach for headers, lists, or quotation marks". — [Anthropic: Prompting Claude Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1); [What's new in Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1)
- [Community/Practitioner] Werner Robitza's "Claudish" (22 Jun 2026) lists recurring Claude phrasings: "The one thing you need to X", "It's not X, it's Y", "That's precisely the X you've encountered", "Honest caveat: X", "It's not what I thought it was, and here's the smoking gun: X". He also describes a degraded mode of "highly compressed half-sentences, with nouns as adjectives, tech lingo and no discernible flow", e.g. "Cap the study-lifecycle handlers so a hung study can't wedge the deep-link…". He attributes this to reasoning leaking into the output. Wikipedia's guide links this post. — [slhck.info: Claudish](https://slhck.info/software/2026/06/22/claudish.html)
- [Community, aggregator] ExplainX's list of "Claudisms" in Claude Opus 5 (drawn from a Hacker News thread of about 1,778 points and an r/ClaudeAI thread, both by 11 Aug 2026): "load-bearing" ("deliberate and load-bearing rather than tidy"), "worth stating plainly", "carry the argument", "full stop", "and the trap", "The X matters more", "This is not X, it's Y", "isn't just faster — it's cheaper", punchy fragments ("Not a detail. A design decision."), em dashes "everywhere", and colon/semicolon overuse. The piece notes "Anthropic has not published an explanation for these stylistic patterns." — [ExplainX](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/claude-opus-5-load-bearing-claudisms-writing-tells-2026)
- [Community, conflicting numbers] A blog post says Fable 5 used em dashes "2.3× more frequently than earlier versions", "load-bearing" about twice as often, and "honestly"/"frankly" 50% more — [paddo.dev](https://paddo.dev/blog/a-dial-worth-turning/). A daily.dev summary, citing a Text Arena analysis, says Fable 5.1 cut back: "Hedging language dropped 36%", "Honesty qualifiers ('honestly', 'frankly') fell 45%", agreement openers fell "from 2.35% to 0.99% of responses", and em dashes decreased while semicolons increased — [daily.dev](https://daily.dev/posts/claude-fable-5-1-cuts-back-on-ai-writing-tics-fewer-em-dashes-hedges-and-sycophantic-openers-rgnhctzkb). Anthropic's official "What's new" page does not publish these numbers. Treat them as unverified.
- [Reporting] Kyle Chayka in The New Yorker (29 Jun 2026) called the Claude design aesthetic "as noticeable as text-based A.I. tics such as overenthusiastic em-dash usage or 'not X ... but Y' constructions". This is from a search-result summary of [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropics-claude-design-shapes-new-design-aesthetic-aaf43749); the New Yorker page could not be loaded.

#### Claude text now carries a watermark
- [Primary] Anthropic, "How Claude's text watermark works" (14 Aug 2026, updated 1 Sep 2026): "Future Claude models will generate text that contains a watermark." On durability: "Light editing probably won't remove the watermark completely; a complete rewrite where every word is replaced will." The detection API is "in private preview … available to eligible organizations as required under EU law (such as regulators, law enforcement, media, fact-checkers, independent researchers …)", with broader access planned. Detection "doesn't work well on small samples … As a passage increases in length, confidence about Claude's involvement increases too." — [Anthropic news](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)
- [Primary] "Text generated by Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1 carries Anthropic's statistical text watermark on every platform where the model is available." — [What's new in Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1)

### Inferences
- The most reliable tells are rhythms and structures, not individual words: contrast-reveal ("not X, it's Y"), triplets, "-ing" significance tails, fragments used for emphasis, and the same paragraph shape repeated (claim, three supports, quotable closer). Banning words alone won't fix that. Freeburg's finding that em dashes survive explicit prohibition in some models suggests prompt-level bans leak, so a human edit pass is still needed.
- For a Claude-written site in late 2026, the specific risks are em dashes (Claude is now the outlier per the Economist, via Wikipedia), "It's not X, it's Y", "load-bearing"/"carries"/"earns its keep" metaphors, "Honest caveat:"-style hedges, and dense compressed noun-stack sentences.
- Claude is documented as more concise and less bold/header-heavy than ChatGPT. So Groundwind's copy is less likely to read as "ChatGPT listicle" and more likely to read as "terse Claude essay with dashes and reversals".
- Text from newer Claude models (Fable 5.1 confirmed) carries a watermark that survives light editing and gets more detectable with length. Long articles that Claude drafts and a human lightly edits are, in principle, attributable to Claude by media and researchers today and perhaps the public later. Short UI strings are less exposed.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous source documenting "Here's the thing", "whether you're X or Y", "quietly" or "seamless" as Claude-specific. They appear in practitioner trope lists (tropes.fyi has "quietly" and "Here's the kicker"), not in model-comparison studies.
- I could not read the Economist (Jul 2026) study's method or per-model numbers; only Wikipedia's one-line summary.
- The Fable 5 to 5.1 tic-frequency numbers (paddo.dev, daily.dev) conflict and are not confirmed by Anthropic.
- I could not verify whether Claude Opus 5.5 (the model writing much of Groundwind) carries the watermark. Fable 5.1 does; models "launched before August 2, 2026" are in a transition period.
- I could not read the full text of Kriss's NYT essay, so the summary of his argument is paraphrase.

## 2. Design tells: what does the AI-generated or "vibe-coded" website look like in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
There are two families of tells, and both are now named by Anthropic itself. The first is the 2024–25 "VibeCode purple" SaaS kit from v0, Lovable, Bolt and shadcn: Inter, indigo/purple gradients, a centred hero, a pill badge above the H1, three identical icon cards, glassmorphism and Lucide icons. The second is the 2026 "Claude look": a warm cream background, a large italic serif display, a terracotta/rust accent, tracked-out all-caps eyebrows, middle-dot meta strings and ticker bars. Anthropic's own frontend-design skill lists cream #F4F1EA with terracotta #D97757 as a tell and notes that #D97757 is Anthropic's own Claude accent. Fixes become defaults quickly: Space Grotesk and Instrument Serif were recommended as escapes and are now scored as tells.

### Cited Findings

#### Anthropic's own list of AI design tells
- [Primary] Anthropic's public `frontend-design` skill, verbatim: "For calibration, AI-generated design right now clusters around some traits: 1. a warm cream background (near #F4F1EA) with a high-contrast serif display and a terracotta or warm-clay accent (often near #D97757 — Anthropic's own Claude-interaction accent, so on a user's brief it reads as a tell); 2. a near-black background with a single bright acid-green or vermilion accent; 3. a broadsheet-style layout with hairline rules, zero border-radius, and dense newspaper-like columns; 4. the SaaS-card kit: content chopped into identical rounded cards, one border-radius on everything regardless of hierarchy, the same soft grey shadow (rgba(0,0,0,.1)) under each, and gradient washes as decoration; 5. template chrome that appears whatever the subject: a tracked-out ALL-CAPS eyebrow label above every heading; meta strings joined with middle dots ('A · B · C'); labels built as 'WORD — fragment' with a spaced em dash; tinted near-black (#0B0B0B, #111) standing in for black; a monospace face for small data labels; a '→' appended to link and button text." — [anthropics/skills: frontend-design/SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)
- [Primary] The same skill, on typography and motion, verbatim: "Avoid these default typographic treatments; they are the commonest tells of a generated page: Accenting just a single word or phrase in a headline, like putting one word in italic/bold or a different color. Using all caps for labels. Adding unnecessary typographic labels above content." Also: "Many generic designs use numbered markers (01 / 02 / 03), but that's only appropriate if the content actually is a sequence". "A big number with a small label, supporting stats, and a gradient accent is the default treatment" for heroes. "Fade-and-slide-up entrances on each section and hover transitions on every card are the generic default and read as AI-generated." #### The vibe-coded / shadcn kit (2024–2026)
- [Study-like, Practitioner] Adrian Krebs scored 1,590 recent Show HN landing pages with deterministic CSS/DOM checks in Playwright (about 5–10% false positives on manual QA). It recounts that Tailwind creator Adam Wathan apologised on X in August 2025 for making Tailwind UI buttons `bg-indigo-500` five years earlier, which "caused every AI-generated interface on Earth to turn purple". — [prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website)
- [Practitioner] Nikki Kipple, The Crit (Sep 2026), gives the generic recipe: "Typography: Inter for everything", "Icons: Lucide or Heroicons", "Colors: Blue-to-purple gradient backgrounds", "Layout: Centered cards with excessive padding", "Components: Rounded buttons, subtle shadows, blur effects". Her suggested fix includes "Instrument Serif + Inter for editorial trust", the same pairing Krebs scores as a tell. — [thecrit.co](https://thecrit.co/resources/vibe-coding-design-guide)
- [Practitioner] CodeMySpec: "Vibe coded websites look alike because nobody decided how yours should look, so the model used the average of every site it trained on." — [codemyspec.com](https://codemyspec.com/blog/vibe-coded-websites-look-the-same)

### Inferences
- The founder's checklist is largely confirmed. Cream/off-white, serif display (italic emphasis), clay/terracotta accent, tracked small-caps eyebrows, hairline rules, rounded identical cards, pill badges, "→" links, middle-dot meta strings, 01/02/03 markers and fade-up-on-scroll are all named, by Anthropic or by Krebs. Fraunces and Tiempos are not named in the sources I found; Instrument Serif and Space Grotesk are.
- Anthropic's trait #2 ("near-black background with a single bright acid-green or vermilion accent") is worth checking against any dark, forest-toned theme with one bright accent colour. Dark-plus-one-neon is also a cluster.
- Each widely shared fix becomes the next default within months (Space Grotesk, Instrument Serif, the editorial cream look). A defensible look has to come from the subject itself rather than from a list of "non-default" fonts.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how often v0, Lovable or Bolt outputs use each pattern. Krebs's data is tool-agnostic.
- I found no source naming Fraunces or Tiempos specifically as AI tells, or confirming which serif claude.ai itself uses.
- The New Yorker article could not be loaded, so designer quotes come second-hand.

## 3. Audience reaction: how do people respond to AI-generated marketing, and is there outdoor/hunting or small-brand evidence?

### Takeaway
Suspicion is the trigger. When people think content is AI-made, trust, authenticity and purchase intent fall sharply, whether or not it really was (Raptive). By 2026 half of US consumers say they prefer brands that avoid GenAI in consumer-facing content (Gartner), and most find AI-heavy ads "less authentic" and "cringey" (Harris). Results on disclosure conflict: an industry study found disclosure raised trust, while experiments find the word "AI" lowers purchase intent, more so for high-risk purchases. The backlash cases show a split: undisclosed AI that viewers can't spot can test well (Coca-Cola on System1), but AI that viewers can spot draws mockery. Hunting media has its own incidents and editors who refuse AI images. Hunters are not anti-technology: they support AI that helps awareness more than the general public does.

### Inferences
- For Groundwind the risk is less "people hate AI" and more "people discount anything that smells of AI". Raptive's finding that perception, not actual use, drives the penalty means a hand-written page that looks like the Claude template is still penalised.
- Hunters split two ways. They are pro-tool: highly active hunters back AI that improves awareness, which is Groundwind's category. They are also anti-fake: AI images get mocked, editors ban them, and chatbots have given hunters wrong rules. The pitch that fits both is "a tool that helps you read the wind", with no synthetic images or invented authority.
- Canada is a below-average-trust market for AI (Edelman 2024), so "AI" framing is a worse bet in Groundwind's home market than in the US data.
- The Coca-Cola case suggests the problem is visible synthetic-ness plus a breach of expectations, not AI as such. Undetected, competent AI work can test fine. That reinforces the point that the tells, not the tool, cause the damage.

## 4. Remedies: how do practitioners and brands de-AI a voice and a look? Concrete examples

### Takeaway
The remedies that hold up all tie the work to something a model can't average over: the real subject and its materials, a named person, specific places and numbers, real field imagery, and honest admissions of error. Anthropic's own guidance says the same: ground the design in the subject's "industry, subject matter, materials, and vernacular", decide the system before building, review it against what you'd produce for any similar page, and "say what you mean". Surface fixes decay: imperfection, hand-drawn marks and editorial serifs are already being copied. Lasting distinctiveness comes from provenance ("who is saying it") and specificity.

### Cited Findings

#### What Anthropic itself prescribes
- [Primary] "Ground your designs in the subject matter … The subject's industry, subject matter, materials, and vernacular are where distinctive visual choices come from." "Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world." "Choose your typefaces deliberately, not the default families you would reach for on any other project." "Visual structure is information … encode useful information about the content rather than decorate it." "Spend your boldness in one place. Let one element be the memorable thing, keep everything around it quiet and disciplined". Process: draft a token system, "then review that plan against the brief before building: if any part of it reads like the generic default you would produce for any similar page … revise that part". — [frontend-design SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)
- [Primary] On copy inside designs: "Describe what something is or does in plain terms rather than selling it. Being specific and legible to new users is always better than being clever." ### Inferences (applied to Groundwind)
- **Design vernacular.** Per Anthropic's own rule, the distinctive palette and type should come from the subject's materials: topographic and FRI stand maps, blaze orange, waterproof field-notebook paper, licence tags, LiDAR hillshade, compass roses, the app's own wind streaks and scent cones. "Open with the most characteristic thing in the subject's world" points to a real map of a real wind over real terrain as the hero, not a headline with an italicised word. Avoid the cream/serif/terracotta cluster, the dark-plus-one-neon cluster and template chrome (tracked caps eyebrows, "→" links, middle-dot meta, 01/02/03, fade-ups).
- **Copy.** Specific numbers and places carry more weight than a voice that sounds human: a named lake, WMU or bearing, a check count, a correlation, a date, a bull heard at a stated distance. These are things a model can't average. The founder's real field notes (gusts, the slot that channels wind toward a bearing, an artifact found and fixed) are the "show the work and the failures" material. Privacy caveat: exact camp coordinates and a sault-test area near the founder's home should not appear.
- **Process.** Use Claude to draft, check, or code the site, but have the founder write or fully rewrite customer-facing articles. Run a fixed banned-structure pass (em dashes, "not X but Y", triplets, fragments, "-ing" significance tails, metaphor-for-statement) because prompt bans leak. The watermark adds a concrete reason to rewrite long-form text rather than lightly edit it.
- **Imagery.** Use real field photos and screen recordings from actual sits (the app already makes video loops from real weather days). Write a one-line no-AI-imagery policy in the spirit of Montana Outdoors. Hunting audiences have caught and mocked AI images (Connecticut DEEP), and editors and photographers in the space say so openly.
- Treat hand-drawn or "imperfect" styling as a garnish, not the strategy. Mosseri and the Space Grotesk / Instrument Serif history show that surface signals get copied. Provenance lasts: a named founder, a real place, verifiable data.

### Gaps
- I found no documented small hunting or outdoor brand that explicitly "de-AI'd" with measured results. Aerie and iHeart are large brands; Procreate, Cara and Acme Weather are small but not outdoor.
- I found no study comparing audience reaction to "AI as editor" against "AI as author" when readers don't know which was used.
- I found no published brand style guide from an outdoor or hunting brand with a banned-AI-phrase list.
- Custom wordmarks and non-default typefaces as differentiators are supported here only by Anthropic's skill and practitioner opinion, not by outcome data.

## 5. How should a "computed accuracy" product (wind modelling) talk about computation and AI without sounding like AI marketing?

### Takeaway
Lead with what the tool tells a hunter, name the actual method in plain words (terrain, LiDAR, a physics wind model, field checks), show how often it's right and when it's wrong, and avoid "AI-powered". The evidence lines up. The word "AI" lowers emotional trust and purchase intent, more so for high-stakes purchases. Most consumers want "meaningless AI marketing" gone. Gartner's advice is "transparent … what it's doing … clearly beneficial". Numeric uncertainty ranges don't cost the source trust. Hunters favour AI that aids awareness over automation, and they distrust chatbots on rules.

### Cited Findings
- [Study] Mentioning "artificial intelligence" in otherwise identical product descriptions lowered purchase intent via emotional trust, more so for high-risk products — [WSU / Cicek et al. 2024](https://server-nexa.polito.it/hyperkitty/list/nexa@server-nexa.polito.it/thread/VI3XU7NAYL2J4VI5RSAIO4OJJSI7E63I/)
- [Survey] "65% … would 'love if brands would never mention meaningless AI marketing again'"; nearly two-thirds see AI messaging as a "marketing ploy" — [Marketing Brew / Harris Poll](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)
- [Survey] Gartner: use AI "in ways customers can immediately recognize as helpful, while being transparent about when AI is used, what it's doing" — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-16-gartner-marketing-survey-finds-50-percent-of-consumers-prefer-brands-that-avoid-using-genai-in-consumer-facing-content0)
- [Reporting] Industry commentary describes a 2026 shift from "AI-powered" branding toward treating AI "as infrastructure rather than innovation" — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/with-ai-backlash-building-marketers-reconsider-their-approach/); [Breef](https://www.breef.com/breefingroom/articles/the-ai-marketing-backlash-why-ai-first-brands-are-starting-to-fall-flat) (search summary; practitioner opinion)
- [Study] van der Bles, van der Linden, Freeman & Spiegelhalter (PNAS 2020; 5 UK experiments, n = 5,780): communicating uncertainty as a numeric range "does not cause people to lose trust in the source", though it lowers confidence in the number itself. Verbal caveats did worse than numeric ranges. (Pre-2024, but the key primary study.) — [PNAS doi 10.1073/pnas.1913678117](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1913678117); [Winton Centre summary](https://wintoncentre.maths.cam.ac.uk/news/being-upfront-about-your-uncertainty-doesnt-necessarily-mean-you-lose-your-audiences-trust-new-paper-published)

### Inferences
- Groundwind's core computation is mostly not generative AI. It is terrain data (LiDAR DEM, forest inventory) feeding a physics wind solver (WindNinja's momentum solver, per the project's own notes) plus a head-height ground-wind model and scoring rules from published hunting science. Calling that "AI" invites the penalties above. Calling it what it is ("a physics wind model run on 1 m LiDAR of this hillside", "checked against N wind checks") is more accurate and fits Procreate's machine-learning-versus-generative-AI line. (That WindNinja comes from the US Forest Service fire lab is background knowledge, not re-verified here.)
- Make uncertainty part of the product voice, the way Acme does. Publish numeric agreement with field checks, including weak results (the project's notes record a WindNinja-vs-other-solver comparison with r ≤ .55 and that the ground-wind model is "not yet field-validated"). The PNAS evidence says numeric ranges keep trust in the source. Verbal hedging ("may", "can help") reads as AI hedge and performs worse.
- Lead with the hunter's outcome in plain words ("where your scent goes at this stand at 7 a.m.") and put the method one tap down. That matches the project's own progressive-disclosure rule and Gartner's "recognize as helpful … what it's doing".
- Any AI that is used (Claude for code and drafts, any learned correction from phone wind checks) can be disclosed plainly and narrowly, as iHeart does ("behind the scenes"), not hidden and not used as a selling point. Raptive's perception finding means the real risk is looking like AI, not admitting where it was used.
- "Computed accuracy" claims carry their own risk. A wrong scent cone that costs a hunt is the same failure as the chatbot that got duck season wrong. Claims should be bounded ("in tested conditions", "against N checks") and the known artifacts disclosed, such as the documented scent-edge pile-up.
