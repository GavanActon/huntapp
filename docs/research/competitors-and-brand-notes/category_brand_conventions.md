# Hunting-app and outdoor-brand conventions: look, voice, story (for Groundwind, October 2026)

Method note: the hunting-app homepages were fetched as text and also screenshotted headlessly on 2026-10-06 at 1280×800. The first five App Store screenshots of nine apps came from Apple's public iTunes Search API on the same day. Descriptions of logos, colours and screenshots marked "(screenshot 2026-10-06)" are first-hand observations of those captures. They are not quotes from a third party. All other claims are cited inline. The web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few Canadian and Québec items are thinner; these are listed under Gaps.

---

## Q1. Which visual and verbal clichés make hunting apps blur together?

### Takeaway
The ten hunting apps share one template: a black or charcoal ground with an orange accent, heavy condensed all-caps headlines over a phone showing satellite imagery with property lines, a "#1" or "most powerful" claim, "by hunters, for hunters", and "smarter" or "edge" verbs. The faces are camo-clad whitetail or elk celebrities. The template is so uniform that swapping the logos would barely register. The only real breaks are Spartan Forge, which leans military and patriotic, and iHunter, which has dry Canadian humour and a moose-pin mark.

### Inferences
- Groundwind's closest rival, Contors, has already claimed "The hunting map built around the wind", plus orange-on-black and "SMARTER" copy. A Groundwind brand that also said "wind", "smarter", orange or "AI" would read as a Contors copy. The way out is to differ in tone, in honesty (Q3) and in region (Q4), not to claim "wind" alone.
- Black and orange is not a deliberate brand choice in this category. It is hunter-orange safety colour plus dark-mode app chrome. Any palette without orange, black or neon green immediately looks like a different category.
- The universal "#1" and "most powerful" claims are cheap to make and carry little weight. The most credible voices in hunting media (Q3) push the other way, toward modesty about what tech can do.
- Most apps are owned by holding companies (GSM owns both HuntStand and DeerCast) or are fronted by celebrities. A named solo builder with a real camp and real place is a position nobody in this set holds.

### Gaps
- I could not open Instagram or YouTube directly, so follower counts and posting styles are not verified. The faces listed come from homepages, App Store text and search summaries.
- ScoutLook's shutdown is inferred from dead DNS and a missing App Store listing, not from an announcement.
- The this.design onX case study returned HTTP 500, so the tagline and icon-system details come from a search summary. No date was confirmed.

## Q2. What did the standout brands do differently, and what did it earn them?

### Takeaway
The distinctive brands each own one non-generic asset and repeat it relentlessly:
- a **founder's field problem** (Stone Glacier, Exo, First Lite, Kuiu);
- a **science claim with a name** (Sitka's GORE OPTIFADE, "designed around animal vision");
- a **stance that costs money** (Patagonia, MeatEater's conservation voice);
- **humour and a mascot** (Duluth's Angry Beaver, CARROT);
- a **signature visual** (Windy's animated wind particles, Arc'teryx's fossil);
- **radical openness from a solo builder** (Slopes).

What it earned them: premium prices (Arc'teryx, Canada Goose, Sitka), loyalty and word of mouth (Slopes, Strava), and sales growth even after telling people not to buy (Patagonia).

The hooded, lace-neck olive "103" bush shirt (1927) was adopted by hunters and farmers. — [Te Ara](https://teara.govt.nz/en/photograph/25332/the-story-of-swanndri); [Wikipedia: Swanndri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanndri)
- **Outdoor Canada.** "Canada's national fishing and hunting magazine, in print since 1972". It now focuses solely on fishing, hunting and conservation and won CSME Magazine of the Year in 2005, 2011 and 2012. — [Wikipedia: Outdoor Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outdoor_Canada). It signs itself "Canada's fishing and hunting authority". Its headlines are built on named places and species: "Great Slave Lake's far-flung East Arm", walleye, pike, lakers. — [Outdoor Canada](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/)
- **Roots**, a counter-example. It was founded in 1973 by two Detroit natives inspired by Camp Tamakwa in Algonquin Park, and its beaver was "borrowed from the Camp Tamakwa crest". This shows that a specific Canadian place, built into the founding story, can make a brand read as Canadian. — [Culture Trip](https://theculturetrip.com/north-america/canada/articles/a-history-of-roots-canada)

**Weather and data products**
- **Windy.** It began on 10 Nov 2014 as "Windyty", a "hobby project" visualising wind by Ivo Lukačovič, founder of Seznam.cz. It had about 300,000 daily visitors by 2018, passed 50M Google Play downloads in 2025, and took a majority of Meteoblue in 2024. — [Wikipedia: Windy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windy_(weather_service)). The origin story is a personal failure: a snow-kite day ruined by fog on Gotthard Pass led him to say "I needed to create my own version of the weather forecasts." (Search summary of Bloomberg.) — [Bloomberg, 2020](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-02-06/windy-com-is-the-new-favorite-website-for-the-climate-obsessed)
- Windy visually: the whole screen is the product, a colour-ramped wind field with animated particles, a red logo, and model chips ("ECMWF", "GFS", "ICON") shown openly on the timeline. Page title: "Windy: Wind map & weather forecast" (screenshot 2026-10-06). — [Windy](https://www.windy.com/)
- **CARROT Weather.** Brian Mueller (Grailr) built "a sarcasm-wielding robot with a heart of weapons-grade plutonium". Personality settings run from "Friendly" to "Overkill (includes Profanity)". (Search summary.) — [TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/apps/carrot-weather-4-0-big-update-snarkiest-weather-app-around/). Mueller: "I probably spent three times the amount of time on the content for CARROT Weather compared to the time I spent on the coding." and "CARROT's really a combination of my mom, my sister, and my wife." — [AppleWorld.Today, 2015](https://appleworld.today/behind-the-scenes-look-at-carrot-the-a-i-construct-that-shames-you-into-submission). Current homepage: "the crazy-powerful weather app" and "Make the most of this nice weather that I generated for you. Or else." on a saturated blue (screenshot 2026-10-06). — [CARROT](https://www.meetcarrot.com/weather/)
- **Dark Sky.** Famous for telling users the precise time to expect rain where they stood. Apple bought it in 2020, and it stopped working on 1 Jan 2023, with its features folded into Apple Weather. — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/apples-popular-dark-sky-app-disappears-tonight_id144545)
- **Strava.** "If it's not on Strava, it didn't happen" became a cultural phrase. Growth came from building sharing, Kudos, segments and leaderboards into the product, rather than paid acquisition or celebrities. (Search summary.) — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/strava-marketing-strategy)
- **Gaia GPS.** "Maps for Every Adventure" and "Leave cell service and your worries behind with Gaia GPS." — [Gaia GPS](https://www.gaiagps.com/). Acquired by Pocket Outdoor Media (now Outside) in 2021. — [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2021/07/05/gaia-gps-acquired)
- **FATMAP.** Acquired by Strava in January 2023 and shut down on 1 Oct 2024. Adventures, guidebooks and waypoints were not migrated. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/26/strava-to-shutter-3d-mapping-platform-fatmap-18-months-after-acquisition); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2024/6/strava-to-shut-down-fatmap-by-oct-1st-and-integrates-key-features-into-premium-subscription/)
- **PeakVisor.** "3D Maps & Peaks Identification" and "Be a superhero of outdoor navigation…". The look is illustrated 3D panoramas with peak labels. — [PeakVisor](https://peakvisor.com/)
- **Slopes.** A solo founder, Curtis Herbert, grew it from "$10,600" in its first two years to $1M ARR in 9 years and won a 2022 Apple Design Award. He documented "every revenue chart and hard lesson in public" in the Slopes Diaries. In his words: "They feel like we're not just some weird, faceless corporation." and "charge your value and charge it every year". — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/slopes-from-indie-side-hustle-to-1m-in-arr-and-an-apple-design-award). The homepage still says "Slopes is built by a small team with a big passion for skiing and snowboarding", with the tagline "Track your edge", on a pale ice-blue ground (screenshot 2026-10-06). — [Slopes](https://getslopes.com/)

### Inferences
- The most useful templates for Groundwind are Stone Glacier and Exo (a builder's field problem: "couldn't find X, so built it"), Slopes (a solo builder working in the open, which earns loyalty) and Windy (a hobby wind map whose visual is the brand). The memory notes show Groundwind already has the raw material: a real camp, field wind checks, and docs about what is "not yet field-validated".
- Sitka shows that a named, defensible science claim ("designed around animal vision") can carry a premium brand. For Groundwind the equivalent is a claim like "wind at head height on your ground, from LiDAR". It should be stated plainly, with the method visible, as Windy shows its ECMWF, GFS and ICON chips.
- Humour works in this space when it is dry and specific (Duluth, CARROT, iHunter's "(most) provincial flavours"). Bro humour does not. iHunter, the incumbent Canadian app, already uses a little of this, so wit alone won't set Groundwind apart.
- FATMAP and Dark Sky show users fear that an app they depend on will be bought and closed. "Your data stays on your phone" and "made by one person in Ontario" can be stated as reassurance, not apology. This is an inference; no direct survey was found.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were fetched for what Sitka, First Lite or Kuiu earned in loyalty or price premiums beyond Kuiu's day-one sales and Canada Goose's revenue growth.
- Patagonia's own pages were down (maintenance) on 2026-10-06, so quotes rely on Wikipedia, and the 40% sales lift is secondary.
- No Brand New or Under Consideration case study for any hunting app was found. The agency pages of Sitka and Kuiu were not located.
- Dark Sky founders, design reputation and quotes: the Wikipedia page was not found at the URLs tried.

## Q3. How do hunters feel about tech and AI, and how should a wind-modelling app talk about technology?

### Takeaway
Hunters split sharply by what a tool does. Mapping and GPS are almost universally accepted as fair chase: 94% of Idaho big-game hunters. AI for finding or identifying wildlife is almost universally rejected: about 90%. The ethical line, as Boone & Crockett and Aldo Leopold frame it, is whether a tool "augment[s] the role of skill" or substitutes for woodcraft. Hunting media is also tired of overclaiming. Even the AI-deer app's own coverage admits "no app, artificial intelligence, or algorithm can fully predict the behavior of a wild whitetail buck." A wind and scent app sits naturally on the accepted side: it describes the hunter's own scent and the land, as weather and maps do. It should never claim to locate or predict animals, and it should avoid "AI", "intel" and "kill more".

### Cited Findings
- In a 2024 Idaho survey of 2,170 resident big-game hunters, "about 90 percent say that the use of artificial intelligence for wildlife identification violates fair-chase principles". "Fully 90 percent" oppose drones, and about 76% say thermal and night-vision optics don't align. GPS and mapping technology: "94 percent say it aligns with fair-chase ethics". Transmitting trail cameras split about half and half. Andrew McKean, Outdoor Life, 9 Jan 2025. — [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/conservation/idaho-hunter-attitudes-technology/)
- Idaho's Hunting and Advanced Technology (HAT) Working Group drew over 750 applicants, including "traditional archers, competitive long-range shooters, houndsmen, trappers…". Its final consensus recommendations are dated 28 March 2025. — [IDFG HAT](https://idfg.idaho.gov/hat-working-group)
- Boone & Crockett's *Fair Chase* (Fall 2024, "The Technology Conundrum", Jodi Stemler) quotes Leopold: "Our tools for the pursuit of wildlife improve faster than we do, and sportsmanship is a voluntary limitation in the use of these armaments. It is aimed to augment the role of skill and shrink the role of gadgets in the pursuit of wild things."
- The same article quotes Leopold on "contraptions" that "are offered as aids to self-reliance, hardihood, woodcraft, or marksmanship, but too often function as substitutes for them."
- B&C's position is that the Club "supports the use of legal technology to the extent it does not take an unfair advantage over the animal." Mary Webster, B&C: "It's not so much about the technology or piece of equipment itself being unethical—a lot of times it's about how it's used."
- The article also warns that if tech raises success rates too much, managers respond with "shorter seasons, fewer tags, or both". All four B&C points: — [B&C Fair Chase, Fall 2024 (PDF)](https://aws.boone-crockett.org/s3fs-public/atoms/files/fc_fall2024_technology_conundrum.pdf)
- B&C's records rule: "The use of any technology that delivers real-time location data … in a manner that elicits an immediate response … is not permitted." Utah, Nevada and Arizona ban trail cameras in season; Montana, New Hampshire and Alaska restrict cellular ones. Alex Robinson, Outdoor Life, Sept 2022. — [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting/is-hunting-with-cellular-trail-cameras-fair-chase-or-cheating/)
- MeatEater (Tony J. Peterson, Aug 2022): "technology isn't a true shortcut to success, but another precise tool". He warns tech "can also convince us that we have everything need to be good hunters without actually learning about the woods and the deer." Minnesota success rates stayed flat from 2010 to 2021 despite the tech. — [MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/wired-to-hunt/whitetail-hunting-gear/why-hasnt-technology-greatly-impacted-deer-hunting-success)
- MeatEater's Mark Kenyon wrote about being troubled by a cell camera with live streaming, and has a piece on "how to create a fair chase cell camera". (Search summary.) — [MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/wired-to-hunt/whitetail-hunting-gear/how-to-create-a-fair-chase-cell-camera)
- "The onX effect": mapping apps blamed for crowding. Forum critics say apps let novices reach spots "that previously required years of relationship-building and fieldwork". Christine Peterson, Outdoor Life, Mar 2024. — [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/conservation/the-onx-effect/)
- Minnesota DNR, Feb 2026: an enforcement officer worries about AI "simulators" that "are able to recreate these big bucks' patterns". A biologist says tools "impact how hunters are perceived by the non-hunting public". — [Outdoor News](https://www.outdoornews.com/2026/02/06/as-outdoor-tech-advances-minnesota-dnr-deals-with-fair-chase-questions/)
- Idaho and Wyoming agencies warned hunters off AI answers about regulations after someone hunted a day early on an AI-given waterfowl opener. Idaho's Roger Phillips: "That's like, 'Well my buddy told me the limit was five trout, not four.'" Dac Collins, Outdoor Life via Yahoo, 23 Oct 2025. — [Yahoo/Outdoor Life](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ai-told-duck-season-open-220141188.html)
- NRA Women (Jo Deering, July 2026): "Technology can't ruin hunting; people can ruin hunting." Mapping is framed as ethical: "an app like onX can help keep us on the property we're supposed to be on without trespassing." — [NRA Women](https://www.nrawomen.com/content/is-technology-ruining-hunting)
- Pope & Young and Moultrie Mobile held a joint discussion on technology ethics (June 2025). (Search summary.) — [Pope & Young](https://pope-young.org/blog/15381/Pope-and-Young-Joins-Forces-with-Moultrie-Mobile-to-Discuss-the-Ethics-of-Technology-and-Hunting-Moving-Forward)
- The Spartan Forge coverage, in its own words: "no app, artificial intelligence, or algorithm can fully predict the behavior of a wild whitetail buck". Such tech should not "remove the need for scouting, but to make scouting smart." — [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/hunting/spartan-forge-hunting-app/)

### Inferences
- **Where Groundwind sits on the line.** Wind and scent modelling is about the hunter's own odour and the terrain. It sits with mapping and weather, which have 94% acceptance, not with AI wildlife identification (about 90% rejection) or real-time location tools. The brand should say this explicitly: "It shows where *your* scent goes. It doesn't find animals."
- **Words to avoid:** "AI-powered", "predict deer or moose movement", "kill more", "intel", "target", "unfair advantage", "never get winded again", and "#1".
- **Words that fit:** "read the wind", "woodcraft", "wind checks", "your ground", "on the ground, at head height", "what the hill does to the wind", "check it yourself", "field-tested at [named camp]".
- Field wind checks (milkweed, powder, thread) are woodcraft hunters already respect. Making the app the tool that *trains* the hunter's own checks fits Leopold's "augment the role of skill" closely. It is a defensible ethic, not just a feature.
- **Technical honesty as voice.** Show the model's confidence and its limits, as Windy names its models, and say when something is "not yet field-checked". This answers the Peterson and Outdoor Life scepticism head on. Contors leans into "Scout AI … tells you which one to sit"; Groundwind can be the brand that tells you *why*, and invites you to check.
- Party moose hunting in Ontario and Québec is social and tradition-heavy (Q4). Language that sounds like "one guy with an AI edge" reads worse there than "the whole camp knows where the wind will carry". This is an inference, not survey-backed.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of Canadian hunters' attitudes to apps or AI (OFAH, FédéCP or provincial). Idaho is the best data available, and it is US.
- I found no MeatEater or Rinella commentary specifically on wind, scent or weather apps, nor a direct Rinella quote on apps. The onX ambassador listing of Rinella may be dated.
- I did not read the HAT Working Group's final recommendations PDF.

## Q4. Which cues signal northern Canadian bush, Crown land, moose camp and Québec chasse culture, rather than Americana?

### Takeaway
Authentic Canadian outdoor brands signal place through **specific geography and working proof**, not flags or stripes:
- Canada Goose: Rangers and McMurdo scientists;
- Kanuk: Québec's humid winters and the harfang;
- Outdoor Canada: Great Slave Lake, walleye and lakers;
- Roots: Algonquin's camp crest;
- iHunter: "(most) provincial flavours", WMUs, moose draws.

The richest shared visual heritage is the Canadian Shield, the windswept pine on rock above a lake. It is directly on-theme for a wind brand. It also carries a well-documented "empty land" critique, as do the HBC stripes. The stripes are now a Canadian Tire–owned trademark. In Québec, French has legal as well as cultural weight.

### Cited Findings
- Tom Thomson's *The West Wind* (1917, AGO) is a pine bent by wind over water. Its tree has been described as growing "in the national ethos as our one and only tree in a country of trees". David Silcox called it and *The Jack Pine* "the visual equivalent of a national anthem". Thomson sketched it in 1916 while a park ranger in Algonquin Park. — [Wikipedia: The West Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_West_Wind_(painting)); [AGO](https://ago.ca/agoinsider/welcome-back-west-wind)
- The Group of Seven repeatedly found its national symbol in "a weathered, twisted (or windblown) tree on a barren outcropping of rock (the Precambrian shield) overlooking a lake". It used "bright colours" and "bold, expressive strokes", and designed its own group logo. Reproductions hung in "every school in the country". (Search summary.) — [Canadian Encyclopedia: Group of Seven](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/group-of-seven)
- The critique: the Group's landscapes present the land as empty, reinforcing terra nullius and erasing Indigenous presence. (Summary of a Walrus essay hosted by the McMichael.) — [The Walrus via McMichael (PDF)](https://mcmichael.com/wp-content/uploads/The-Walrus-The-Group-of-Seven-Doesn%E2%80%99t-Define-Canadian-Art-February-19.pdf)
- HBC point blanket: made from 1779, white with "green, red, yellow and indigo stripes". "For some Indigenous people the HBC point blanket represents colonization and the dispossession of their land". In 2022 HBC and the Chanie Wenjack Foundation started the Blanket Fund. (Search summary.) — [Canadian Encyclopedia](https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/hudson-s-bay-point-blanket); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7488401)
- On 15 May 2025, Canadian Tire agreed to buy the "HBC Stripes and various company names, logos, designs, coat of arms and brand trademarks" for $30M. Greg Hicks: "Some things are just meant to stay Canadian". — [Canadian Tire press release](https://canadiantire.mediaroom.com/2025-05-15-Canadian-Tire-plans-to-steward-the-HBC-coat-of-arms-and-Stripes,-with-purchase-of-iconic-Hudsons-Bay-Company-brand-assets)
- The Woods brand (from 1885, canvas tents and bags "designed for the harshest regions in Canada") is now held by Canadian Tire in North America. (Search summary.) — [Made in CA](https://madeinca.ca/?p=2580)
- Kanuk: snowy owl as "The official bird of Québec", "Designed for Québec's humid winters", bilingual site. — [Kanuk](https://kanuk.com/en/pages/about-us)
- iHunter's voice is wry, provincial and modest ("Available in (most) provincial flavours."). Its mark puts moose antlers on a map pin, and it states "Designed and developed in Canada. 🍁". — [iHunter](https://www.ihunterapp.com/)
- Bill 96 and the Charter of the French Language, from 1 June 2025: "A 'registered trademark' or a 'recognized trademark' in another language need not be translated". But "Any generic term or a description of the product included in the trademark must be translated". On exterior signs, French must be "markedly predominant", meaning at least twice the space. — [PCK IP](https://www.pckip.com/article/what-brand-owners-need-to-know-about-trademarks-in-quebec). Only *registered* English marks qualify, with a French descriptor. (Search summary.) — [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2022/06/bill-96-and-trademarks-public-signage-and-commercial-advertising)
- Ontario moose culture: hunters "apply to a random draw" for an adult tag, and others "party hunt" on it, so parties of 10+ share one tag. Camps run for decades with up to about 20 owners. Crown-land camps face a 21-day limit under the Public Lands Act. (Search summary of ERO comments and CBC.) — [Ontario: Moose management review](https://www.ontario.ca/page/moose-management-review); [CBC Thunder Bay](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3231995)
- The National Topographic System dates from 1927 and today uses 1:50,000 and 1:250,000. At 1:50,000, 1 cm equals 500 m. — [NRCan NTS](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/maps-tools-publications/maps/topographic-maps/national-topographic-system-maps); [Canadian Encyclopedia: NTS](https://www.thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/national-topographic-system)
- Contrast with Americana: Spartan Forge's hero is a full-bleed US flag, and the category's scale claims are US-federal ("BLM", "all 50 States", "1.8 billion acres of public land"). — [Spartan Forge](https://spartanforge.ai/) (screenshot 2026-10-06); [onX](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt)

### Inferences
- **Cues that read as authentically northern Canadian bush, and that no app uses:**
  - Crown land, WMU numbers and NTS sheet numbers used as typographic elements (for example "WMU 21B · NTS 42C");
  - bush-road and cut-block vocabulary ("the cut", "the old skid trail");
  - spruce, jack pine, tamarack and the bog;
  - the moose call and party-hunt social unit ("the camp", not "the hunter");
  - a field-notebook or tally-sheet texture;
  - bilingual labels on a level footing ("vent au sol / ground wind").
- **The West Wind is unusually on-theme** for a wind brand: a tree shaped by the wind, painted in Ontario's Algonquin Park, and already part of the national picture of the Shield. Use it as a *reference* (a wind-bent conifer drawn as data or as a streamline), not as pastiche, and avoid the "empty land" framing. Show a camp, people, a bush road: an inhabited, worked land.
- **Avoid:**
  - HBC stripes: now a Canadian Tire trademark, with colonial baggage;
  - maple leaves as decoration;
  - Indigenous motifs or inukshuks;
  - red-and-black buffalo check used as kitsch;
  - "True North" (Canadian Tire's own strategy name);
  - anything resembling a US-flag or military register.
- **Québec.** A registered English trademark "Groundwind" can be used untranslated. Any descriptive words (for example "hunting wind map") need French, and Québec hunters expect French-first copy. Native-sounding French copy is essential, not translated English. Terms like *orignal*, *câller*, *pourvoirie*, *ZEC*, *camp de chasse* and *vent* should be checked with a Québécois hunter. This is unverified; see Gaps.
- In the 2025 "stay Canadian" climate that Canadian Tire's own HBC release leans on, "made in Ontario by one person, for the Canadian bush" is a credible differentiator against a field that is all US companies. The general consumer trend was not verified; see Gaps.

### Gaps
- My web-search budget ran out before I could source: Québec *chasse à l'orignal* culture in French (FédéCP, Sépaq, ZEC vocabulary, Québec hunting media such as *Sentier Chasse-Pêche*); Indigenous perspectives on moose hunting in Québec (a Reporterre article returned 403); and the 2025 "Buy Canadian" consumer data.
- One unverified figure surfaced only in a search summary: about 122,500 Québec moose hunters, 1.38 million hunting days and $121.5M spent per year. It appeared to come from a BAPE document. Verify before use. — [BAPE DB29 (PDF)](https://archives.bape.gouv.qc.ca/sections/mandats/r175-60_227/documents/DB29.pdf)
- I did not verify NTS map colour and typography conventions in detail; the sources found cover only scale and history.
- Whether Bill 96 rules apply to app-store listings and in-app text was not covered by the sources found.

## Q5. What naming, mark and colour territory is unclaimed?

### Takeaway
The category has claimed:
- **names**: "Hunt-", "-cast" and military compounds;
- **marks**: X, antlers, crosshair, shield, deer head, map pin;
- **colours**: hunter orange on black, with neon green as the alternative, plus camo texture.

Contors has added contour lines and "wind". Unclaimed: a name from natural phenomena or fieldcraft rather than "hunt" (Groundwind already qualifies); a mark drawn from air movement or a wind-shaped tree rather than weapons or animals; a cool northern palette (spruce black-green, lichen, birch white, slate, a cold sky or wind cyan) instead of orange; and a voice built on dry, specific, bilingual technical honesty.

### Cited Findings
- **Names:** HuntStand, HuntWise (with HuntCast and WindCast), GoHunt, iHunter, DeerCast, BaseMap's HuntWind™, Spartan Forge, onX. — [HuntStand](https://www.huntstand.com/); [HuntWise](https://www.huntwise.com/); [DeerCast](https://www.deercast.com/); [BaseMap](https://www.basemap.com/); [iHunter](https://www.ihunterapp.com/); [Spartan Forge](https://spartanforge.ai/)
- **Marks** (screenshot 2026-10-06):
  - an X in an orange square (onX);
  - an X built from an antler (HuntStand);
  - an orange antler (HuntWise);
  - a shield (Spartan Forge);
  - a deer head in a shield (DeerCast);
  - a mountain in a green square (BaseMap);
  - a GOHUNT chevron and pin;
  - moose antlers on a red pin (iHunter);
  - an orange compass or crosshair star in the O (Contors).
- **Colours** (screenshot 2026-10-06): black or charcoal with orange for onX, HuntWise, GOHUNT, Contors and DeerCast; black with neon green for BaseMap; black with green CTAs and orange polygons for HuntStand; flag reds and olive for Spartan Forge.
- Outside hunting: Windy owns the full-screen particle wind field and a red logo; CARROT owns saturated blue and a snarky robot; Slopes uses pale ice-blue; Sitka uses white space and documentary photos (screenshot 2026-10-06). — [Windy](https://www.windy.com/); [CARROT](https://www.meetcarrot.com/weather/); [Slopes](https://getslopes.com/); [Sitka](https://www.sitkagear.com/)
- Contors already uses contour-line backgrounds and claims "built around the wind". — [App Store: Contors](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/contors/id6790434038)
- Arc'teryx's designer on why a hard-to-reproduce, distinctive mark is "ultimately worth it". — [Outdoor Rec Archive](https://outdoorrecarchive.substack.com/p/origins-of-the-archaeopteryx)

### Inferences
- **Name.** "Groundwind" sits outside every pattern above: no "hunt", no "cast", no weapon word. Keep it, and resist adding "Hunt" or "Cast" product names (no "GroundCast"). Use plain field words for features: "wind check", "sit", "the camp", "cone".
- **Mark directions with no rival in the set:**
  - a single streamline bending over and around a ridge or along the ground, like a telltale thread;
  - a wind-bent conifer drawn in one line;
  - a smoke or milkweed wisp;
  - a survey-style bench mark or tick.
  Avoid crosshairs, antlers, shields, pins and X shapes, and avoid contour-line backgrounds, which Contors now uses.
- **Colour.** Leave orange to the field. A cool northern palette is unclaimed: deep spruce or bog black-green, lichen and sage, birch or paper off-white, Shield granite grey, and one cold wind colour (cyan or sky). This also matches the app's existing chrome as recorded in project memory (forest dark, camo-sage accent, cyan wind, no yellow). Use orange only if it means "hunter orange" safety.
- **Imagery.** Do not use trophy bucks, camo hunters holding phones, or tilted 3D phones in clouds. Use the land and the air: wind-bent spruce, flagging tape on a bush road, a hand holding milkweed fluff, a camp at dawn, a canoe or a quad on a cut line. Show moose sign, not a moose trophy, and real people from a real camp, Québec and Ontario.
- **Voice.** Unclaimed in hunting apps: dry, specific, technically honest, bilingual and slightly wry (between CARROT and Windy). For example "Not yet field-checked at your spot. Do a wind check." In place of "#1": a named camp, real WMUs, a solo builder's notes, as in Slopes Diaries.
- **Story.** The founder-and-field-problem template has been used in packs and apparel but not in apps. "I hunt a moose camp near White River; the forecast wind was never the wind at my sit, so I modelled it from the LiDAR" follows the Stone Glacier and Windy origin pattern and would be unique among the ten apps.

### Gaps
- No trademark search was done for wind-mark shapes or for "Groundwind" in Canada (CIPO) or the US. This is needed before committing to a mark, and given Bill 96 a Canadian registration matters.
- The colour observations come from one desktop screenshot per site and five App Store images per app. Dark and light variants, social templates and Android listings were not checked.
