# Make Groundwind sound like its founder

Groundwind holds the head-height wind position alone for now, and its largest brand risk is a site that reads like the default output of the model that wrote it. About twenty apps sell wind or scent to hunters, and most of them draw a straight cone from a forecast or a weather station. The one that comes close, Contors, launched publicly on 24 September 2026 for US whitetail only and gives its terrain-bent scent plume away on its free plan. No rival claims head-height wind under the canopy, wind checks that correct the model, combined party scent, forecasts by satellite text or Ontario and Québec moose, and **no app in the category has published an accuracy figure for its wind or scent**. An audit of groundwind.app finds the familiar AI tells already removed, with no em dashes and no hype, but the structure left behind is Claude's: about ten "X, not Y" contrasts at the top of the page, period-split two-beat headlines, a colon in 35 lines of homepage text, one feature row repeated nine times, and a light theme whose cream is a near-exact match for the colour Anthropic's own design guide names as an AI tell. The app behind the "Open the beta" button still calls itself Pic River and wears the icon from your boat app. What would make Groundwind impossible to confuse with anyone else already sits in your docs and memory notes, including the bog check that found the model 65° out and the bull that circled downwind of you on 27 September. The fix is to put your name and your camp's measured numbers on the site in your own words while Claude sticks to code and typo correction, and nothing on the site should call the wind model AI. Moose in Ontario and Québec is the starting market. Your pitch of 6 October also names elk, deer and bear, and it closes by asking hunters for their wind checks so the model improves for everyone. Shared checks would give Groundwind an asset no rival can copy by adding a solver, and they need a privacy promise the site does not make yet.

**Status on 6 October.** Most of the site and app fixes in this report were made later on 6 October and are not yet committed or deployed. The table of changes marks each one. Line numbers in the audit refer to site/index.html as committed in 47d3998, before the homepage was rebuilt. On that version the audit counted 9 feature rows, 12 phone frames, 12 mono dot-tags and 33 lines of visible text with a colon. The rebuilt homepage leads with "Don't get winded." and five short pillars in your order (scent, learn, plan, bush, no bars). Each pillar opens its own depth in place with a "How" button, and the science stays in view as its own section, "A world-class wind model.", with the six layers and their sources. It has 5 rows, no dot-tags, 13 lines with a colon and no heading in the "X, not Y" form.

## About twenty apps sell wind to hunters, and Contors comes closest to Groundwind's model

The category sorts by where its wind comes from. The simplest apps show forecast or station wind at a pin and compare it with an "ideal wind" the hunter sets for each stand. onX, Moultrie, HuntWise, DeerCast and iHunter work this way, and onX takes its wind from the nearest of its Weather Underground and NOAA stations ([onX blog](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/wind-weather-has-arrived)). The next step up draws a straight scent cone downwind of the forecast. HuntStand's HuntZone and BaseMap's HuntWind™ do this, as do 2026 newcomers such as ProHunt and CoHunt ([HuntStand](https://www.huntstand.com/fieldnotes/deer/a-detailed-guide-to-huntstand-app-tools-for-deer-hunters/); [ProHunt](https://prohunt.app/)). Two tiny apps claim more in a line of store copy. Fur Ledger says its "Scent Cone is now terrain aware", and XHUNT draws a "bounded animated plume" ([Fur Ledger](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6749603003); [XHUNT](https://apps.apple.com/bf/app/xhunt/id6744701432)). **No hunting app discloses its solver or its grid resolution.** None describes what the forest canopy does to the wind, and none names WindNinja or any other CFD model.

Contors is the rival to study. Contors LLC of Madison, Alabama, founded by Orry Moody, put out a launch release on 24 September 2026 claiming "4 industry firsts". Among them are a hunter's scent drawn as a plume "spilling into draws, wrapping points, pooling where the air goes slack" and "Scout, an AI assistant that runs the app" ([EIN Presswire](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/944773435/contors-hunting-map-app-launches-on-ios-android-and-web-with-4-industry-firsts)). Its site says it "solves the forecast wind over the terrain of your property" and "classifies the air as wind-driven, thermal or mixed" ([Contors](https://contors.com/features/wind-scent-thermals/)). The taglines are "Scout Smarter. Hunt Smarter." on the homepage and "The hunting map built around the wind." in the App Store ([contors.com](https://contors.com); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/contors/id6790434038)). It is "built for Whitetail, and nothing else" and covers "U.S. properties only". Its paid plans cost US$39.99 and US$79.99 a year with one property free, and the company says **"the wind, scent and live weather tools are part of every plan, free included"** ([Contors](https://contors.com/features/wind-scent-thermals/)). A hunter who has used Contors will expect a scent cone for nothing. It admits limits in a voice close to Groundwind's footer ("This is a model, not a measurement") and flags the map "when the field is uncalibrated for your area", but nothing on its pages lets the hunter correct the model. Its Hunt Mode is described for sitting ([Contors](https://contors.com/features/field-first-interface/)), and its 13 App Store ratings are too few to show how the plume does in the field. Its terrain comes from USGS 3DEP LiDAR, which stops at the border. My reading is that Canada would need a new data pipeline; the company itself says only "U.S. properties only".

### Canadian apps sell land data, and NordQuest is the first to claim terrain wind in Québec

iHunter is the Canadian incumbent, rated 4.7 from about **30,000 ratings in the Canadian App Store, roughly 13 times onX's 2,300** there ([App Store CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/ihunter/id570558600); [onX CA](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/onx-hunt-canada-hunting-maps/id672902340)). ### Your pitch aims past moose and past one hunter's checks

## The site has dropped Claude's vocabulary and kept Claude's sentence shapes

You put the worry this way: "if everyone looks and sounds like Claude, we won't stand out." The research supports it, and the audit of groundwind.app shows where it already applies. Claude drafted most of the site's words. The words themselves are clean, but the shapes of the sentences and of the page are recognisable. This section sets out the evidence first, then the specific lines and settings to change.

### Looking generated costs trust even when a person wrote the page

The penalty follows suspicion. In Raptive's survey of 3,000 US adults, people who suspected content was AI-made rated it **48% lower on trust and 57% lower on authenticity, with purchase consideration down 14%**, and Raptive found "it doesn't matter if the content was generated by AI or not" ([Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/the-ai-stink-is-real-and-its-costing-brands/)). A 2026 Harris Poll found 73% of consumers less likely to trust an ad they suspected was made with AI ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)). Detection depends on the reader. In an ACL 2025 study, annotators who use language models heavily identified 90% of AI-generated text, while people who rarely use them did only slightly better than chance ([Russell et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.267/)). The early adopters most likely to open a beta from a QR code are probably heavy users, though that is my inference and nobody has tested it with hunters. Canada is a harder market for anything that reads as AI. Only 31% of Canadians in the 2024 Edelman survey trusted the technology, 19 points under the global average ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/distrust-of-ai-significantly-higher-in-canada-than-other-countries-survey-finds/)). Hunting media is openly hostile to synthetic images. Montana Outdoors' editor says "we don't accept images manipulated with artificial intelligence", and Connecticut's DEEP deleted an AI hunter-orange safety image after people mocked its extra fingers ([Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/01/26/an-ai-for-an-eye-digital-trickery-through-artificial-intelligence-bedevils-wildlife-photography/); [NewsTimes](https://www.newstimes.com/connecticut/article/connecticut-deep-ai-art-hunting-season-21049963.php)). The visual style now has a public name as well. Kyle Chayka's New Yorker piece on the AI design aesthetic described "beige- and cream-colored backgrounds, rusty orange-hued accents" and subheadings that are "tracked out" ([Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/claude-aesthetic/)).

The site has already removed the tells people check for first. Across the six public pages the audit found zero em dashes and zero exclamation marks in visible copy. The phrase "whether you're" never appears, and the spelling is Canadian ([site/](file:///C:/dev/huntapp/site/)). Contors' homepage subheading, by comparison, contains a spaced em dash. What remains is structural, and the research treats structure as the more reliable tell. Wikipedia's guide to AI writing lists em dashes, negative parallelism ("It's not …, it's …"), the rule of three and punchy fragments among its signs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). A 2026 study of 12 models found that em dashes survive explicit instructions to drop them in some models ([Freeburg](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27006)). A word ban in a prompt does not change sentence shape, and even bans on a single character leak.

### Four drafting habits keep the patterns from coming back

Fixing today's lines will not last if the next page is drafted the same way. Four habits would hold. First, write the customer-facing words yourself, typed or dictated from camp, and use Claude to fix typos and check facts. Second, run a structure check over site/ before each deploy that counts em dashes, ", not " and lines opening with "Not", headings without a verb, colon-led sentences and three-item lists, because instructions in a prompt leak and only a check of the output catches what got through. Third, rewrite long articles completely when Claude has drafted them. Anthropic says light editing "probably won't remove the watermark completely" and that "a complete rewrite where every word is replaced will", and it confirms the watermark in Fable 5.1 text on every platform; whether the Opus 5.5 model that drafted much of this site carries it is not confirmed ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark); [What's new in Fable 5.1](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1)). Fourth, keep a short file of your own verbatim lines from the memory notes as the voice reference, so every draft is checked against how you talk. This report was written by Claude under instructions to avoid the patterns above. That shows they can be removed on request. Whether what remains sounds like you is something only your own drafting settles.

### Groundwind's computation is physics and field checks, so calling it AI only costs trust

Hunters accept tools that describe the land and reject tools that find animals for them. In a 2024 survey of 2,170 Idaho big-game hunters, **94% said GPS and mapping fit fair chase, and about 90% said AI for wildlife identification violates it** ([Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/conservation/idaho-hunter-attitudes-technology/)). Boone and Crockett quotes Leopold's test, that sportsmanship "is aimed to augment the role of skill and shrink the role of gadgets" ([B&C Fair Chase](https://aws.boone-crockett.org/s3fs-public/atoms/files/fc_fall2024_technology_conundrum.pdf)). Wind and scent modelling describes the hunter's own scent and the ground, which puts it with maps and weather, and the powder check is woodcraft hunters already respect. The word "AI" has the opposite effect. In Washington State University experiments with more than 1,000 US adults, adding "artificial intelligence" to otherwise identical product descriptions lowered purchase intention through lower emotional trust, more so for high-risk products ([WSU study summary](https://server-nexa.polito.it/hyperkitty/list/nexa@server-nexa.polito.it/thread/VI3XU7NAYL2J4VI5RSAIO4OJJSI7E63I/)). In the 2026 Harris Poll, 65% of consumers said they would "love if brands would never mention meaningless AI marketing again" ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)). Contors sells "Scout AI", and Spartan Forge calls itself "The ONLY hunting app that uses artificial intelligence to predict deer movement" ([Contors](https://contors.com/); [spartanforge.ai](https://spartanforge.ai/)). Groundwind has no reason to join them, since its method is Environment Canada's HRDPS forecast, 1 m LiDAR, the Ontario and Québec forest inventories, WindNinja's momentum solver and published hunting science. Name those on screen the way Windy shows which forecast model it is using as chips on its timeline ([Windy](https://www.windy.com/)).

Numbers build more trust than caveats do. In five UK experiments with 5,780 people, stating uncertainty as a numeric range did not reduce trust in the source, and verbal caveats did worse than numbers ([PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1913678117)). Acme Weather, from Dark Sky's founders, says outright that "Forecasts are often wrong" and shows a spread of alternate forecasts so users can see how unsure it is ([Acme Weather](https://acmeweather.com/blog/introducing-acme-weather)). Groundwind's footer and FAQ hedge in words. Once your checks are exported, replace those lines with the tally the hunt log already keeps. Add a page of known problems that names the scent cones running long at tree lines, where head-height wind drops, and the fact that the ground-wind model has been field-checked on one evening ([memory: huntapp-scent-edge-pileup.md](file:///C:/Users/gavan/.claude/projects/c--dev-huntapp/memory/huntapp-scent-edge-pileup.md); [memory: huntapp-ground-wind.md](file:///C:/Users/gavan/.claude/projects/c--dev-huntapp/memory/huntapp-ground-wind.md)). The game heat maps sit nearer the fair-chase line than the wind does. Your pitch already frames them that way, as where the animals are likely to be, which "doesn't mean they're there". Present them as habitat read from published studies, and keep away from percent-chance scores like HuntWise's "78%" movement probability and "100.0% WINDCAST" ([HuntWise App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/huntwise-a-better-hunting-app/id645518545)).

Say where Claude helped, and keep it short. Raptive's finding puts the penalty on content that looks generated, so a page that reads as yours loses little by naming the tool. iHeartMedia's "Guaranteed Human" policy keeps AI voices off the air while using AI "behind the scenes for scheduling, insights, workflow automation, show prep, and editing" ([AOL/AP](https://www.aol.com/articles/guaranteed-human-audio-giant-iheartmedia-213100883.html)). A line on an About page saying Claude helps write the code, and that the field notes and wind checks come from you at camp, fits Gartner's advice to be "transparent about when AI is used, what it's doing" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-03-16-gartner-marketing-survey-finds-50-percent-of-consumers-prefer-brands-that-avoid-using-genai-in-consumer-facing-content0)).

## Conclusion

The site's argument about wind applies to its own copy. A forecast averages a wide area and misses what the bog does at your sit, and a language model averages a great deal of writing and misses how you talk at camp. A wind check corrects the first, and your field notes correct the second. The cost of skipping that correction is already visible. The rival nearest to Groundwind already writes the same contrast sentences, so a hunter reading both sites hears one voice describing two products. The product with the deeper model gets no credit for it in the words.

Timing matters because the two rivals that come closest both launched this year, NordQuest in June with the FédéCP behind it and Contors in late September. As far as the public record shows, Contors' reliance on US LiDAR is what keeps it out of Canada. The cheapest change is also the one beta users see most. Renaming the app and swapping its icon was done on 6 October and waits only on a deploy. Your 60 wind checks are off the phone, so publishing their tally should come next, because it would be the first accuracy figure for hunting wind and would turn the site's caveats into numbers. The shared checks you approved on 6 October carry that further, from your tally to one for every area, and they are the closing ask of your own pitch. Camp photographs wait on the next trip, and a French site and a CIPO filing need to come before any push into Québec against NordQuest.
