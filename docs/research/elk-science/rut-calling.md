# Elk (Cervus canadensis) rut, bugling, calling and closing on a calling bull

Notes for Groundwind's activity model and hunting mode. Researched 2026-10-05. Labels: [S] peer-reviewed or agency science, [A] agency or park web page, [H] hunter practice (guides, champions, forums, magazines), [I] my inference. Where a number came only from a search-engine summary and I could not open the page, it says so.

## 1. Rut timing by region and latitude; is it photoperiod-driven and stable?

### Takeaway
From 36°N (Tennessee) to 58°N (Russian wapiti), and from coast to plains, peak breeding and peak bugling fall in the same window: about 20 September to 10 October. Median conception is about 20 September to 3 October. Latitude barely moves the peak. Herd make-up moves it more: herds bred by mature bulls conceive about two weeks earlier and over a shorter span than herds bred by yearlings. Day length is the accepted trigger. Year-to-year timing looks stable, but no multi-year bugle series was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Starkey, NE Oregon (about 45.2°N, 1,116–1,502 m, Rocky Mountain elk, 78 km² fenced herd, 1989–93)** [S]. Mean conception date by age of the principal sires: yearlings 7 Oct (range 15 Sep–24 Nov; median 2 Oct; 90th percentile 21 Oct); 2-yr-olds 3 Oct (median 1 Oct; 90th 12 Oct); 3-yr-olds 25 Sep (median 23 Sep; 90th 6 Oct); 4-yr-olds 27 Sep (median 25 Sep; 90th 3 Oct); 5-yr-olds 21 Sep (range 9 Sep–19 Oct; median 20 Sep; 90th 28 Sep) — [Noyes et al. 1996, J. Wildl. Manage. 60(3):508–517](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/research/docs/ELKEffectsofbullageonconceptiondatesandpregnancyratesofcowelkinoregon.pdf)
- Same study: the breeding span shrank from 71 days (yearling sires) to 41 days (5-yr-old sires). In 1993, with 5-yr-old sires, 52% of all conceptions fell in the single week 15–21 September. Non-lactating cows conceived about 9 days earlier than lactating cows in 3 of 5 years. The elk oestrous cycle is about 21 days (citing Morrison 1960). Pregnancy rose from 89% to 97% as bull age rose, but not significantly — [Noyes et al. 1996](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/research/docs/ELKEffectsofbullageonconceptiondatesandpregnancyratesofcowelkinoregon.pdf)
- Same study: conceptions dropped about 6 days after the 30 September deer-season opener (0.9 hunters/km²). The authors did not attribute the drop to hunting — [Noyes et al. 1996](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/research/docs/ELKEffectsofbullageonconceptiondatesandpregnancyratesofcowelkinoregon.pdf)
- **Grand Teton / southern Yellowstone (about 43.5–44.5°N)** [A/S]. Conception ran from 2 September to 29 November (1963–66), with peak breeding mid-September to mid-October. Peak calving was the last week of May to the first week of June — [NPS Research Report GRTE-N-1, ch. 3](https://npshistory.com/series/fauna/8/chap3.htm)
- **Rocky Mountain NP, Colorado (40.3°N)** [A]. "The peak of elk rut ... generally lasts from mid-September to mid-October, although it is often possible to hear elk bugling into November" — [NPS ROMO, Elk Bugles](https://www.nps.gov/romo/elk_bugles.htm)
- **Wind Cave NP, South Dakota (43.6°N)** [A]. Elk bugle from early September through October, as early as late August and as late as early November, with the peak in early October — [NPS Wind Cave, Listen for Bugling Elk](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/elk-bugling-wica.htm)
- **Tennessee, North Cumberland WMA (about 36.4°N; Manitoban elk from Elk Island NP, Alberta, released 2000–08)** [S]. Recorders counted bugles from 12 Aug to 29 Oct 2021. Peak bugling ran 23 Sep–10 Oct, with a second peak 17–19 Oct. Conception, backdated from 2024 calving with a 247 ± 3-day gestation (n = 8 births): peak mating 26 Sep–7 Oct, range 17 Sep–2 Nov, mean 3 Oct (SE 4.8 days), mode 26 Sep. The peak-bugling window matched the backdated conception window — [Metts et al. 2026, J. Southeast. Assoc. Fish Wildl. Agencies 13:83–92](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Kentucky (about 37°N)**: peak breeding late September through October (Kentucky Elk University 2025, as cited by Metts et al.) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Pennsylvania (about 41.4°N)**: the rut "is triggered by photoperiod," runs from about mid-September to early or mid-October, and peaks in "the last 10 days of September." This is a search-result summary of PA Wilds / Game Commission material; I could not confirm the wording on the page — [PA Wilds, Hear the Bugle](https://pawilds.com/hear-bugle-pa-wilds). The Game Commission's own 2022 release says the bugling season runs to about mid-October — [PGC, Elk Cam back for another season](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/pgc/newsroom/elk-cam-back-for-another-season)
- **Jasper and Banff, Alberta (51–53°N)** [A, via news]. The rut runs September into October, "until mid-October" in Jasper. Parks Canada's advised distance is 30 m — [CBC, Parks Canada rut safety tips](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3741621)
- **Roosevelt elk, Prairie Creek Redwoods, California (about 41.4°N, 1973)** [S]. The rut ran August to November. Activity was crepuscular and synchronised across sex and age classes — [Bowyer 1981, J. Mammal. 62(3):574 (abstract via search summary)](https://academic.oup.com/jmammal/article-abstract/62/3/574/893391). Redwood NP describes the Roosevelt rut as late August to early October, with bulls "heard for miles"; this was a search summary, not checked on the page — [NPS Redwood, Roosevelt elk](https://www.nps.gov/redw/learn/nature/roosevelt-elk.htm)
- **Siberian wapiti (C. c. sibiricus) on Russian farms (56.5°N and 58.4°N, 2013)** [S]. At Tver, calling was single-humped: high from 24 Sep to 21 Oct, with the top on 6 Oct. At Kostroma it was two-humped, peaking about 22 Sep and 25 Oct. The wild Siberian rut runs from the 2nd–3rd week of September to the end of October (Fedosenko 1980, as cited) — [Volodin, Volodina & Golosova 2016, Russ. J. Theriol. 15(2):91–99](http://kmkjournals.com/upload/PDF/RJT/15/ther15_2_091_099.pdf)
- **Mechanism**: Hudson & Haigh (2002) tie the elk rut to the autumnal equinox and note that nutrition also shifts it. In Tennessee, day length plus a peak-breeding factor was the best model of daily bugle counts (AIC weight 0.85). Bugles were more than 4× as many in the peak-breeding window (β = 4.45, SE 0.435). Daily maximum and minimum temperature and precipitation added nothing (ΔAIC +3.44) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Popular hunter guide** [H, no sources cited]: peak September 10–25; Pacific NW peaks late September around the equinox; Southwest (AZ, NM) earlier, sometimes late August; "higher elevations rut slightly earlier" — [onX Hunt, When is the elk rut](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/elk-rut). The science above puts median conception later (about 20 Sep–3 Oct) than this guide's "peak," so the guide's dates read more as a call-response or bugle onset than as breeding.

### Inferences
- [I] One continental calendar curve, with small herd-level offsets, fits all the regions in scope. Rule proposal for "elk rut stage", by date: 25 Aug–9 Sep pre-rut (bugling starts, bulls patternable) 0.4; 10–19 Sep 0.75; 20 Sep–8 Oct peak 1.0; 9–20 Oct 0.6 (late cows recycle on the 21-day oestrous cycle, giving the second peaks seen at 17–19 Oct in TN and late Oct at Kostroma); 21–31 Oct 0.3; November 0.1.
- [I] Offsets: herds dominated by mature bulls (limited-draw units, parks, eastern restored herds with low bull harvest) shift −5 days. Herds with few mature bulls (heavily hunted general units) shift +7 to +14 days and widen; Noyes's means run 21 Sep vs 7 Oct. Latitude: no evidence for a shift greater than about 1 week between 36°N and 58°N.
- [I] Because conception backdates cleanly from calving (247–255 day gestation; the Tennessee work used 247 ± 3), any area with known peak calving can be set from it. For example, a peak calving of 25 May–7 June implies peak breeding of roughly 20 Sep–3 Oct.
- [I] Roosevelt elk appear to run a similar or slightly earlier, longer rut (Bowyer: Aug–Nov). I found no Roosevelt conception-date distribution to set it precisely.

### Gaps
- No multi-year bugle-count series was found, so year-to-year stability of peak bugling (as opposed to conception) is unquantified. The Tennessee team says breeding "has appeared consistent across years" but recorded bugles in only one year.
- No primary source was found for Manitoba (Riding Mountain), Saskatchewan, BC, Alberta outside the parks, Michigan, or the Ontario (Bancroft, Nipissing, Lake of the Woods) herds' rut timing. The web search budget ran out before provincial or Michigan agency pages could be found.
- No Roosevelt elk conception-date study (Olympic, Oregon Coast Range) was retrieved.

## 2. Daily bugle activity: hour, temperature, wind, moon, weather, hunting pressure; acoustic-recorder studies

### Takeaway
Elk bugle at every hour in peak rut. The only North American recorder study, about 28,900 bugles in Tennessee, shows two peaks at 07:00–09:00 and 18:00–20:00 local time, roughly the 1–1.5 hours either side of sunrise and sunset. Neither temperature nor precipitation predicted daily bugle counts there or on Russian wapiti farms. The one wild Siberian series found a weak negative tie to temperature, and calling stopped entirely during three very cold days with snowfall. Moon effects have no evidence either way. Hunting pressure plausibly suppresses daytime calling but has not been measured acoustically.

### Cited Findings
- **Tennessee recorder study** [S]. Seven Song Meter Mini recorders were mounted 1.52 m up in grassy openings at the highest point of each site, at least 2 km apart. Each recorded the first 30 min of every hour, 24 h a day, from 12 Aug to 29 Oct 2021. 8,919 half-hour files held 28,853 bugles: 18,728 at the busiest site (unhunted, at the viewing tower), then 7,927, 1,331 and 820, and only 9, 17 and 21 at three low-density sites — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- Same study: "Although elk bugling occurred at all hours, peak bugling was between 7:00–9:00 and 18:00–20:00." One site peaked at midnight. No effect of daily maximum or minimum temperature or precipitation was found. Hunted and unhunted sites showed "the same" general pattern, but only 10 hunters were successful across the hunted units — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Siberian wapiti farms** (5 min per hour, 24 h, 70 days, 3 Sep–11 Nov 2013; 4,341 and 145 calls) [S]. At Tver, calls were highest from 18:00 to 09:00, that is through the night. At Kostroma there were two peaks, 07:00–09:00 and 16:00–18:00. With week and hour in the model, temperature had no significant effect at either farm; the univariate correlation at Tver was r = −0.29. The authors cite Alpine red deer calling mostly at night and early morning with a peak at 05:00–07:00 (Bocci et al. 2013), versus mostly daytime calling on Rum (Clutton-Brock & Albon 1979). Roar counts make a poor census index partly because of weather effects on both stags and counters (Douhard et al. 2013) — [Volodin et al. 2016](http://kmkjournals.com/upload/PDF/RJT/15/ther15_2_091_099.pdf)
- **Wild Siberian wapiti, Western Sayan (four synchronised recorders)** [S]. "Stag vocal activity weakly negatively correlated to air temperature and ceased completely during three very cold days with snowfall" — [Volodin et al., Russ. J. Theriol. 12(2):99–106](https://zmmu.msu.ru/rjt/articles/article.php?volume=12&issue=2&pages=99-106)
- **Call rate**: "Males emit 1 or 2 bugle calls per minute during peak hours of calling; females emit <1 per day." This appeared in a search snippet attached to the Feighny et al. 2006 record; I did not see it in the fetched abstract — [Feighny et al. 2006 (ResearchGate)](https://researchgate.net/publication/242082400_North_American_Elk_Bugle_Vocalizations_Male_and_Female_Bugle_Call_Structure_and_Context)
- **Older bulls bugle more** often than younger bulls and sire more calves (Struhsaker 1967; Kie et al. 2013, as cited) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Predation and pressure**: Joel Berger's work suggests elk in Rocky Mountain NP "bugle more often and for longer periods of time" and form larger groups than in Yellowstone, which has wolves and grizzlies — [NPS ROMO](https://www.nps.gov/romo/elk_bugles.htm)
- **Hunting and diel behaviour**: male elk responded to hunting most in daylight hours, and more in rifle season than in archery (Smith et al. 2021, as cited) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf). A seven-year Montana FWP / Montana State study found "hunting activity and hunter access have a greater impact on elk distribution" than wolves — [RMEF, When elk talk and when they don't](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/when-elk-talk-and-when-they-dont/)
- **Park guidance** [A]: "The best time to listen for elk is after sunset and before sunrise. Elk also bugle during the night." Elk "tend to stay away from roads until after dark" — [NPS Wind Cave](https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/elk-bugling-wica.htm). Pennsylvania: "The top time to see elk on camera is late in the afternoon" — [PGC](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/pgc/newsroom/elk-cam-back-for-another-season)
- **Hunter practice** [H, low-quality web guides]: the first two hours of light and the last hour are best; cold calm mornings are most vocal; wind suppresses bugling; rain pushes elk into timber; a spike follows a storm front — [SmartSlice, When do elk bugle](https://smartslice.blog/when-do-elk-bugle-rut-patterns); [Biology Insights](https://biologyinsights.com/when-do-elk-bugle-the-annual-rut-and-daily-patterns/). Hot windows are "often 60–90 minutes around first light and again at dusk" (MeatEater search-result summary, not confirmed on a page).

### Inferences
- [I] Rough rate scale from the Tennessee totals: 28,853 bugles in 8,919 half-hours is about 3.2 bugles per half-hour, or about 6–7 per hour, averaged over all sites, hours and 11 weeks. Assuming about 60 working days at the busiest recorder, it averaged about 12 per half-hour (about 25 per hour). Peak hours in peak weeks must be several times that.
- [I] Tennessee clock times against the sun: at Oneida, TN in late September, sunrise is about 07:30 EDT and sunset about 19:25 EDT. The 07:00–09:00 and 18:00–20:00 peaks therefore sit at about sunrise −0.5 h to +1.5 h and sunset −1.5 h to +0.5 h. Hour weights for "elk vocal": 1.0 in those two bands; 0.6 at night (bugling continues, and some sites peak at midnight); 0.5 in the hour on either side of each band; 0.3 from mid-morning to mid-afternoon. In peak weeks (20 Sep–8 Oct), raise midday to 0.5, since bugling "at all hours" is documented.
- [I] Temperature should not be the strong multiplier it is for moose. Neither bugle-count study found a temperature effect on counts. A gentle hunter-practice nudge is defensible: cool and calm 1.0; above 20 °C midday 0.7. Add a hard cut for snowstorm or extreme cold days (calling ceased in Sayan): 0.3.
- [I] Wind and rain should act mainly on hearing range (section 3), not on the elk's willingness to bugle. No study separates the two.
- [I] Pressure: in hunted or roaded areas, shift weight from day to night and to the first and last 30 minutes of light. A "pressure" knob of 0.5–0.8 on daytime calling response is a judgment call, not measured.

### Gaps
- No study was found relating elk bugling to moon phase, barometric pressure, wind speed or rain intensity. "Wind suppresses bugling" and "storm fronts trigger bugling" are hunter lore only.
- No acoustic study compared bugle rates between hunted and unhunted elk under real pressure. Tennessee's hunted units had about 10 successful hunters.
- No hour-of-day breakdown by week of the rut was published; the Tennessee hourly figure pools the whole season.
- Bowyer 1981 likely has hourly activity budgets for Roosevelt elk, but only the abstract summary was available.

## 3. Bugle and cow-call acoustics: carry distance, terrain, vegetation, wind, inversions; can a person judge bearing and distance?

### Takeaway
Bugles are loud, tonal and high-pitched: fundamental 0.55–2.1 kHz, about 80 dB at 10 m, 2–4 s long. That suits long-range signalling. Measured or claimed carry distances run about 1–1.5 km in typical conditions, "several km" in the literature, and well under 200 m in snow or heavy cover. Calm, inverted evenings and nights should extend range markedly (as shown for elephants). Humans locate a sound's bearing to within a few degrees when facing it, but get up to 20° wrong to the side and confuse front and back about 6% of the time. Distance judgments are strongly compressed, so far bugles are judged much nearer than they are.

### Cited Findings
- **Bugle structure** [S]: three segments (on-glide, whistle, off-glide). Male bugles are longer than female bugles. Aggressive bugles carry 4–5 low-frequency formants and are harsher and wider-band; non-aggressive bugles are tonal with no formants. Female bugles are "rarely heard." Recorded in Rocky Mountain NP, 1998–99 — [Feighny, Williamson & Clarke 2006, J. Mammal. 87(6)](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Mammalogy/volume-87/issue-6/06-MAMM-A-079R2.1/NORTH-AMERICAN-ELK-BUGLE-VOCALIZATIONS--MALE-AND-FEMALE-BUGLE/10.1644/06-MAMM-A-079R2.1.full)
- **Frequency and loudness** [S]: the fundamental "starts at 550 Hz and successively increases to a maximum of 2100 Hz". About 80 dB SPL at 10 m (at 605 Hz); up to 90 dB at 5 m in farmed elk. A 100 Hz call would be about 10 dB weaker for the same effort. High pitch gives a roughly five-fold efficiency advantage — [Titze & Riede 2010, PLoS Comput. Biol. 6(8):e1000897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2924247/)
- Bugles "typically occur between 1.000 to 7.500 kHz, last 2 to 4 sec, and can be heard several kilometers away" (citing Feighny et al. 2006; Frey et al. 2013). Tennessee bugles fell mostly at 0–4 kHz. About 1–5 grunts sometimes follow the off-glide — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf). In Rocky Mountain NP, bugles with grunts made up "about 16% of the time" — [NPS ROMO](https://www.nps.gov/romo/elk_bugles.htm)
- **Siberian wapiti** [S]: bugle maximum f0 1.23 ± 0.21 kHz, minimum f0 0.29 ± 0.05 kHz, duration 3.07 ± 0.52 s (n = 153). A human with a wind-instrument lure produced bugles of similar structure (n = 27) — [Volodin et al., RJT 12(2)](https://zmmu.msu.ru/rjt/articles/article.php?volume=12&issue=2&pages=99-106). "On natural breeding grounds in Siberia, rutting calls of male Siberian red deer propagate by a distance of 1.5 km," and recorders at maximum gain picked up stag calls from "about 1 km" — [Volodin et al. 2016](http://kmkjournals.com/upload/PDF/RJT/15/ther15_2_091_099.pdf)
- **Hunter observation** [H, forum via search summary]: 0.5–0.75 mile (0.8–1.2 km) on clear mornings or evenings; farther in sparse timber or high meadows; "over a mile" in optimal wind; under 200 yards (180 m) in snow or poor conditions — [Bowsite, Elk sounds, how far do they travel](https://forums.bowsite.com/tf/bgforums/thread.cfm?forum=5&threadid=386923)
- **Temperature inversions** [S, analogue species; via search summaries, not read in full]: modelling at Etosha found that low-level nocturnal radiative inversions plus low surface wind make early evening the best time for low-frequency calls. Calling area falls quickly after sunrise, when the inversion breaks: daytime areas usually <50 km², early-evening areas often >200 km² — [Larom et al. 1997, J. Exp. Biol. (index record)](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/12068995); [Elephant Listening Project](https://www.elephantlisteningproject.org/?p=2424). A physics model of recorder detection distance over the diel cycle and across seasons exists for forest sites — [Haupert et al. 2023, Methods Ecol. Evol.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/2041-210X.14020)
- **Forest attenuation** [S, via search summary]: attenuation rises with frequency, sound pressure level falls steadily and rapidly at ground level within forest, and the 1–2 kHz band (where bugles sit) is attenuated strongly on entering forest — [USFS, Propagation of noise over and through a forest stand](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/11549.pdf)
- **Bearing accuracy** [S]: localisation is most accurate straight ahead, with average errors of about 2° horizontal and 3.5° vertical. Errors grow toward the periphery, up to about 20° at extreme positions. Front/back confusions occurred in 6% of free-field trials (Makous & Middlebrooks 1990) — [PubMed 2348023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2348023)
- **Distance judgment** [S]: perceived distance follows a compressive power law, r′ = k·r^a with a < 1 and a rough average of a ≈ 0.4. Near sources are overestimated and far ones underestimated — [Kolarik et al. 2016 review, PMC4744263](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4744263); [Zahorik et al. summary](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/auditory-distance-perception-in-humans-a-summary-of-past-and-pres/)
- **Hunter tell** [H, forum, via search summary]: a bull elk does not cow-call, so a bugle followed by cow calls from the same spot is "almost guaranteed to be a human" — [Outdoors Codidact](https://outdoors.codidact.com/posts/54986)

### Inferences
- [I] Hearing radius for the "heard by map tap" distance picker and for the activity verdict's "you will hear them" line: default 1.0 km for an open or mixed landscape at dawn or dusk in light wind; 1.5 km for calm, clear evening or night conditions over valleys (inversion); up to 2–3 km across open basins under inversion, matching "several km". Cut to 0.5 km in wind above about 20 km/h or in continuous dense timber, and to 0.2 km in falling snow. These figures are bounded by the Siberian 1.5 km figure, the 1 km recorder range and forum observations. They are not measured for North American elk in specific terrain.
- [I] Because the bugle's energy is at 0.5–2 kHz, it is shadowed by ridges much more than a moose's low grunt. The app's terrain model could treat a bugle heard over a ridge as coming from the ridge crest direction (diffraction), not necessarily the bull's true bearing. Echoes off cliffs can create a false second bearing; see Gaps.
- [I] Bearing input: ask the hunter to turn and face the sound before setting the rose. That moves the source to the 2° zone instead of the 20° zone. Draw a ±10° wedge by default, ±20° when there was wind or the bugle came off a canyon wall, and offer a "behind me?" flip for the 6% front/back case.
- [I] Distance input: hunters will underestimate far bugles. With a ≈ 0.4, a true 1,500 m bugle can feel like a few hundred metres. The picker should offer coarse bins (close <150 m / near 150–400 m / far 400–1,000 m / very far >1 km) rather than a slider, and widen the uncertainty band for the farther bins. Hearing the grunts or chuckles after the bugle is widely used as a "close" cue in hunter practice, but I found no source with a number.
- [I] Two or more bugles heard from different spots (the hunter has moved) allow triangulation. Elk call far more often than moose, so the heard log will usually have enough points to intersect bearings.

### Gaps
- No North American field measurement of bugle detection distance by habitat (open park vs lodgepole vs aspen) was found. Frey et al. 2013, cited for "several km", was not retrieved.
- No study of how far cow calls (mews, chirps) carry was found. Hunters treat them as short-range, but I found no numbers.
- No source was found on canyon echoes misleading bugle bearing, or on accuracy of human distance judgments for real bugles outdoors; the psychoacoustic data are from lab and free-field tests. Rokslide's "Judging bugle distance" thread was blocked (HTTP 403).
- No elk-specific study of wind speed or wind direction on detection range was found.

## 4. Bull behaviour: herd vs satellite bulls; response to bugles vs cow calls; call shyness; how bulls approach a call

### Takeaway
Mature herd bulls bugle most, feed least and rarely leave their cows to answer a call. Satellites bugle and approach, but younger bulls often flee aggressive challenge bugles. Bulls that come usually circle to the downwind side, sweep side to side to wind the "cow", and hang up about 50–100 yards (45–90 m) out if they cannot see the source. Repeatedly called or hunted bulls go quiet or "bugle and run". Most of this is hunter consensus, not measured.

### Cited Findings
- **Energetics of herd bulls** [S]: Roosevelt elk herd bulls spent 24% of the day feeding during the rut, against 53% for non-breeding males. Rank was set mostly by non-contact interactions before the rut and carried into it, and it was correlated with mating success. Elk moved a mean 2.4 km per 24 h in a home range of about 400 ha — [Bowyer 1981 (abstract via search summary)](https://academic.oup.com/jmammal/article-abstract/62/3/574/893391)
- **Aggressive vs advertising bugles** [S]: aggressive bugles are harsher, with formants; non-aggressive ones are tonal — [Feighny et al. 2006](https://bioone.org/journals/Journal-of-Mammalogy/volume-87/issue-6/06-MAMM-A-079R2.1/NORTH-AMERICAN-ELK-BUGLE-VOCALIZATIONS--MALE-AND-FEMALE-BUGLE/10.1644/06-MAMM-A-079R2.1.full). Bugles both herd harems (Bowyer & Kitchen 1987) and signal aggression to other males (as cited) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Herd bulls hold, satellites react** [H]: mature herd bulls show "laser focus" on cows in oestrus and won't leave for a convincing call. Young satellites often flee aggressive challenge bugles but may come to weaker calls. Bulls "bugle and run" because of hunting pressure, fear of losing cows, or sensing danger (wind, unnatural sounds, movement) — [Elk101, Why do elk bugle and run](https://www.elk101.com/2016/03/why-do-elk-bugle-and-run/). Hunters "constantly try to convince a herd bull to abandon a group of cows because they made a soft little cow call from 300 yards away" (Jason Phelps) — [MeatEater, 10 biggest mistakes](https://www.themeateater.com/hunt/elk/the-10-biggest-mistakes-elk-hunters-make-every-year)
- **Late rut** [H]: October bugling has a second peak as unbred cows cycle and herds mill together. Mature bulls increasingly leave for "safer and often higher haunts" later in October, and young satellites keep bugling. Bulls learn to stay quiet after being called to hunters once or twice — [RMEF, When elk talk and when they don't](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/when-elk-talk-and-when-they-dont/)
- **Approach path** [H, three-time World Elk Calling Champion Corey Jacobsen]: bulls typically circle to come in from downwind. "Visualize a straight line from the caller to the bull, then draw an imaginary arc on the downwind side" as his likely path, and set the shooter on that arc, not on the straight line — [RMEF, Follow the ARC](https://www.rmef.org/media/elkcallingchampfollowthearc/)
- **Sweep and hang-up** [H, NM guide Ralph Ramos]: "Many bulls will close a long distance gap but hang up around 100 yards when they don't see a cow." "Bulls will move side-to-side, sweeping the area in front of a shooter trying to wind a cow or you" — [RMEF, The X-Zone](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/the-x-zone/). A MeatEater search summary gave "about the fifty yard marker" as the usual hang-up; this was not confirmed on a page. Phelps: "the bull hangs up behind a creek or thick brush, and it's game over" — [MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/hunt/elk/the-10-biggest-mistakes-elk-hunters-make-every-year)
- **Coming in silent** [H]: "many clean kills happen within bow range when the bull never fully answered" (search summary of RMEF / guide material). This is widely held but not quantified.

### Inferences
- [I] Hunting mode should mark a heard bull's type from the call pattern the hunter taps. Repeated bugles from a moving spot plus cow sounds suggest a herd bull with cows: low odds he comes to the call, so plan to go to him. A lone bugler that answers and moves toward you is likely a satellite or a bull without cows: high odds he comes, so set up and let him swing.
- [I] The existing moose swing (least-cost route to the first ground where the hunter's scent reaches him) transfers directly. For elk, end the hang-up "close-in" cost at about 90 m (100 yd) for a bull that cannot see a cow, and at about 45 m (50 yd) when there is cover he can approach behind. That compares with 45–75 m for moose.
- [I] Expect lateral sweeping on the last 100 m. The swing could be drawn as a band about ±50 m wide rather than a line once the bull is inside 150 m.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed playback experiment was found measuring the proportion of bulls that approach, approach speed, silent vs vocal approach, or the downwind circling angle. Circling downwind is strong consensus but not measured for elk.
- Struhsaker (1968) and Geist (1982) describe rut behaviour in detail but were not retrievable. Bowyer & Kitchen 1987 (Am. Midl. Nat.) was seen only as a citation.
- No quantitative data were found on "call shyness" (response rate vs prior hunter exposure).

## 5. Hunter tactics (evidence or broad consensus) and how they map to route planning to a heard bull

### Takeaway
Consensus: obey the wind without exception, stop calling once a bull is located, close the distance silently using terrain and the thermals, then set up. The shooter goes 30–150 yards in front of the caller, on the bull's downwind arc, in a slight crosswind, with shooting lanes ranged. With a herd bull, get close and sound like competition (cow plus bull "messing with" cows) rather than cow-calling from 300 yards. No controlled studies exist; these are expert practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Wind first** [H]: "Obey the wind! No argument, no excuses." Hide from sight, sound and smell ("Always Remember Concealment"). Range trees and rocks beforehand; draw only when the bull's head is turned or behind cover. A partner lets the bull pass the shooter — [Jacobsen, RMEF](https://www.rmef.org/media/elkcallingchampfollowthearc/)
- **Shut up and close** [H]: "Once you've located a bull, it's usually best to shut up. Use the terrain, watch your thermals, and sneak in." "If you position yourself with a slight crosswind, you can often predict exactly where he'll try to circle." "Get close. Mimic a bull messing with one of his cows. Trigger jealousy, competition, and aggression." — [Phelps, MeatEater](https://www.themeateater.com/hunt/elk/the-10-biggest-mistakes-elk-hunters-make-every-year). onX ambassadors: "I try to get in without calling — if bulls are talking, I move in quietly," with calling as Plan B — [onX](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/elk-rut)
- **Caller–shooter spacing** [H, conflicting]: "The caller needs to be a minimum of 100 to 150 yards from the shooter," and the caller moves away from the bull's bugles to draw him past the shooter (Ramos) — [RMEF, The X-Zone](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/the-x-zone/). For a challenge-bugle setup, the caller is about 30 yards behind the hunter — [onX](https://www.onxmaps.com/hunt/blog/elk-rut). The difference tracks the situation: cow-calling to pull a bull from far (long spacing) vs close-in challenge (short spacing).
- **Thermals** [H, via search summary]: thermals rise in the morning and fall in the evening (MeatEater "Cutting the Distance" material). The search summary also said "morning setups should position you downslope ... evening setups should flip that"; I could not confirm this on a page — [MeatEater, Ep. 36: When thermals go wrong](https://www.themeateater.com/listen/cutting-the-distance/ep-36-when-thermals-go-wrong-and-how-to-beat-the-wind)
- **Disturbance distances** [A]: Parks Canada advises staying at least 30 m from rutting elk for safety — [CBC / Parks Canada](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.3741621). This is a viewing-safety distance, not a hunting tactic.

### Inferences
- [I] **Route to a heard bull.** Plan to a setup point, not to the bull. Place the setup about 100–150 m from his last estimated position (outside the 90 m hang-up, inside the range where a soft call or raking is believable). It goes on the side where the modelled head-height wind (plus thermals) carries the hunter's scent away from him, with crosswind preferred (60–120° off the line to the bull). Avoid the straight-downwind approach: his swing will come round to it.
- [I] **Thermal side**, by drainage physics rather than fixed rules. When flow is downslope (from before sunset through the night to roughly 30–90 min after sun hits the slope), scent sinks, so approach and set up below or level with the bull. When flow is upslope (late morning to mid-afternoon on sunny slopes), approach from above or level. Note: rule 5 in docs/HUNT-FISH-SCIENCE.md says "evening: sit above the target." That looks reversed for downslope evening drainage unless the bull is expected to come down to the hunter; it is worth re-checking. The per-minute ground-wind model already computes the flow, and the router should use it directly.
- [I] **Contour vs straight.** Approach on the contour at the bull's elevation band, or slightly below during drainage, so scent stays on the safe side. Use dead ground behind ridges and timber edges for the last 300 m. Avoid crossing the bull's downwind sector. A straight line is acceptable only when the whole line is crosswind or upwind of him.
- [I] **How much to swing downwind.** The hunter's route should hold off the bull's downwind sector by at least the scent-cone width at that range. The app's 60° sector out to 450 m is a reasonable default for elk too, since elk noses are no worse than moose. That implies a detour of about 30–60° of arc around the bull at 300–500 m.
- [I] **Caller and shooter.** For a two-person setup, place the shooter 30–140 m (30–150 yd) toward the bull from the caller, offset onto the bull's predicted downwind arc. Use about 30 m for a close challenge setup and 90–140 m for cow calling at range. The moose rule is 40–50 m.
- [I] **Herd bull moving with cows.** Do not route to his current spot; route to intercept ahead of the herd's heading, which the app can get from two or more bugles. Close to under 150 m before any call.
- [I] **Quiet rule**: after the first locate, the app should suggest no calling until the setup point is reached ("he's located; close in quiet").

### Gaps
- None of these tactics has controlled-study support. I found no data on success rate vs caller spacing, approach direction or calling volume.
- No source gave a measured distance at which elk detect human scent or footfall in the rut. The 450 m scent sector is the app's moose default, not elk data.
- The source of the "morning downslope, evening flip" thermal rule could not be verified, and it may conflict with drainage physics depending on which party is moving.

## 6. Seasons and weapons that overlap the rut, and how elk differ in post-rut rifle seasons

### Takeaway
In most western states the archery season (about 1–30 September, Montana to 18 October) is the rut-calling season. Muzzleloader seasons sometimes overlap mid-September (Colorado 12–20 Sep). General rifle seasons mostly open mid- to late October or later, after median conception. Eastern restored herds (KY, PA, TN, ON) put firearm seasons inside or at the tail of the rut. In rifle seasons, elk respond more strongly to hunting and in daylight, and mature bulls move to security cover.

### Cited Findings
- **Colorado 2026**: archery 2–30 Sep; muzzleloader 12–20 Sep; rifle 1st 14–18 Oct, 2nd 24 Oct–1 Nov, 3rd 7–15 Nov, 4th 18–22 Nov. Third-party guide, verified against the CPW 2026 brochure in July 2026 — [Colorado Hunting Guide](https://thecoloradohuntingguide.com/elk-seasons/)
- **Montana 2026**: archery 5 Sep–18 Oct; general rifle 24 Oct–29 Nov. Third-party guide, via search summary; not checked against FWP — [The Inside Spread](https://theinsidespread.com/state-guides/montana-elk-hunting-2026)
- **Idaho 2026**: most general archery A-tag elk zones 30 Aug–30 Sep. **Wyoming 2026**: special archery opens 1 Sep in most areas; area 2 opens 20 Sep; areas 11, 35–37 and 67–69 open 15 Sep. Search summary of a third-party blog; the page itself returned 403 — [HLRBO](https://www.hlrbo.com/blog/archery-elk-season-2026-openers-drought-access/)
- **Kentucky 2026–27** (official): archery/crossbow either sex 12–25 Sep and 5–11 Dec 2026; bull firearm week 1 26–30 Sep, week 2 3–7 Oct 2026; cow firearm 28 Nov–2 Dec 2026 and 2–6 Jan 2027 — [KDFWR seasons planner](https://fw.ky.gov/Hunt/Pages/ky-hunting-fishing-seasons-planner.aspx)
- **Pennsylvania 2026–27**: archery 12–27 Sep; new first firearms season 3–11 Oct ("covers the latter portion of the rut"); second firearms 31 Oct–8 Nov; late 9–17 Jan 2027; 155 tags (80 antlered). Third-party guide; not checked against PGC — [East Meets West Hunt](https://eastmeetswesthunt.com/blogs/journal/how-to-hunt-elk-in-pennsylvania-2026-pa-elk-hunting-application-strategy)
- **Ontario 2026** (official): 21 Sep–4 Oct, residents only, WMUs 57, 58, 60–62, 63A, by draw; bows count as firearms — [Ontario Hunting Regulations Summary, Elk](https://ontario.ca/document/ontario-hunting-regulations-summary/elk). Conflict: a search summary of an older O. Reg. 670/98 version gave 16–29 Sep for the same WMUs. Dates move year to year — [O. Reg. 670/98](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980670/v40)
- **Tennessee 2021** (in the recorder study): archery 25 Sep–3 Oct; young sportsman 4–10 Oct; gun/muzzleloader/archery 11–17 Oct. All of these overlapped peak bugling (23 Sep–10 Oct) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf)
- **Behaviour under rifle pressure** [S]: male elk responded most to hunting in daylight hours and more in rifle than archery season; females also responded in daylight and avoided forage, roads and trails (Smith et al. 2021; Spitz et al. 2019, as cited) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf). Hunter access affected Montana elk distribution more than wolves did — [RMEF](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/when-elk-talk-and-when-they-dont/). Mature bulls go to "safer and often higher haunts" later in October — [RMEF](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/when-elk-talk-and-when-they-dont/)

### Inferences
- [I] The app's "calling" verdict should key off the rut curve (section 1), not the weapon. Weapon only sets defaults: archery means close setups, a 30–90 m shooting distance, and the caller-behind-shooter layout. For rifle seasons opening after about 15 October in the West, the verdict should switch from "call and close" to "glass and still-hunt security cover". Calling weight should be the rut-curve value (0.3–0.6) multiplied by a pressure factor.
- [I] Eastern firearm seasons (KY late Sep–early Oct, PA early Oct, ON late Sep–early Oct, TN mid-Oct) are rut rifle hunts. Calling and the heard-bull route apply fully there, with rifle shooting distances, so the setup can sit 150–300 m off.

### Gaps
- Not verified for 2026: Alberta, BC, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Michigan, Oregon, Washington, Idaho controlled hunts, Utah, New Mexico, Arizona. The search budget ran out, and the Michigan DNR page returned 403.
- Montana, Idaho, Wyoming and Pennsylvania dates come from third-party pages, not agency documents.
- No direct study of post-rut bull habitat (bedding cover, elevation, group size) under rifle pressure was retrieved. Proffitt et al. and Cleveland et al. on security areas were not reached.

## 7. How much of the moose calling practice transfers to elk, and what differs

### Takeaway
Much of it transfers: crepuscular timing, a late-September to early-October peak, the downwind circling swing, the caller set upwind of or behind the shooter, the heard log with bearing and distance, and wind discipline. The differences: elk call far more, from farther and at higher pitch; they live in herds, so there are many noses; herd bulls won't leave cows; the caller–shooter spacing is wider (up to 150 yd); and the temperature effect on calling is weak. The moose temperature multiplier should not be copied.

### Cited Findings
- **App moose rules** for comparison [I/H from the project doc]: moose peak calling response about 25 Sep–5 Oct with an effectiveness curve; strong temperature multipliers (1.0 at ≤10 °C down to 0.3 above 20 °C); caller 40–50 m upwind of the shooter; hang-up 45–75 m; downwind swing on the LiDAR going grid — [docs/HUNT-FISH-SCIENCE.md](../../HUNT-FISH-SCIENCE.md)
- **Elk timing** is almost the same window: peak bugling 23 Sep–10 Oct (TN), and median conception 20 Sep–2 Oct depending on bull age (OR) — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf); [Noyes et al. 1996](https://www.dfw.state.or.us/wildlife/research/docs/ELKEffectsofbullageonconceptiondatesandpregnancyratesofcowelkinoregon.pdf)
- **Volume of calling**: one Tennessee recorder logged 18,728 bugles in a season of half-hour samples — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf). Moose do not produce anything close to this as a broadcast signal (no comparable count found).
- **Temperature**: no effect on elk bugle counts (TN; Russian farms), weak negative in the wild Sayan series — [Metts et al. 2026](https://seafwa.org/sites/default/files/journal-articles/j13-09-metts-et-al-83-92.pdf); [Volodin et al. 2016](http://kmkjournals.com/upload/PDF/RJT/15/ther15_2_091_099.pdf); [Volodin et al. RJT 12(2)](https://zmmu.msu.ru/rjt/articles/article.php?volume=12&issue=2&pages=99-106)
- **Calling grammar** [H]. Moose: quit cow-calling once a bull announces himself and talk to him as a bull — [Outdoor Canada, 5 moose hunting lessons](https://www.outdoorcanada.ca/5-moose-hunting-lessons/). Elk: hunters carry bugle and cow calls, but bulls don't cow-call, so mixing both from one spot can reveal a human — [Outdoors Codidact](https://outdoors.codidact.com/posts/54986). Elk vocal set: bugles with grunts and chuckles (grunts 2–4, deeper; chuckles 4–8, higher; forum) — [Elk101 forums, Chuckles and grunts](https://forums.elk101.com/threads/chuckles-and-grunts.5201)
- **Spacing and hang-up** [H]: elk caller 100–150 yd behind the shooter and hang-up about 100 yd (Ramos) — [RMEF X-Zone](https://www.rmef.org/elk-network/the-x-zone/). The moose equivalents are 40–50 m and 45–75 m.

### Inferences
- [I] **Reuse as is**: hunting-mode heard log; downwind swing routing; scent cone and ground-wind and thermal model; calling-site geometry rules 1–4 and 6–9; crepuscular hour weights; rut calendar shape.
- [I] **Change for elk**: (a) Calendar: centre on about 27 Sep, with the peak band 20 Sep–8 Oct and a second hump 15–25 Oct. (b) Temperature: replace the moose multiplier with a weak one, plus a snowstorm cut. (c) Hearing radius: about 1–1.5 km default, larger than for moose grunts, especially in calm evening inversions. (d) Heard-sound types: bugle, bugle with grunts, chuckle, cow mew/chirp, cow bark (alarm), raking, glimpsed. (e) Hang-up 90 m without visual, 45 m with cover. (f) Caller–shooter offset 30–140 m depending on call type. (g) Herd-bull flag: route to intercept, don't expect him to come. (h) Many noses: a bull with cows has a wider effective detection sector, so widen the downwind avoidance sector, for example 60° to 90°, when cows are heard.
- [I] **Elk-only feature**: frequent bugles make bearing triangulation practical. Two bearings a few hundred metres apart, taken within about 10 minutes, could pin a bull far better than a guessed distance.

### Gaps
- No study was found comparing moose and elk response to calling, or giving moose call frequency or carry data, for a like-for-like acoustic comparison.
- No evidence was found on whether elk circle downwind as consistently as moose do, or at what radius. The circling rule is consensus for both species.
